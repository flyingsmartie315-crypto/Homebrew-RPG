# NPC_Generation_Protocol.txt
# How an NPC is generated: order of work, dice, tables, and the Experience spend.
# Status: DRAFT
# Split from Format_NPC_Profile.txt, which keeps the record layout, the BIO,
# the current state, the relations, internal thoughts, and the Memory Book.
 
Run on first creation of an NPC, and for every NPC in a scene population.
Results are written into the profile (Format_NPC_Profile.txt, OUTPUT ON CREATION)
and do not change unless a later event alters them.
 
--------------------------------------------------
GENERATION SEQUENCE
--------------------------------------------------
Follow these three steps in order when an NPC is first created.
 
1. Establish context
   Surroundings, environment, culture, settlement, and the immediate scene.
   Who would be here, doing what, at this hour.
   This context decides occupation, Peoples file, and the range of possible lives
   before any dice are rolled.
 
2. Generate the stats and mechanics profile
   Gender, age, occupation, rarity, characteristics, pre-adulthood skills,
   adult Experience spend, ageing, Looks, sensory traits, quirks, height, weight, appearance.
   Follow the rules below. Record the mechanical block in Entity style.
 
3. Generate a short bio
   REQUIRED. Do not omit. The BIO is written in the NPC's own voice
   and sits in the profile before the scene internal_thoughts.
   What it covers and how it is written: Format_NPC_Profile.txt, BIO.
   A profile without the BIO block is incomplete.

 
--------------------------------------------------
GENERATION PROCESS
--------------------------------------------------
Run when the NPC is first created.
Results are written into the profile and do not change unless a later event alters them.
 
Gender
Male or Female.
 
Virath gender rule
Default to medieval norms. Martial roles, hazardous labour, and many skilled trades are male only unless a Peoples file or settlement entry already states an exception. A personal guard, log driver, miner, smith, or man-at-arms is male by default.
 
Age
Years. Set by context or roll appropriate to the occupation and role.
 
Occupation
Based on prior contact and the scene.
Refer to Economy_Labour.txt for wage bands and illustrative roles.
The lists are not exhaustive.
Elite rulers, their retinue, men-at-arms, knights, and similar are placed by context, not by the common labour tables.
 
Rarity Tier
Determine by random distribution or by correlation to the role occupied.
 
--------------------------------------------------

Elite rulers, their retinue, men-at-arms, and knights
are not rolled on these tables. Place them by context.
 
Apply the flat bonus to every characteristic:
 
Lumpen     All characteristics +5
Common     All characteristics +10
Uncommon   All characteristics +15
Elite      All characteristics +20
 
--------------------------------------------------
CHARACTERISTICS & RARITY SET FILTERING
--------------------------------------------------
Each set is 3 × 1d10.
A higher rarity rolls more sets. Discard the lowest sets until exactly nine remain.
The nine kept totals are the NPC's raw characteristic scores, one for each of the nine characteristics.
Add the rarity-tier bonus after allocation.
 
Lumpen    : Roll 9 sets   | Discard 0 | Keep 9 | Add +5 to all characteristics
Common    : Roll 10 sets  | Discard 1 | Keep 9 | Add +10 to all characteristics
Uncommon  : Roll 11 sets  | Discard 2 | Keep 9 | Add +15 to all characteristics
Elite     : Roll 12 sets  | Discard 3 | Keep 9 | Add +20 to all characteristics
 
Order of work
1. Roll every set for the rarity tier.
2. Discard the lowest sets until nine remain. If sets tie at the discard line, either may go.
3. Allocate the nine kept totals, one to each characteristic (rule below).
4. Add the rarity-tier bonus to every characteristic.
Do not swap or re-roll a kept set after it is allocated.
 
Allocation Rule
Assign the nine kept totals to the characteristics, one each, to suit the role.
A river hand takes Strength and Toughness.
A counter takes Intelligence.
A factor takes Fellowship.
Higher results go to the characteristics the occupation uses.
 
Personality and Values
Use the characteristic scores to inform personality and values.
High and low scores suggest tendencies. They do not dictate a single personality.
Write the resulting lean into the profile for later monologue use.
 
Talents, Failings, Afflictions
Determine any entries from Core_Talents_Failings_Afflictions.txt
that fit the occupation, rarity, age, and characteristic profile.
Record them in the profile.
 
Height (cm)
Set by the HEIGHT table below.
 
Appearance
Drawn from the PHENOTYPE block of the Peoples file
the NPC belongs to.
Use the phenotype as a descriptive range, not a rigid type.
Individuals vary.
 
Weight
Weight in pounds.
Consistent with height, build (from phenotype somatotype), and occupation.
 
--------------------------------------------------
HEIGHT
--------------------------------------------------
Height is a stature term from the Peoples file, turned into a mean,
then moved along a bell curve by one 1d100 roll.
 
Stature terms and means (cm)
Term          Male    Female
Short          160     149
Modest         165     153
Average        170     158
Rather tall    175     163
Tall           180     167
Very tall      185     174
 
1. Read the Stature line of the NPC's PHENOTYPE block.
   A ranged term ("Tall to very tall") uses the midpoint of its two terms.
   Average the two means for the NPC's gender (male 180 and 185 give 182.5).
   If the Peoples file gives no Stature, use Average unless the GM sets one.
2. Take the mean for the NPC's gender from the table.
3. Roll 1d100. The roll is the NPC's place on the bell curve.
   50 is the middle of the curve: the mean. Low is short for the stock, high is tall.
4. Add the offset for the roll. Height = mean + offset, in whole cm (round .5 up).
 
Offset by roll (cm, both genders)
Roll        Offset
01            −17
02–03         −13
04–05         −11
06–10          −9
11–15          −7
16–25          −5
26–35          −3
36–45          −2
46–55           0
56–65          +2
66–75          +3
76–85          +5
86–90          +7
91–95          +9
96–97         +11
98–99         +13
100           +17
 
Example: a Jarnvolk man. Stature "Tall to very tall" is the midpoint of Tall (180)
and Very tall (185): mean 182.5.
Roll 50 gives 183 cm. Roll 08 gives 174 cm. Roll 100 gives 200 cm.
 
The offsets are a normal curve with a standard deviation of 6.5 cm,
so most NPCs fall within 5 cm of the mean.
Roll once at creation. Height does not change with Rarity or occupation.
 
--------------------------------------------------
APPEARANCE DESCRIPTION STYLE
--------------------------------------------------
Write appearance notes in 3–4 concrete, observational sentences.
Blend terse, matter-of-fact campaign prose with sharp attention to lineage,
phenotype, upbringing, and sumptuary dress.
Do not use the scientific language of the Peoples phenotype blocks.
 
1. Lineage & Bone Structure
   Anchor skin, hair, eye colour, skull/face shape, and build directly to the
   relevant Peoples_[Name].txt PHENOTYPE block and the generated height/weight values.
 
2. Material Upbringing & Wear
   Show Rarity Tier, age, and trade through physical marks—nutrition (gaunt vs. well-fed),
   sun/wind exposure, soot, calluses, dental wear, or old scars.
 
3. Dress & Sumptuary Rank
   Note exact fabrics, dyes, cuts, and legal hardware (weapons, signets, coifs, aprons)
   dictated by the culture's dress laws.
 
4. Bearing & Tell
   Close with one grounded detail of posture, gaze, or physical habit
   that reveals temperament without poetic metaphors.
 

--------------------------------------------------
TALENT AND FAILING ALLOCATION
 
For random NPC generation, certain Talents and Failings
may manifest at the start of life.
Roll once on the relevant table at creation.
The result is fixed unless a later event changes it.
 
LOOKS
Roll 1d100.
 
01–02   Hideous (Failing)     –30   2%   Severe congenital deformity
03–08   Ugly (Failing)        –20   6%   Markedly asymmetrical, heavily weathered, or visibly off-putting features
09–20   Homely (Failing)      –10   12%  Plain, coarse, or slightly unbalanced features; easily overlooked
21–80   None / Ordinary         0   60%  Unremarkable, functional features typical of their stock
81–92   Comely (Talent)       +10   12%  Pleasant, symmetrical, and healthy features; appealing by local stock standards
93–98   Beautiful (Talent)    +20   6%   Noticeably handsome or fair; commands immediate positive attention
99–100  Striking (Talent)     +30   2%   Rare, unforgettable presence; exceptional symmetry and bone structure
 
The modifier applies to Fellowship tests and to social reactions
where appearance is relevant.
It does not alter the base Fellowship characteristic.
 
If both parents are known, do not roll.
Average the parents’ Looks modifiers and apply the nearest result
on the table above.
A child of two Ordinary parents is Ordinary.
A child of Beautiful and Homely averages toward Ordinary or Comely
according to the mean.
 
Innate Talents rolled at creation
Roll only where the entry says so. The result is fixed.
Prerequisites (Core_Talents_Failings_Afflictions.txt, section 0) are checked once.
A Talent with a time-in-a-place prerequisite (Streetwise, Parish Wise) needs the
years named in the BIO.

KINDRED SPIRIT. Roll 1d100 only if the upbringing was in a wilder or
animal-husbandry setting (herding, stock and stable work, hunting and trapping,
frontier and forest life, hand-rearing beasts). On 01 the NPC has the Talent.

COLD SPINE. Roll 1d1000 for every NPC. On 001 or 002 the NPC has the Talent.
Set the three personal sensations at creation (Watched, Wrong place, Don't).
Write them in the profile. They enter the monologue and the FACT CHECK.

Kindred Spirit and Cold Spine are rolled, not bought. Their Tier is a placeholder.
Other Talents are chosen by the GM to fit the occupation, rarity, age, and
characteristic profile. Bought Talents are paid from the Experience pool at the
entry's Tier (Core_EXP.txt).

Other Talents and Failings
Additional tables may be added in the same format.
Occupation and rarity may raise or lower the chance of
profession-linked Talents, but congenital results such as Looks
are rolled (or inherited) before occupation is applied.
 
 
--------------------------------------------------
SENSORY TRAITS
--------------------------------------------------
Rolled at creation for baseline humans.
Higher grades (20/30) are locked and reserved for beasts or the kind.
 
HEARING
Roll 1d100.
 
01        Hard of Hearing (30)   –30   1%   Profoundly deaf; relies on lip-reading, vibration, and visual cues
02–04     Hard of Hearing (20)   –20   3%   Severe hearing loss; requires shouting at close range, misses ambient sound
05–15     Hard of Hearing (10)   –10   11%  Noticeable impairment; struggles in crowds or wind
16–90     None / Ordinary          0   75%  Standard human baseline
91–100    Keen Hearing (10)      +10   10%  Natural human cap; sharp, discerning ear
Locked    Keen Hearing (20/30)  +20/+30  0%  Reserved for beasts / the kind
 
SIGHT
Roll 1d100.
 
01        Poor Sight (30)        –30   1%   Legally blind or severely cataract-clouded; light, shadow, and close shapes only
02–05     Poor Sight (20)        –20   4%   Heavy refractive error or one eye lost; faces and details blur past a few paces
06–20     Poor Sight (10)        –10   15%  Near-sighted, far-sighted, or night-strained; misses distant movement or fine print
21–90     None / Ordinary          0   70%  Standard human baseline; functional vision in usable light
91–100    Keen Sight (10)        +10   10%  Natural human cap; exceptional acuity, lookout or archer grade
Locked    Keen Sight (20/30)    +20/+30  0%  Reserved for raptors / the kind
 
SMELL
Roll 1d100.
 
01        Poor Smell (30)        –30   1%   Total anosmia; unable to detect smoke, rot, or gas
02–03     Poor Smell (20)        –20   2%   Severe hyposmia; detects only overpowering stenches at close range
04–10     Poor Smell (10)        –10   7%   Dulled sense; misses subtle scents
11–92     None / Ordinary          0   82%  Standard human baseline
93–100    Keen Smell (10)        +10   8%   Natural human cap; discerning nose
Locked    Keen Smell (20/30)    +20/+30  0%  Reserved for hounds / beasts / the kind
 
Modifiers apply to relevant Perception and skill tests
(Awareness, Silent Move, Track, etc.) where the sense is used.
They do not alter the base Perception characteristic.
Occupational exposure may later worsen a result;
it does not improve a congenital deficit beyond the table.
 
 
--------------------------------------------------
QUIRKS
--------------------------------------------------
Quick NPC quirks. Flavour only. No mechanics attached.
A quirk is not a Talent or a Failing. It carries no bonus, no penalty, and no test.
It colours the BIO, the internal_thoughts, and the appearance notes.

Roll at creation, with the Looks and sensory traits.
Chance: to be set. (Draft: 1d100, 01-12 one quirk, 13 two quirks, 14-100 none.)
For each quirk roll on the list below (1d185, one number per entry). Re-roll a duplicate.
The list may grow. The die is 1d[number of entries].
Re-roll a result plainly out of place for the NPC (a Dramatic Fainter who is a knight).
Do not hold both of an opposed pair (re-roll the second): Waits for the Other Speaker to
Finish and Finishes Other People's Sentences; Horses Know Their Weight and Horses Do Not;
Can Start a Fire in the Wet and Cannot Start One in the Dry; Remembers the Slight and Not
the Praise and Forgets Who Did Not. A holder of the Kindred Spirit Talent re-rolls Horses Do
Not and Cats Ignore Them.
The GM may pick a quirk for a named NPC the story needs.
Rarity does not change the chance.
Where a quirk overlaps a Talent, the Talent is the mechanic and the quirk is the story.
Record each in the profile under Personality and Values as: Name, one line on how it shows.

Tavern, Vice and Misadventure
 1. Hollow Leg: Can drink a Dwarfen miner under the table and still find their own boots the next morning, though memory of the night remains entirely optional.
 2. Professionally Lost: Never arrives at the intended destination, yet somehow stumbles directly into secret passages, hidden taverns, and plot-critical trouble.
 3. Suspiciously Well-Fed: Manages to emerge from month-long sieges, barren swamps, and dungeon cells slightly plumper than when they went in.
 4. Chronic Over-Packer: Carries a clattering, improbable mountain of pots, spare socks, folding stools, and "just-in-case" twine on every expedition.
 5. Punchable Face: Possesses an expression so naturally smug or irritating that tavern brawls reliably start with them, even when they haven't spoken a word.
 6. Rat-Taster: Has eaten so much questionable stew, mystery sausage, and dungeon fungus that actual poison barely tastes worse than supper.
 7. Sleep of the Dead: Could snore peacefully through an artillery barrage, a tavern fire, and a goblin raid, provided nobody touches their blanket.
 8. Always on the Tab: Somehow convinces barkeeps, ferrymen, and innkeepers that an "associates' guild" will settle the bill next Tuesday.
 9. Terrible Cook, Enthusiastic Chef: Treats campfire rations like high cuisine, proudly serving grey sludge with theatrical flourishes and aggressive eye contact.
10. Heavy Tipper (With Other People's Money): Generously buys rounds for the entire taproom the moment a companion's coin purse is within reach.
11. Sweet-Toothed Menace: Will risk ambush, arrest, or demonic pacts for a decent honey-cake or stolen orchard pear.
12. Loud Chewer: Crunches dried rations with a deafening, rhythmic persistence that echoes down quiet dungeon halls.
13. Blanket Thief: Automatically rolls up every bedroll within ten feet into a tight cocoon around themselves by midnight.

Social Blunders and Dubious Charm
14. Loud Whisper: Attempts at discretion carry across crowded halls like a town crier announcing a tax hike.
15. Accidental Authority: Looks so confidently busy while holding a clipboard, scroll, or lantern that guards routinely wave them straight past restricted doors.
16. Compliment That Stings: Delivers heartfelt praise that somehow sounds like a devastating insult to everyone within earshot.
17. Unfortunate Likeness: Bears an uncanny resemblance to a notorious bandit, a minor noble's bastard, or the face on half the wanted posters in the province.
18. Terrible Liar, Honest Face: Spins transparent, absurd excuses with such wide-eyed sincerity that listeners assume nobody could possibly make something that stupid up.
19. Tactless: Blurts out the exact truth nobody in the room wanted spoken aloud, usually right before a toast or treaty signing.
20. Magnet for Weirdos: Every village idiot, doomsday prophet, and three-legged stray hound in town immediately gravitates toward them for a chat.
21. Allergic to Silk: Breaks out in dramatic sneezes, hives, or uncontrollable fidgeting whenever forced to wear clean, respectable clothing.
22. Lullaby Voice: Tells bedtime stories, marching chants, or historical anecdotes so monotonous that sentries struggle to stay awake.
23. Haggles Downward: Gets so carried away arguing over prices that they occasionally talk merchants up from the original asking price.
24. Forgets Faces, Remembers Hats: Recognizes people solely by their footwear, scars, or millinery, leading to catastrophic misidentifications.
25. Over-Formal with Ruffians: Addresses gutter thugs, goblins, and tavern brawlers with stiff, aristocratic courtesy.
26. Over-Familiar with Nobles: Slaps dukes on the back, borrows signet rings to inspect them, and calls high priests "mate."
27. Apologizes to Furniture: Reflexively says "Excuse me, pardon me" whenever bumping into statues, trees, or corpses.
28. Too Honest for Commerce: Voluntarily points out every flaw, rust spot, and curse on items they are actively trying to sell.

Combat, Cowardice and Dumb Luck
29. Duck! Possesses an uncanny instinct to bend down and tie a bootlace at the exact millisecond a crossbow bolt whistles past their ear.
30. Dramatic Fainter: Drops like a sack of turnips at the first sign of real danger, routinely convincing bloodthirsty enemies they are already dead.
31. Tactical Retreat Specialist: Can sprint backward over rough terrain while shouting dignified excuses about "regrouping" and "flanking."
32. Too Stubborn to Notice: Regularly fails to realize they have been stabbed, poisoned, or cursed until someone points it out after the fight.
33. Collateral Magnet: If a stray arrow, falling chandelier, or spooked cart horse is loose in the area, it will find them first.
34. Bluffing with Empty Hands: Can make a rusty butter knife, a rolled-up map, or an empty flintlock look like a terrifying, custom-forged weapon of mass destruction.
35. Armchair Expert: Has read half a pamphlet on every subject under the sun and will happily lecture seasoned masters on how they are doing their job wrong.
36. Haunted by Minor Inconveniences: Attracts harmless, petty supernatural phenomena. Milk sours near them, candles flicker dramatically, and doors creak on cue.
37. Wrong End of the Telescope: Notices tiny, irrelevant details (like a thread on a duke's cuff) while completely missing the charging ogre behind them.
38. Creative Accounting: Can make any set of ledgers, expense claims, or tavern tabs add up in their favour, at least until someone checks the maths twice.
39. Famous Last Words: Has a habit of declaring "What could possibly go wrong?" immediately before everything does.
40. Scabbard Fumble: Rarely draws their weapon cleanly on the first tug, requiring an awkward hop or two-handed yank to free the blade.
41. Flinches Early, Swings Late: Ducks before anyone actually swings, then retaliates three seconds after the threat has wandered off.
42. Immaculate Hair in a Riot: Emerges from mud-baths, dragon fire, and tavern brawls with their hair, hat, or moustache mysteriously undisturbed.

Mishaps and Misfortune
43. Chair-Breaker: Unerringly selects the one rotted stool, cracked bench, or unstable wagon wheel in any establishment.
44. Perpetually Damp: Even in the middle of a drought, their boots squelch and their cloak smells faintly of wet hound.
45. Noise-Maker: Couldn't cross a carpeted ballroom without stepping on a squeaky floorboard, loose bell, or sleeping cat.

Knowledge, Caution and Superstition
46. Confidently Wrong: States wildly inaccurate historical and geographical "facts" with such booming conviction that locals briefly doubt themselves.
47. Over-Prepared for Specific Threats: Carries silver garlic, anti-basilisk mirrors, and troll-repellent ointment, but zero bandages.
48. Draws Maps Nobody Can Read: Sketches dungeon layouts covered in cryptic doodles, coffee rings, and meaningless labels.
49. Superstitious Overkill: Refuses to step on cracks, spits three times at black cats, and nails horseshoes over every inn door they rent.
50. Mistrusts Bridges: Insists every stone arch, wooden plank, and rope bridge is a structural death-trap, testing each step.
51. Misquotes Proverbs: Combines famous sayings into nonsense wisdom like "A bird in the hand is worth two in the soup."

Grounded and habitual (52 to 185)
52. Cold Hands: The hands stay cold even in a hall. Gloves go on first and come off last. People notice the grip.
53. Always Hungry: Eats as if the next meal is not certain. Finishes other people's crusts without asking and without apology.
54. Slow to Warm: Takes half an hour by a fire before the shoulders drop. Until then the answers are short.
55. Walks on the Outside: Takes the road-edge or the wall-side without thinking. Old habit from a bad stretch of country or a bad street.
56. Counts Steps: Knows how many paces to the gate, the well, the stair. Says the number under the breath when the light is bad.
57. Left-Handed in a Right-Handed Yard: Tools, doors, and the sword-belt are all slightly wrong. Compensates without comment.
58. Weather-Nose: Claims to smell rain or snow a day out. Is right often enough that the yard has stopped arguing.
59. Sweats in the Cold: The brow is damp in a hall that everyone else finds cool. The wool is darker at the collar by noon.

Work and Craft

60. Talks to the Work: Mutters to the rope, the timber, the blade, or the ledger while the hands move. Stops if someone is close enough to hear the words.
61. One Good Knife: Everything else can be borrowed. The knife is not lent, not shared, and is checked before sleep.
62. Measures Twice, Then a Third: Will not cut, lash, or sign until the measure has been taken again. The delay is small. The irritation is not.
63. Keeps the Offcuts: Pockets nails, cord-ends, and bent pins. The pouch is heavier than it looks and occasionally useful.
64. Hates a Dull Edge: Will stop a march to put a stone to a blade. Will not stop for a conversation.
65. Writes Small: The hand is tight and the ink is spare. A page that should take half a sheet takes a corner. Hard to read by lamplight.
66. Names the Tools: The hammer has a name. The awl has a name. Anyone who borrows one and does not return it by name has made an enemy.

Faith, Superstition and the Unveiling

67. Won't Sleep Facing Away from the Door: Turns the bed, the pallet, or the body so the back is to the wall and the face is to the opening. Does not explain it.
68. Spits at the False Moon: A small, automatic spit when the light is wrong. Not a speech. A habit from a grandmother or a bad winter.
69. Counts the Company: Knows how many went out and how many came back. Says the number once, quietly, when the gate closes.
70. Won't Share a Cup: Drinks from their own. Will pour for another. Will not drink from a vessel that has already been at someone else's mouth.
71. Marks the Dead: A nick in a stick, a stone on a cairn, a name scratched where it will weather. Does it even when no one is watching.
72. Hates Bells: Church, watch, or cattle. The sound puts the shoulders up. Will walk the long way around a ringing yard.

Speech and Silence

73. Answers the Second Question: Hears the first. Answers the one that was not asked. The first comes later, if it comes.
74. Repeats the Order: Says the order back, once, in the same words. Then does it. People who change the words mid-sentence learn not to.
75. Laughs Once: One short sound, usually at the wrong moment. Not mockery. The timing is simply off.
76. Speaks to the Horse: The horse gets the longer sentences. People get the short ones.
77. Finishes Other People's Sentences: Correctly, and too soon. The speaker stops. The air goes thin.
78. Hums the Same Four Notes: A work-song fragment, or a hymn with the words worn off. Starts when the hands are busy and stops when they are not.

Company and Coin

79. Pays in Small Coin: Breaks a silver into brass before spending it. Likes the count in the palm. Dislikes a single piece that has to be changed.
80. Remembers the Debt: Not the grudge. The exact sum, the date, and who was present. Will mention it once, years later, as a fact.
81. Shares the Last of It: The bread, the oil, the dry socks. Keeps none back and does not mention having done it.
82. Won't Sit With Their Back to the Room: Takes the bench against the wall. If the wall is taken, stands.
83. Knows Who Owes Whom: In a yard, a hall, or a taproom. Does not trade the knowledge. Uses it only when the count is wrong.
84. Leaves Before the Toast: Stands, nods, and is gone before the cup is raised. Not rudeness. The room has already been enough.

Animals, Road and Country

85. Dogs Like Them: Strays, yard-dogs, and hounds that belong to other people come to the hand. They do not call them.
86. Horses Do Not: Mounts sidle, lay ears, or refuse the mounting block. The ride goes ahead anyway. The ride is not graceful.
87. Finds the Dry Spot: In a camp, a barn, or a ditch. The ground under them is the least wet. They do not offer to share it.
88. Walks Faster Alone: The pace drops when there is company. Alone they cover ground as if the light is failing, which it often is.
89. Reads the Smoke: Knows a cook-fire from a signal from a roof gone wrong by the colour and the lean of it. Says so once.

Small Cruelties and Kindnesses

90. Corrects the Knot: Will retie another's lashing without being asked. The knot is better. No one is thanked and no one thanks.
91. Saves the Ugly One: In a litter, a drove, or a choice of mounts, takes the one no one else wants. Does not say why.
92. Won't Kill a Thing Twice: If the first blow should have done it and did not, finishes it clean and does not speak of the first.
93. Keeps a Child's Name: Remembers the name of a child met once, in a yard or on a road, and uses it if they meet again.
94. Looks Away at the End: When a thing is dying and there is nothing to be done, finds a task for the hands and does not watch.

Body and Bearing

95. Cracks the Knuckles: Does it before a hard job, a hard talk, or a long wait. The sound is the only warning.
96. Rolls the Shoulders: A small, habitual set of the harness or the coat. People who know them know the talk is over when they do it.
97. Chews the Inside of the Cheek: A ridge of scar tissue on one side. They do not notice they are doing it.
98. Stands With the Weight on One Leg: The other is slightly turned out. An old knee, or an old habit from a saddle.
99. Breathes Through the Mouth: In cold air the breath shows more than it should. In a quiet room it is the loudest thing about them.
100. Squints in Good Light: The eyes narrow even when the sun is behind cloud. Distance work costs them more than they admit.
101. Picks at a Scar: An old cut on the hand or the forearm. The nail finds it when they are thinking.
102. Sleeps in Their Boots: Or with them within arm's reach. The laces are never fully undone.
103. Washes the Hands and Not the Face: The fingers are clean. The jaw and the neck are not. A trade habit that survived the trade.
104. Shivers Once and Stops: A single hard shake on coming in from the cold. After that they are still.

Food, Drink and the Table

105. Eats the Fat First: Leaves the lean. Or the reverse. Either way the plate is finished in an order no one else uses.
106. Will Not Eat Alone: If there is no company they wait, or they do not eat. A full plate in an empty room unsettles them.
107. Salts Everything: Even fruit. Even bread that was already salted. The pouch of salt is as regular as the knife.
108. Drinks Water Like Ale: Long pulls, no pause, the cup set down hard. Those who sip look at them and look away.
109. Saves the Heel: The end of the loaf, the last onion, the rind. It goes into a cloth. It comes out two days later.
110. Won't Touch Eel: Or tripe, or blood sausage, or whatever the local cheap meat is. Will go hungry before eating it.
111. Eats Standing: Even when there is a bench. The plate is held in one hand. Sitting to eat feels like stopping.
112. Shares From the Point of the Knife: Offers a cut of whatever they are eating, on the blade, without a word. Accepting it means something. Refusing it means something else.

Sleep and the Dark

113. Wakes at the Same Hour: Whether needed or not. The habit is older than the job.
114. Sleeps With a Light: A stub of candle, a banked coal, a lamp turned low. Full dark is not rest.
115. Talks in Their Sleep: Fragments. Names. Sometimes an order. The people nearest learn not to answer.
116. Cannot Sleep in a Room With a Window: Or can only sleep in one. The preference is fixed and not explained.
117. Checks the Latch Twice: Door, shutter, or gate. The second check is the one that lets them lie down.
118. Dreams of the Same Road: Mentioned once, if asked. Not described. The telling would not help.

Tools, Kit and the Hands

119. Wraps the Grip: Leather, cord, or cloth on anything held for long. Bare wood or bare iron does not suit the hand.
120. Keeps a Spare Lace: Boot, belt, or scabbard. The spare is older than the one in use.
121. Oils What Does Not Need Oil: Hinges, buckles, the pommel. The habit is older than the reason.
122. Loses Gloves and Not Knives: The knives stay. The gloves are always the pair about to be bought.
123. Ties Knots That Only They Untie: Efficient, ugly, and personal. Anyone else wastes time on them and learns to ask.
124. Mends in the Evening: A tear, a loose sole, a frayed edge. The work is small and it is done before sleep.

Roads, Weather and Country

125. Faces the Wind: Turns into it rather than away. The habit looks like defiance. It is only how they hear.
126. Reads the Birds: Not as a woodsman reads them. As someone who has been surprised once and does not mean to be again.
127. Hates Loose Stone: On a path, a stair, or a yard. Kicked aside or stepped over. Never left.
128. Smells the Forge Before Seeing It: Or the tannery, or the river. The nose arrives first and the face shows it.
129. Will Not Cross New Ice: Or new snow over old. They go around. The long way is the one they trust.
130. Counts the Bridges: On a route they know. A missing one, or a new one, is worth a longer look.

Speech, Silence and the Room

131. Answers With a Number: How far, how many, how long. The colour of the answer is left out.
132. Waits for the Other Speaker to Finish: Even when the other is wrong and taking their time. The correction comes after, and once.
133. Uses the Old Name: For a place, a tool, or a person. The new name is known. It is not the one said.
134. Speaks Slower to Strangers: Not from kindness. From care. The words are the same. The gaps are longer.
135. Repeats a Phrase: One line, worn smooth. "It'll hold." "Not yet." "Ask the captain." It does the work of a longer speech.
136. Goes Quiet When the Room Gets Loud: The opposite of most people. Noise is when they listen.

Coin, Debt and the Yard

137. Counts the Change: Every time. Even when the sum is small and the other party is known. The count is the courtesy.
138. Pays the Exact Sum: No round number, no "keep it." The brass is counted out and the hand is closed.
139. Owes No One and Likes It: Will go without rather than take a favour that cannot be returned the same day.
140. Keeps a Tally Stick: Not for the yard. For themselves. Nicks for days worked, or days owed, or days they do not want to forget.
141. Will Not Gamble the Knife: Or the boots, or the coat. Other things can go on the table. Those cannot.
142. Buys the Same Thing: Ale, bread, tobacco, or nothing. The order does not change. The barkeep stops asking.

Animals and Beasts

143. Horses Know Their Weight: They settle under them faster than under others. They do not remark on it.
144. Will Not Kick a Dog: Even a bad one. They will shout, they will bar a door, they will not use the foot.
145. Cats Ignore Them: Entirely. They have stopped trying to understand it.
146. Knows the Sound of Their Own Beast: Among others. A horse, a hound, an ox. The wrong one in the dark is noted at once.
147. Feeds the Birds and Denies It: Crumbs on a sill, grain on a step. If asked, the wind did it.

Faith, Mark and Habit

148. Touches the Lintel: Going out. A palm on the wood or the stone. In and out. The door is not just a door.
149. Won't Whistle After Dark: An old rule from a mark or a mother. Kept without the story.
150. Turns a Coin in the Pocket: When deciding. The sound is small. People who know them wait for it to stop.
151. Bows to the Empty Altar: Or the empty niche, or the place where a shrine was. The habit outlasted the reason.
152. Keeps a Saint's Day They Do Not Name: One day in the year no work is done if it can be helped. They do not say whose day it is.
153. Spits Before a Bargain: Not on the ground in front of the other party. Off to the side. The bargain is then the bargain.

Company and the Edge of It

154. Sits at the End of the Bench: Not the head. The end. They can leave without climbing over anyone.
155. Will Not Be the Last to Leave: Or will not be the first. One or the other, fixed. The room learns which.
156. Remembers the Slight and Not the Praise: The slight is exact. The praise was noise and has already gone.
157. Stands When an Elder Enters: Or when someone of rank does. The habit is from a house that had rules. It has not been dropped.

Small Competences and Small Failures

158. Can Start a Fire in the Wet: Not quickly, and not with a show. It catches. That is the whole trick.
159. Cannot Start One in the Dry: Some days. The tinder is wrong, or the hands are, or the patience is. They do not explain.
160. Ties a Load That Does Not Shift: On a cart, a pack, or a raft. Others retie theirs at the first mile. This one does not need it.
161. Loses the Path in Country They Know: Once in a while. The country has not changed. They have. They do not like it.
162. Hears the Loose Shoe: On a horse, a cart, or a person. The sound is wrong and they say so before anyone else.
163. Mends What They Did Not Break: A gate, a hinge, a fence rail. They were passing. It was there. They do not leave it.

The Long Memory

164. Knows the Winter It Happened: Not the year by the calendar. By the winter. "The year the river froze to the bend." That is the date used.
165. Keeps a Dead Person's Habit: A way of folding a coat, a phrase, a stance. They do not notice they have taken it on.
166. Will Not Say the Name: Of a place, a person, or a year. The avoidance is consistent. Asking twice does not help.
167. Remembers the Price: Of a thing bought years ago. Not from greed. From the habit of knowing what things cost.
168. Has One Story They Tell: The same one, when a story is required. It is short. It is true enough. There is no second.

Dirt, Cleanliness and the Body's Record

169. Scrapes the Boots Before Entering: Even a barn. Even a tent. The threshold is the threshold.
170. Trims the Nails Short: For the work. A long nail catches. They keep them back and do not think of it as care.
171. Lets the Stubble Come In on the Road: Shaves or trims in a place with a mirror and hot water. Not before.
172. The Scar Is Older Than the Story: People ask. The answer given is short and is not the first one they had. The short one has replaced it.
173. Washes the Wound and Not the Shirt: The cut is clean. The cloth around it is not. Priorities are visible.

Weather in the Bones

174. Sleeps Badly Before a Thaw: The air is wrong. They wake more than once and do not say why in the morning.
175. Hates a Still Day: Wind is information. A day with none makes them check the horizon more often.
176. Works Better in the Cold: The hands are slower to start and then they are fine. Heat makes them short.
177. Will Not Work in a Thunderstorm: Tools down, under cover, until it passes. Not fear of the loud. An old rule about iron and open ground.

The Edge of Usefulness

178. Can Be Relied On for the Dull Part: The count, the watch, the second shift. The part no one wants. They take it.
179. Cannot Be Relied On for the Speech: If someone must talk to the stranger, the officer, or the hall, it should not be them.
180. Finishes the Job and Leaves the Credit: The work is done. Their name is not on it. They prefer it that way and will not explain why if asked.
181. Starts What They Do Not Finish: A mending, a tally, a story. Something else arrives. The first thing waits.
182. Keeps the Worst Watch Willingly: The cold one, the late one, the one after bad news. They sleep after. That is the bargain they make with themselves.
183. Has a Limit They Do Not Advertise: A kind of work, a kind of order, a kind of company. They will do a great deal before reaching it. At the limit they stop, and the stop is final.
184. Remembers Who Helped: Not the grand gesture. The ordinary one. A share of a fire, a word at a gate, a hand with a load. It is returned if possible, and if not the debt is not forgotten.
185. Forgets Who Did Not: Or rather, no list is kept. The absence of a grudge is its own habit. Some people find this harder to read than anger.


--------------------------------------------------
SKILLS AND EXPERIENCE SPEND
--------------------------------------------------
After characteristics are generated, list the skills the NPC possesses.
Skills begin at Unknown unless granted by pre-adulthood development
or purchased with Experience.
 
Calculate the NPC’s total Experience from age and rarity
(see Core_EXP.txt):
Adult years (normally from 16) × rarity rate.
 
Spend the full pool before the NPC is met.
Experience may be spent on skills or on characteristics.
Spend only on advances that match the occupation, upbringing, and lived experience.
Characteristic advances use the cost in Core_EXP.txt (+1 = 10 XP × current tens digit).
Do not leave unspent Experience unless the GM explicitly reserves it.
 
General rule for expenditure
1. Top priority: any skills required for the profession or trade.
   Buy these to the rank the occupation needs before other spending.
2. After the profession skills are covered, aim for a loose 50/50
   allocation between further skills and characteristic advances.
   The split is a guide, not a hard lock. A labourer may lean
   toward Strength and Toughness; a clerk may lean toward
   Intelligence and relevant Lore. Keep the balance visible in the record.
 
Languages and scripts
Speak Language and Read/Write are per language and per script. Record each by name.
Languages and scripts gained free before 18 are Known and cost nothing.
Record the order in which any further language or script was bought with
Experience. The Polyglot and Lettered Mind ladders (Core_Talents_Failings_Afflictions.txt,
7b) price by that order.

Record each skill with its final rank and the Experience spent to reach it.
List the remaining unspent total (normally zero).
Ageing follows this spend (AGEING below).
 
Example layout under Characteristics:
 
Skills
Drive (Cart)          Experienced   525 XP
Athletics             Proficient    225 XP
Trade (Porter)        Known          75 XP
Awareness             Known          75 XP
...
Total spent: xxxx XP
Unspent: 0 XP
 
--------------------------------------------------
AGEING
--------------------------------------------------
Run after the Experience spend, before the characteristics are recorded as final.
Rules are in Core_EXP.txt under AGEING.

1. Physical group: Strength, Toughness, Agility, Weapon Skill, Ballistic Skill.
   One roll per characteristic at each five-year mark after 40
   (45, 50, 55 ...) up to the NPC's age.
2. Mental group: Intelligence, Willpower, Perception, Fellowship.
   One roll per characteristic at each five-year mark after 60
   (65, 70, 75 ...) up to the NPC's age.
3. At a mark, roll 1d100. If the roll is equal to or below (Age at that mark − 10),
   the characteristic loses 1 point. Roll for each characteristic separately.
4. Floor of 10. Skills and Talents are unchanged.
5. Record the final characteristics. Note the total points lost for each group
   in the record, so a gnarled hand or a failing memory can be played.

Example: a Common carter aged 52 has a Strength of 34 after the Experience spend.
Marks reached: 45 (35%) and 50 (40%). He rolls 1d100 twice for Strength, and the
same for each other physical characteristic. A roll of 28 and a roll of 71 cost
him 1 point. His final Strength is 33.
Under 45 there are no rolls. Under 65 the mental group makes none.

Ageing does not change the Peoples phenotype, height, or Looks.
Describe the toll in the appearance notes: grey, stooped, slow to rise.


--------------------------------------------------
SCENE POPULATION
--------------------------------------------------
When a scene requires more than one NPC, generate them together
under the same context.
 
Procedure
1. Establish the shared context once
   (place, time, culture, what is happening, who would reasonably be present).
2. Decide how many NPCs the scene needs and what roles they fill.
   Do not generate bystanders who have no function in the scene.
3. For each NPC:
   - Run the full generation sequence (context already set, then stats, then bio).
   - Write one internal_thoughts iteration for this scene only.
   - Write the CURRENT STATE fields (disposition toward the PC, last met, current goal).
   - Add Relations entries pointing at the other NPCs generated for the same scene,
     each with its own X/Y/Z from this NPC's point of view.
4. Mark every profile with the generation note:
   Generated as if a PC interaction had been triggered in this scene.
   Only one iteration has been written.
   Further iterations occur only on subsequent PC interactions.
 
Named NPCs who are known to the group but not physically present
(example: a sibling at another camp) may be generated for the Relations list
but receive no scene iteration.
 
The system may populate a yard, a road, a hall, or a drive
with a working team, a household, or a watch
when the context supports it.
It does not populate a scene with unnamed extras who will never be addressed.
