# Bestiary_Rules.txt
# Rules for Interpreting HSI and RHK Scores in Bestiary Entries

GLOBAL HUMAN POPULATION
Virath’s current estimated human population is locked at approximately 170 million.
This figure accounts for the limited founding population (5–10k colonists), several millennia of growth, extreme topography, and ongoing attrition from the Unveiling.


--------------------------------------------------
PLACEMENT ORDER
--------------------------------------------------

Every creature, Unveiling or natural, is placed in this order:

1. Domain
2. Biome
3. HSI

Domain is a hard gate.
Biome is a quick gate.
HSI is the score.

Do not run HSI on a thing that has already failed Domain.


--------------------------------------------------
DOMAIN
--------------------------------------------------

Domain is where the creature actually lives. It is not a climate.

Land
Freshwater
Marine
Air
Amphibious

Write one Domain, or two only if the creature truly uses both (otter: Land, Freshwater).

- Land against a Marine region → stop
- Freshwater against a dry ridge with no water feature → stop
- Marine against the Hornwood → stop

Air does not ignore the ground. A flying hunter still takes the land or sea biome under it, then HSI.

Domain is not the same as a water biome tag. A pike is Domain: Freshwater and Biome: Lake, River. A seal is Domain: Marine, Land and Biome: Strand, Cold sea.


--------------------------------------------------
BIOME
--------------------------------------------------

Biome uses the locked list in Geography_Rules.txt.
Do not invent tags.

On a creature line:

Domain: Land
Biome: Boreal, Alpine, Polar
HSI: Temperature: X–Y | Rainfall: X–Y | Human Density: X–Y | Flora: X–Y | Fauna: X–Y | Topography: X–Y | Hydrology: X–Y

Biome: Any skips the biome gate. Use it for things that follow people or water wherever they go (rat, crow, many fish). Then HSI only.

HOW THE GATE READS A LOCAL REGION

The Local Region has one Biome and any Sub-biomes.

- Region Biome is in the creature’s list → candidate for the country as a whole. Then HSI against the region, or against a feature if the encounter is tied to one.
- Only a Sub-biome matches → candidate on those features only. Then HSI against the matching feature, not against the region line.
- No overlap → skip
- Any → skip this gate

A red deer tagged Temperate, Boreal belongs in Kannavald as a whole.
An ibex tagged Alpine belongs on Ebon Spire and the massif, not on Underwood Shores.
A lake-trout tagged Lake, River belongs on Kolvatn and the inlets, not in the Hornwood.

A stray can cross the biome gate. Domain cannot.


--------------------------------------------------
HSI – HABITAT SUITABILITY INDEX
--------------------------------------------------

HSI uses the same seven measures as Geography_Rules.txt.
Each is a 0–5 range of preferred conditions, derived from the creature’s description.

Temperature
Rainfall
Human Density
Flora
Fauna
Topography
Hydrology

Write them on one line:

Temperature: X–Y | Rainfall: X–Y | Human Density: X–Y | Flora: X–Y | Fauna: X–Y | Topography: X–Y | Hydrology: X–Y

A single preferred value is written as X–X.
A dash (—) means the creature is indifferent to that axis. Indifferent axes are omitted from the percentage; they are not counted as full overlap.

Flora and Fauna are separate.
- Flora is preferred plant cover.
- Fauna is preferred animal life around the creature, not the creature’s own numbers.
A closed canopy with little game is Flora 4, Fauna 2.
A steppe with great herds is Flora 2, Fauna 4.

Human Density is how much settlement the creature will tolerate or seek.
Low means it avoids people. High means it hunts, haunts, or scavenges among them.

Oceanic features use the oceanic meanings from Geography_Rules.txt.
Do not mix land meanings and oceanic meanings in the same comparison.

Height is not an HSI axis.


--------------------------------------------------
COMPARING HSI TO A REGION
--------------------------------------------------

Use this only after Domain and Biome have passed.

If the encounter is in the country as a whole, align the seven categories with the Local Region’s Environmental Scores.
If the encounter is tied to one feature, use that feature’s scores instead.
If only a Sub-biome matched, you must use a matching feature.

If the date is known, apply the climate template of that region or feature first.
Season shifts Temperature, Rainfall, Flora, and Fauna only. Then compare.

For each axis the creature actually uses:
- Region or feature score inside the preferred range = 1 point (full overlap)
- Score 1 step outside the range = 0.5 points (partial overlap)
- Score 2 or more steps outside = 0 points (no overlap)

Suitability Percentage = total points ÷ number of axes used.

Likelihood of Presence
- <30% = Unlikely (rare sightings)
- 30–60% = Possible (occasional presence)
- 60–80% = Likely (regular encounters)
- >80% = Highly suitable (common in the area)

Adjust final likelihood by the creature’s RHK Rarity (lower rarity reduces likelihood, higher rarity increases it).

Worked example
Creature
Domain: Land
Biome: Boreal, Alpine
HSI: Temperature 1–3 | Rainfall 3–5 | Human Density 0–2 | Flora 3–5 | Fauna 2–4 | Topography 3–5 | Hydrology 2–4

Kannavald Deepwood (Harvest, country as a whole)
Biome: Boreal — match
Temperature 2 | Rainfall 4 | Human Density 2 | Flora 4 | Fauna 2 | Topography 3 | Hydrology 3

Temperature 2 inside 1–3 → 1
Rainfall 4 inside 3–5 → 1
Human Density 2 inside 0–2 → 1
Flora 4 inside 3–5 → 1
Fauna 2 inside 2–4 → 1
Topography 3 inside 3–5 → 1
Hydrology 3 inside 2–4 → 1

Total 7 ÷ 7 = 100% → Highly suitable, then apply Rarity.

Same creature on Ebon Spire (Harvest)
Biome: Alpine — still a match
Temperature 1 | Rainfall 4 | Human Density 0 | Flora 2 | Fauna 1 | Topography 5 | Hydrology 2

Temperature 1 inside → 1
Rainfall 4 inside → 1
Human Density 0 inside → 1
Flora 2 is 1 outside 3–5 → 0.5
Fauna 1 is 1 outside 2–4 → 0.5
Topography 5 inside → 1
Hydrology 2 inside → 1

Total 6 ÷ 7 ≈ 86% → Highly suitable, thinner than the basin.

Same creature on Underwood Shores
Biome: Strand — no overlap with Boreal, Alpine → skip
Do not run HSI.


--------------------------------------------------
RHK – RARITY, HARM, KNOWLEDGE
--------------------------------------------------

All RHK scores use a 1–10 scale.

RARITY (Global Population & Scarcity)
Rarity reflects approximate global numbers, calibrated against 170 million humans.

1 – Mythical (<10 global)
2 – Ultra-rare (10–50)
3 – Very rare (50–200)
4 – Rare (200–500)
5 – Uncommon (500–2,000)
6 – Occasional (2,000–5,000)
7 – Common (5,000–20,000)
8 – Frequent (20,000–50,000)
9 – Abundant (50,000–200,000)
10 – Ubiquitous (>200,000)

Regional modifiers of ±1 to ±3 may be applied based on habitat.

INDIVIDUAL HARM (Threat of a single creature)
1 – Negligible vs civilian
2 – Minimal vs unarmed person
3 – Low vs armed individual
4 – Moderate vs skilled fighter
5 – Significant vs small group
6 – High vs patrol
7 – Severe vs company
8 – Extreme vs battalion
9 – Deadly vs army
10 – Apocalyptic vs kingdom

COLLECTIVE HARM (Threat of groups)
Derived as a combination of Rarity and Individual Harm.
As a practical guide:
- Low Rarity + High Individual Harm = still limited collective threat
- High Rarity + Moderate Individual Harm = serious regional problem
Use judgement rather than strict formula. Cap at 10.

KNOWLEDGE (How well the creature is understood)
1 – Anecdotal / mythical
2 – Sparse tales
3 – Basic folk knowledge
4 – Moderate regional lore
5 – Documented by hunters/clergy
6 – Studied by scholars or specialised groups
7 – Detailed behavioural understanding
8 – Expert (taming, warding, or exploitation methods exist)
9 – Comprehensive
10 – Fully mastered

Regional Knowledge modifiers of ±1 to ±3 may be applied according to cultural exposure and history with the creature.


--------------------------------------------------
ENCOUNTER GUIDANCE
--------------------------------------------------

Likelihood of encountering a creature in a suitable area is influenced by both its HSI suitability percentage and its Rarity score.

Quantity present in an encounter generally scales with Rarity:
- Rarity 1–3: usually solitary or pairs
- Rarity 4–5: small groups
- Rarity 6–7: packs or modest numbers
- Rarity 8–10: potentially large numbers or swarms (tempered by Individual Harm)

Unveiling conditions (False Moon, thin veil areas, megastructures, etc.) can temporarily increase both likelihood and aggression.


These rules exist to keep bestiary entries consistent and usable. They are tools for the GM, not rigid constraints. When in doubt, favour atmosphere, logic, and the established tone of Virath.
