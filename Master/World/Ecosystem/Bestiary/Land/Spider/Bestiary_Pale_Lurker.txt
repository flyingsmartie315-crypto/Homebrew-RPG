Overview
Pale Lurkers are the great spiders of cold stone and of the dark the stone taught the wood. Thick grey-white fur, a span of ten to sixteen feet, eyes poor in sun. Native ground is the adit, the rift, the alpine hole. They take a boreal wood only where the firs are old enough and thick enough to rebuild that dark: sun-starved, hyper-shadowed, the canopy doing the work of a limestone roof. They do not spin the forty-foot vertical circle of an Orb-Wright. They weave a pale horizontal sheet between ancient roots, root-plates, or a low ravine, and the sheet chokes the light. They do not drop from a hub. They lumber out of a hollow root-mass, or the first bend of an adit, and drag the catch into the black. Venom does not drop a man in a breath. It steals heat until the body goes the way of the cave. They are not Old Eight-Lines. They are not Deepwights.

Signs
- A pale horizontal sheet across roots, a root-plate, or a low ravine
- Thick sheet-silk across a shaft, adit, or rift, pale as limestone
- Fur-rub on wet stone or on the bark of the den-tree, grey-white
- Bats, goat, or a man drawn in and wrapped, cold to the touch
- Slow drag-marks into the dark, or into the hollow root
- A working that the clickers will not use
- On a False Moon, a pale bulk at a cave-mouth or a root-mouth after dusk

Behaviour
One sheet, one female. She sits the web and the dark behind it. In stone that dark is the first bend. In boreal fir it is the hollow root-mass. Movement is slow until the silk pulls, then the reach is long and the haul is into the black. Prey is bat, stray stock, the explorer, and — if a bear dens in her mouth — the sleeper, wrapped and waited on. She does not run a bear on open ground.

Bright light ruins them. Open wood, a ride, a logged fringe: they lumber, miss footing, and go for the nearest hole. The dense canopy is not that wood. It is the adit with bark. False Moon hunger is what puts one on the scree below a mine-mouth, or on the edge of the sheet after dusk. Dawn puts it back, or kills it.

Venom is slow. Bite, then cold, then stillness. Fire and a hard fight in the first minutes are the chance. A man left in the sheet is already the cave’s, or the root’s.

Young scatter down side-rifts or along the dark under the firs. Most die on a Deepwight, a fall, a rival sheet, or each other. A second adult on the same mouth is a fight, not a pair.

Encounter
One. The sheet is the encounter, not a duel in a hall.
Cutting silk or pushing past it starts the haul (Harm 6).
Open ground: slow and half-blind. Do not give it a ride or a logged fringe.
Boreal floor only under the old canopy, Flora 4–5, Temperature 0–2. The sheet is horizontal. The haul comes out of the root, not down from a hub.
False Moon: a mouth-watch on the scree, or at the sheet’s edge. Same Harm, worse nerve.
Deepwights in the same hill use other rifts or lose people to the sheet. Not a pack.
