Overview
Horned Ones are the brutes of the Cloven.
The ditch-imp is the hand and the sting.
This is the mass. A goat-head or a bull-head
on a man’s frame gone wide, crowned, and
stooped for the charge.

The horns are the rank. The herd reads
them before it hears the roar. A small
horn is a small brute. A matched sweep
is a lord of the wood, until a larger
pair takes the stone.

They despise a shield. The hide takes
the small blow. They want two crude
weapons, or a branch, or a stolen axe.
They do not forge. They do not farm.
A ditch-imp mends the iron. This body
takes it, or it breaks a limb off a tree.

Signs
- A cloven print deeper than a goat, and wider
- Horn-scored bark at the height of a tall man
- Undergrowth broken, not slipped
- A headbutt-tree: bark gone, wood starred
- Ditch-imp camps with a larger scrape uphill
- Two crude axes, no shield, on a dead pale

Behaviour
The charge is the gait. Hind legs launch
the top-heavy frame. The point is the
shield wall, and the mass is the argument.
They are not silent. They crash the brush.
The lope is long. Dense wood for hours
does not empty them the way it empties a man.

In the fight they do not hold a rank.
Weight shifts. A swing, then the horns.
No patience. No craft. A weapon is stolen,
demanded from a ditch-imp, or a branch.

The ditch-imp is the punching bag, the
meat in front, and the smith they will
not thank. Larger kin, not yet written,
are envied. A brute looks for the challenge.
Among themselves: posture, roar, and a
headbutt that can kill. Pride, instinct,
and the stone cities. The kill is for
the False Moon.

False Moon: the herdstone, with the
under-caste drumming, and this body
in front of the raid.

Encounter
One on a trail is Harm 5. It charges
or it bellows. It does not scout.
A handful behind a ditch-imp wall is
the herd-fight. Collective 6 until
the larger kin have a page.
Do not give this body a shortbow.
That is the imp.
