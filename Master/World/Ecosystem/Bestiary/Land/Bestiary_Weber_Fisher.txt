Overview
Weber-fishers are the water-edge hangers. Riverfolk call them Old Eight-Lines. Dark blue-black or reed-mottled, short slick hair, long thin legs, hooked claws for wet stone, piling, and branch. They do not run down a man. They dangle eight silk lines into the current, each tipped with a sticky drop or a small lure, and they wait. When a line goes taut they reel, haul, bite, and wrap. They are the least of the Unveiling-adjacent water-threats, and they were here as a stable kind, not a new spawn.

Signs
- Eight thin lines in the water under a branch, rock, or piling
- Small lures or droplets on the drift
- Silk wrap in the overhang: fish-head, frog-bone, a duck’s foot
- Hook-scratch on wet timber
- A gap in the small-fish take on a favoured stretch
- On a False Moon, the lines take a faint light

Behaviour
One hang, one bird. They do not hunt as a pack and they do not keep a chief. The female owns the overhang. She sits still for hours. The lines fish for her. Prey is fish, frog, and waterfowl. A floater will be taken if it hits a line; that is all the “cleaning” they do. They do not go into the open water after a kill.

They are shy of men. Sudden move or torchlight and they go up into the cover or drop and run along the bank. They bite a person when the nest is touched or a line is cut. The venom is weak: numbness and swelling in the limb, not a drop. Children and drunks are the ones who get a hand in the silk.

Riverfolk leave a small fish on the piling or the root. The gift is to keep the hang where it is, and to keep a hand off the lines. It is not worship.

False Moon makes the silk show and the spider bolder. Then they will dangle nearer a boat or a lone rod. They still run from a lantern if it is shoved at them.

Young hatch from a sac in the same overhang and scatter. Most die. A stretch of river may hold several hangs, each its own.

Encounter
One spider on one hang. A second hang is a second beast, not a pair-bond.
Cutting lines or climbing the nest is what starts a fight.
False Moon: easier to see, closer to boats, same Harm.
Fenwight water can hold both. They are not allies. A fenwight will take a wrapped catch if it can reach it.
