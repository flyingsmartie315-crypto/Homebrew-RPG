Overview
Skitterlings are the small pest-spiders of ruin, cellar, sewer, tower-stair, and the cracked edge of town. Mottled to the local dust. Trip-lines in doorways and corners. Venom is a paralytic that takes a limb or a child, not a stag. One is a fright. A nest in an old keep is a clearing job. They eat rat and insect and then they eat the store. They are not Old Eight-Lines, not Orb-wrights, not Pale Lurkers.

Signs
- Fine trip-silk across a jamb, stair, or drain-mouth
- Dust-coloured bulk in a high corner
- Rat-husks and fly-wrap in the same turn
- Many small lines, no thirty-foot orb, no cave-sheet
- A cellar that goes quiet of mice and then of men

Behaviour
Opportunistic. They sit a corner or string a knee-high line and wait. Prey is rat, beetle, pigeon, the reaching hand. They scavenge what the dark already holds.

Mostly one to a room. In a long-empty ruin they stack: lines on lines, a swarm if you kick the nest. That is numbers, not a hunt-pack with a chief.

They follow warmth and food. Warmer sewers and southern cellars take them first. Cold alpine dark without people or rats is poor ground; that hole belongs to the Pale Lurker if it belongs to a spider at all.

Light and a ready blade end one. Fire in a nest ends the stretch. They flee a prepared group. They do not stand a door-fight.

False Moon makes them bolder on the stair and the yard-drain. It does not put an orb across the high street.

Encounter
One in a cellar is Harm 3: trip, bite, panic.
A ruin-nest is Collective 5: many lines, bad footing, more than you see.
They will be where men store grain and dump slops. Deepwood floor without a hut is a skip unless the ruin is there.
Do not run a 2–4 ft bird as a 10 ft cave-sheet.
