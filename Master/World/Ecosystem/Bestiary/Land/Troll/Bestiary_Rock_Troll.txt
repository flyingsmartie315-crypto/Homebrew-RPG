Overview
Rock trolls are the highland Unveiling of the river troll. Folk say stone-troll. Eleven to twelve feet, grumpier, and heavier. The hide is thick and rocky except the belly. Stood still against a face, it takes the colour of the stone. Freezing peaks and hot crags are both home, if there is a cave or a stand of trees to sleep in. They are not a pair at a dam. One holds a range.

Signs
- A boulder field that did not fall. Stones sitting on a path, a shelf, or a roof
- Trees uprooted and the branches ground off on the rock
- A trunk kept, chewed and split, until the hands finish it
- A hide scraped into a rough lioncloth, prey-hair still on it
- Scat of meat and pine on a high trail
- A belly-scar on a dead one. The back will not show the killing blow

Behaviour
More solitary than the river kind. A pair is a quarrel, not a household. The young stay until they are large enough to live, then they roam until a range is empty. An adult does not share.

They eat what the height gives. Goat, bird, a winter-killed stag, a man on the pass if he is alone. Omnivore in the lean months. They do not walk a town. Ambush is the boulder from above, then the fists and the claws. A trunk is a club for as long as it lasts in those hands. It is not a kept weapon. It is this week's tree.

Intelligence is the river troll's. Low. They fashion a lioncloth from torn hide. That is the horizon of the craft. No speech a man has proved. No trade.

The hide is the prize and the reason hunts fail. It blends when the troll is still. The belly does not. Fire and a deep wound in the open finish one. A cut on the back is a pause. Lords pay for the hide. The pay does not bring the hunters back.

False Moon puts them on the ridges. They throw. They uproot. Paths close under stone. Ordinary nights they hold the cave.

A young one, still under the Size line, can be taken. An adult almost never breaks. Economy_Unveiling_Beasts.txt prices the broken young, not the wild range-holder.
