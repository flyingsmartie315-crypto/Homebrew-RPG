Overview
Scylla are the smaller Unveiling kin of the kraken, of the shallows and the reef, not of the open deep. One central body, slim barbed tentacles, a faint eye-glow in stirred water. They take a swimmer or a small boat and pull it under. A prepared crew can drive one off with spear and fire. They are a shore-peril, not a navy’s problem.

Signs
- A rumble like far thunder under the hull
- Eye-glow in murk, then the water coming up
- Slim coils on a piling, a keel, or a swimmer
- Foam and a muffled cry, then a boat that sits too low
- Ink-cloud in the shallows after a miss
- Egg-mass in a hole under the reef

Behaviour
One den to a stretch of reef or rock-shelf. The female sits it. She hunts by trailing tentacles into the working water — channel, landing, the lee of a skerry — and by waiting. The rumble comes first. Then the grab. Barbs hold. The body stays low. Prey is fish, seal, man, and anything that floats small. They do not attack a high-sided ship for preference.

They are not a pack. Two on one reef is a disputed den or a mother and almost-grown young. The young leave. Most die on the next shelf.

They do not come ashore to walk. Strand is the intertidal hunt, not a land-beast’s road. Fire hurts them. Spear in the mass ends it; a severed limb is not a kill. Ink is for the escape.

False Moon puts the glow up and the hunt closer in to the boats. It does not breed a second bird on the same rock.

Encounter
One. A small boat or a swimmer is the usual take (Harm 5).
A landing-party in the water is worse ground than a deck.
Target the body. Limbs grow back slowly; they do not make two scylla.
Kraken is a different file and a different depth. Do not run a scylla as a ship-killer.
