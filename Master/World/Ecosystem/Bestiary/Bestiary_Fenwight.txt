Overview
Fenwights are a stable Unveiling strain of stagnant water. Riverfolk call them drowners. Pallid, long-limbed, webbed at finger and toe, mottled green-grey. Gills along the neck, a second lid for silt, lungs that take foul air, breath held for hours. They breed true. They keep enough cunning to twist nets, barbed hooks, and low cuts in the deep swamp. They are not a people. They are not trolls. They are not a market beast. To those who live by the water they are the fen learning to hunt.

Signs
- Ripple or splash where no fish should rise
- Web-print in silt, too long for a child
- Stilt-prints in the shallows: two deep holes, a long stride, no keel-wake
- Nets and bone hooks on a drowned path
- Half-sunken ruin, reed-mat, or hollow trunk used as a nursery
- Fish, fowl, or a traveller taken under without a cry
- Channels cut where no landing has a right to be
- Peat-smoke from a hollow that is not a landing
- Reed-and-hide rags; bone, tooth, and hair hung on poles
- Effigies of mud, gut, and stolen kit, set facing a path or a landing
- Eyes in the mist on a False Moon

Behaviour
They live in colonies: drowned stone, floating mats, hollow cypress, the backs of old channels they have cut themselves. The water is home. Land is for the raid and the breed-haul. They move through reed and shallows with little wake. On open water and soft mire they take stilts — swamp-wood, bound with gut — and cross where a boat would stick. Smell is for carrion on a slow current. Saliva carries a mild venom that softens what they hold. Skin takes leech and swamp-fever poorly.

They hunt fish, frog, fowl, and the walker who leaves the path. The kill is a drown. Webbed hands, net, and hook; pull under; wait. They do not waste a noisy fight in the open.

A pair is enough to start a colony. Young take the water in months. Riverfolk burn nests because an unchecked year fills a backwater. They do not pair for life. They hold a stretch of rot and the channels that feed it. Expelled groups move down-current and start again.

A colony answers to one dominant male. Riverfolk call him the chief. He is not a king. He keeps the best of the take and the others give way. He wears a skull-mask and a crown of antler, horn, or bone, whichever the country yields. Lesser males take lesser trophies or none. Challenge is a fight in the water. The loser leaves or is drowned.

They dress from the swamp: reed, bark, hide, gut, and the parts of what they kill. They keep trophies of man and beast and work them into trappings and fetish-effigies — poles of hair and finger-bone, mud-men stuffed with stolen cloth, bird-skulls facing the path. The work is for display and for the boundary. It is not a rite anyone has explained.

Tools are crude. Earthworks are real. A deep-swamp colony can change how a fen drains. That is labour, not law or speech. They have no houses, no names, no trade with men. They burn peat in the hollows they keep. The fire is for warmth and the drying of a catch, not for light or craft.

False Moon puts them in a swarm: gills working, eyes pale, hunts run up to boat and palisade. Ordinary nights they keep to the reed and the ruin.

Encounter
A single fenwight on a path is a drowning, not a duel (Harm 3).
A colony is nets, stilts, water, and numbers (Rarity 7, Collective 6).
The skull-mask is the dominant male. Killing him does not end the colony; another male takes the bone.
Nursery water — warm pool, mat, or trunk — is the place to burn if you mean to clear a stretch.
Effigies on a path mean the stretch is already claimed.
False Moon: more of them, closer to landings and villages.
They will not be on a dry ridge or a running alpine burn. Domain first. Then Wetland, River, Lake, or Pond. Then HSI.

Notes
- Locked name: Fenwight. Spoken name: drowner. Do not write Mirelurk or Mire-Kin.
- Domain is Freshwater and Amphibious. Marine is a skip. A dry feature is a skip.
- Rudimentary structures and peat-fires let a colony hold cooler water than the body alone would take. Kolmire at Temperature 2 is endurable, not comfortable. They do not belong on ice-fen or Polar water.
- Skull-mask and antler-crown are rank, not culture. Do not write them a court, a language, or a law.
- Bald cypress and reed-country from Flora_Temperate are their cover, not their kin.
- River/Swamp Troll is a different file: few, heavy, dam-makers. Do not run them as the same threat.
- Economy: not livestock. No honest price. Bounty and burn-money, if any, sit on the river-town, not on a market stall.
