# House_of_the_Spent_Working.txt
# Kinship of the Spent One. Working hall account and GM constraint.
# Session draft. Read with Wound_Current_Working.txt.
# No Earth-names in hall speech.

WHAT THIS FILE IS
The house behind the First Body: champion, mother, younger likeness, eldest witness.
It answers the four Unveiling questions only as far as this house can answer them.
It is not yet a Faith_ file. No living church is named here.

--------------------------------------------------
GM META — WHAT IS LOCKED
--------------------------------------------------
- Two natures, one spending. Neighbour’s life and the Real at once.
- Father: champion or hero. Fell facing the perils of the Unveiling.
- His death sits far outside the birth-window of the Spent One. He is the late father.
- Mother: mortal. Bore both sons. No office after death.
- Birth order: the Witness is the eldest. The Spent One is the younger.
- Likeness: the younger so resembled the champion that the floor called him the hero’s.
- The elder did not bear that likeness.
- The elder is the first saint. Hall titles: the Witness, the Chronicler.
- Virtue he embodies: humility.
- The Spent One is not a saint. He is the body used up.
- Coalescence of the Chronicler as a keeper-form is not locked.

--------------------------------------------------
HALL CLAIM — THE HOUSE
--------------------------------------------------
The champion fell in the perils. The sky was not mended.
Long after that death, outside any ordinary window, a woman of his house bore two sons.
She bore them in the ordinary way. She is not asked for. She died an ordinary death.
The younger wore the late man’s face. The floor called him the hero’s, because it could not call him anything else.
The elder kept the house-right and would not take the face.
The Real took the younger life, the one that already wore the likeness, and used it up.
The elder stayed. He attested. He wrote. He would not be pointed at.

--------------------------------------------------
THE MOTHER
--------------------------------------------------
She secures the human nature.
The body was born into a house, not deposited, not worn as a show.
She can say the brother and the Spent One came by the same bearing.
She can refuse the crowd at a birth. Birth is a thinning and wants quiet spending, not a spectacle.

She does not mediate.
She does not intercede.
She does not remain as a clean gate.
A hall that keeps her image, asks her to spend in her son’s place, or makes the bearing the gospel has built a mother-office. That office is forbidden.

--------------------------------------------------
THE FATHER
--------------------------------------------------
He is a gate-death, not a retrieved hero.
He spent himself against the perils and did not end them.
The son does not fetch him back.
A reading in which the First Body goes down to bring the champion out is a completed rescue. It makes the father’s death the real gospel. Do not write it.

He may keep a hall-name later, as the man who held a gate.
He does not join a holy family.
His likeness in the younger is inheritance in the human nature, not a mask the Real put on.
If the Real copied the hero’s face, Measure is right: the earthly body is an image, and an image is a door.

--------------------------------------------------
THE ELDEST — THE WITNESS, THE CHRONICLER
--------------------------------------------------
He is the first saint because he set the pattern saints are measured by.
A saint is a life or a death that held a leak, or a share of bread that held one, without feeding the Night Face on spectacle.
He had the eldest claim on the dead man’s name, the gate, and the face, and he yielded the face.
Later saints are measured by whether they will be unnamed.

Humility here is not softness and not Still-current cooling.
It is the Wound virtue with the spectacle cut out.
He stood where a Face could have fed: eldest son of a gate-death, brother of the likeness, the one man who could have sold the face or been sold as its return.
He pointed at the younger and would not be pointed at.

WITNESS
House-title. Not the Opener use of “Witnesses.”
Opener cells mean people who treat looking into the tear as piety.
His witness is the house. Halls keep the distinction, or ash-listeners will steal the name.

He attests three clauses only.
1. The mother bore both sons in the ordinary way.
2. The younger wore the champion’s face, and the elder did not.
3. The younger was spent.

He does not explain the gap.
He does not name the instrument.
He does not report a return.
A fourth clause is no longer witness. It is a gospel.

CHRONICLER
The same refusal in writing.
The account exists so the spending cannot be owned by one mouth, and so it cannot swell into a passion-play.
Humility in the text is omission.
The chronicle may record the fall, the two sons long after, the likeness, and the using-up.
It may not record a childhood, a mother-office, companions, the manner of the spending, or an empty grave.
If the book saves, is kissed as a face, or is closed as the last word, it has failed.
The page is covered on a False-Moon night. No new line is cut while the second moon hangs.

Bread joins the body that holds.
The page does not.
The heir who keeps the blood as the church has failed the humility.

--------------------------------------------------
FOUR HALL-ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------
Short enough that the chronicle cannot swallow them.

1. What is the False Moon?
The spending seen from the ground. Not a feast. Not Lorn.
The champion met its perils and fell. The younger was used up long after. Neither closed it.

2. What may be done on the nights it hangs?
Stay in the house. Cover the page. No new writing. No midwifery if it can wait.
Iron and salt as the floor already keeps them. Do not narrate what is heard.

3. What are the things that walk?
Own heat given face. Not hungry kin of this house. Not a test sent by the champion.
A neighbour becoming a Face is already on the same gradient as a skinwight.

4. What happens to the dead?
The father stays at the gate he held. He is not fetched.
The elder speaks over the grave because he will not claim it.
Uncommitted bodies are doors. The chronicle does not commit them. Watching and bread do.

--------------------------------------------------
WHO MAY CLAIM HIM
--------------------------------------------------
Keepers
He wrote so the holding could be joined and not finished.

Sealers
He wrote so the father’s one spending would not be repeated by a vanity in the father’s face.
They may claim the book only while it refuses the ending.

Soft-Wound
He did not hang his kin for a face.

Hard-Wound
He could stand at a hanging and refuse the credit.

Measure
The book is a partner-face. The answer is that the chronicle does not join anyone. Bread does.
Do not erase this quarrel.

Station-halls
They will try to give the elder the office and the younger the function.
Permitted only if the office cannot spend in the younger’s place.

--------------------------------------------------
WHAT PLAYERS AND PRIESTS KNOW
--------------------------------------------------
A Wound-hall priest can recite the three clauses and the four answers.
He does not know substrates, faith-mass, or that keeper-forms are condensates.
A learned reader may call the eldest first of the unnamed.
That is still hall speech, not Aperti mechanics.
The personal names of mother, father, and both sons are not in this file.
Inventing them in play is a local custom, not a lock.
Imperial halls may keep the birthday as a day and month, and count A.D. from the spending.
That reckoning is in ImperialCult.txt. It does not narrate a childhood, and it does not number the age yet.
