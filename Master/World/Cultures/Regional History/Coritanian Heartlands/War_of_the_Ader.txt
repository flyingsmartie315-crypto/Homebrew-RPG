# War_of_the_Ader

First war of the Whitmark cycle.
Isern chronicle-name: Harthold’s Folly.
Cortani hall-name: the Ader War.

See Wars_of_the_Whitmarks.txt for the cycle index.

LOCKED NAMES
--------------------------------------------------
Border river: the Ader. Outflow of Rhonmere.
Lake at the Rhennod foot: Rhonmere. Tale-name: Giant’s Pool. Isern rolls: Adersee. Old Syllvani: Llyn Rhön.
Highway river: the Virelle (old Cortani). the Audava (contemporary).
Capital: Vayrac (old). Audecourt (contemporary).
First town taken: Aderhall, on the Ader.
Second objective: Berthall — the Giant’s Hearth / Giant’s Hall. Hilly wooded country on the Ader, 165 miles upriver from the crossing-reach before the water narrows.

Isern king of the first host: Harthold.
Slain border lord: Aymon of Aderhall.
Son, main figure of the first war: Evran of the Ader.

Independent horselords in the first war (no high king):
- Audovald of Vayrac — Lord of Vayrac. Richest hall, slowest to ride.
- Rolund of Berthall — Giant’s Hearth. Name-link to the pool and the hall.
- Loher of Chevalac — neighbour of Aderhall. Commits around the fall of Berthall. Opportunistic. Not close with Evran and Rolund.
- Sellis of Elaynac
Aymon of Aderhall was the fifth seat. After his death the seat rides in Evran.

Kannavi in the first host: paid stave-companies citing Osric’s compact. The hamlets stay home. Not a nation marching.
Contingent lord: Hallvard Ask of Westwood — second son of the Westwood ruling house.
Eyrenhost: not present in the first war.

PERSONS OF INTEREST — ACT 1
--------------------------------------------------
Isern / Kannavi
- Harthold — king of the first host. Dies at Folly. Barrow on the hill.
- Hallvard Ask of Westwood — Kannavi contingent lord. Second son. Holds the Ader after Berthall. Relinquishes when Harthold falls. Carries lessons north.

Cortani
- Aymon of Aderhall — slain at the threshold.
- Evran of the Ader — son of Aymon. Guerrilla. Last stand. Raises the barrow.
- Rolund of Berthall — Giant’s Hearth. Evacuates, breaks out, last stand.
- Audovald of Vayrac — richest hall, slowest to ride. Commits late. Takes the ransom-bounty.
- Loher of Chevalac — opportunist. Commits around Berthall’s fall.
- Sellis of Elaynac — independent hall. Present so a rival cannot claim the prize alone.

--------------------------------------------------
ISERNVOLK — HOW THEY STAND (FIRST WAR)
--------------------------------------------------
This kit is Harthold’s host only. Not Thormund. Not the later iron-riders.
Cavalry is scarce and elite. They are not a horse-people yet.
Crossbows are a signature, not the whole host.

What they have not learned yet
Levy spears are still ordinary length. They have not met full Cortani heavy horse in the open.
Spikoar is born on the Ader as a barge tool during this war. It is not standing-line doctrine yet.
Lengthened poles and Spikoar-in-the-line are written after Folly.

Levy
Good shoes. Hard-wearing clothing that feels scratchy.
Long gambeson as standard, often under a simple coloured waffenrock for identification.
Mittens favoured on the front — padded cloth, not mail.
Padded coif and some form of wide-brimmed kettle hat.
Sidearms: hatchet, club, dagger, whatever is to hand.
Main pair: ordinary-length spear and a round shield.
Crossbow rare at this tier. The device is complex.
Thrown weapons and slings always an option.

Standing soldiers
Mail shirts appear, and coifs; mittens where they matter.
Padded chausses and cuffed boots.
Front rank still trusts the round shield as primary cover.
Rear ranks fight over them with standard-length spears or one-handed arms.
Nasal helms and kettle hats still the look of this class.
Dedicated crossbows more common. They shoot from behind pavise.

High end
Career soldiers, noble retainers, the few who can be spared as horse.
Full visored helms. Segmented rigid layers over a mailed torso.
Longer mail hems and sleeves. Mail to the limbs, with rigid elbows and knees or splinted work.
Fashion of this generation: round shield slung on the back, two-handed axe; greatsword for the wealthiest.
Some take spiked polearms and forearm shields and trust the armour.
Higher-poundage crossbows with mechanical advantage appear and stay rare.
This rung is small.

--------------------------------------------------
CORTANI — HOW THEY RIDE (FIRST WAR)
--------------------------------------------------
This kit is the five halls of Harthold’s war. Not the unified North-facing host after Evran’s death.
Herd-people at war. Horse is wealth and movement.
Syllvani longbow memory remains. They favour the shorter compound for the saddle.
The warrior caste is the trump. It is not the average rider.
Full heavy horse in the open is rare until Folly. That is Audovald’s late commitment, not the everyday war.

Below levy
Walkers and poor herders. Not the war-host. They exist so “every Cortani is mounted” does not leak into settlement files.

Levy — freemen
Owns one or more horses. No warhorse.
Simple light saddle for riding, not for couching a lance.
Long textile coverage, thinner than Isern work: aketon, not the heavy gambeson.
Good outdoor clothing and high boots.
A helmet is heavily favoured but not standardised. A thick hat is the floor.
Kite shield essential. Spear essential. Hand weapon desirable.
Thrown weapons common.
They ride to the best ground, then often dismount into a shieldwall.

Soldier
Multiple mounts; one may be a warhorse.
Saddle with stirrups. Lance.
Sword, or horseman’s axe, or hammer.
Kite shield — used in the saddle and after a dismount.
Mail on torso and head/neck.
Nasal helm or spangenhelm with side neck flaps.
Compound bow on foot or mounted, javelins, better thrown weapons.
May strip to a lighter load for far-ranging rides.
This class is why Isernvolk will later lengthen poles. In the first war they still meet it with round shield and ordinary spear.
Bows here matter. They are not so common that every soldier is a horse-archer.

Elite — warrior caste
The soldier’s grammar, more iron and more horse.
Scale and laminar layered on, skirts or tassets, full mail chausses.
Bigger warhorses and fastest coursers as choices, not one beast for all work.
Longer lances, heavier-poundage bows — either or, not both in the same pass.
They may swap if they regain their own side and have time.
Mail ventail under a crested nasal helm. Colour and crest stay the look.
Horses in fabric often; horse-mail rare.
Full stirrups, lance, and mounted archery live here. This caste is small and expensive.
A man who couches a heavy lance is not also drawing a heavy bow in the same charge.

--------------------------------------------------
KANNAVI — HOW THEY DRAW (FIRST WAR)
--------------------------------------------------
Hallvard Ask’s contingent only. Paid combatants. Not a draft. Not a nation marching.
Those who come east sit mid-to-upper in Kannavi kit and training. The hamlets stay home.

Feet and legs
High-cuffed boots and Windiglas are the norm — often the envy of other foot.
Waxed Deepwood last, cuff above the mud-line, made for wet banks and not for parade.
Light padded chausses or simple hosen.

Body
Long-sleeved aketon, hem to knee or mid-thigh, most common.
Some take a thicker gambeson of the same length.
Many layer a mail vest over.
Mail jack-chains sewn into the sleeves when they can get the strips.
Something goes over the mail: a dull waffenrock of textile or hide.
Green ribbons are the field-mark. Worn when identification matters. Stowed when it does not.

Head
Felt or fur hats. Arming cap or coif under a skull cap or nasal.
Kettle brims are refused — they obstruct the draw.
Draped hoods, pulled up when weather warrants.

Arms
Signature: powerful longbow. Bodkin is the war-arrow. A few broadheads ride in the same quiver for horse and hide.
Six-foot stave — walking stick on the march, club or spear in a pinch.
Typical sidearm: one-handed bearded axe, or a single-edged cutter (falchion, hunting sword).
Most men carry bow, stave, and one sidearm.
Two-handed axes and mauls are camp and work tools. They come out when the bank-fight turns to timber or a barge-crew.

They are in their element on wooded river-banks. They are not spent as levy in the open green.

--------------------------------------------------
ALLIES
--------------------------------------------------
Kannavi — Isern side, ancient landing-alliance
Not a nation marching. Companies of longbows, stave-men who remember Unveiling woods and do not love open occupation.
They honour Osric’s compact. They resent how Isernvolk grew and consumed.
They go east as allies, not as subjects. They remind careless Adern captains that Kannavald has borders, even here.
Most like: longbows on the flank of a standing line, or on a new white wall.

The Eyrenhost — Isern side, vale-elite
Few. Bonded. Older than knighthood.
They make selected fields and selected sieges possible that Isern foot alone could not finish.
They do not replace the missing Isern cavalry mass.

Plateau Syllvani — Cortani side, stingy cousins
Official aid is thin: a sealed blessing, a refusal dressed as counsel, or help that arrives after a hall is already white.
Unofficial woodland bands are another matter. They join Rolund of Berthall during the siege and take pay from raided river transports.
Kannavi are not these woodsmen. Kannavi remain Isern allies.

--------------------------------------------------
FIRST WAR — HARTHOLD OVER THE ADER
--------------------------------------------------
Isernvolk look south and see rich farmland, lightly peopled.
The land is already claimed. Unlike the Kannavi at the landing, the Cortani are sovereign here. They will not relinquish it.

Harthold assembles a host. No Eyrenhost. Kannavi mercenaries present — longbow companies paid in silver and in the old compact.
They march south over the Ader.

The Cortani are not surprised.
There is no unified ruler. Five halls compete: Aderhall, Vayrac, Berthall, Chevalac, Elaynac.
Token resistance at the threshold. They do not try to stop the host on the border road. Aderhall was never meant to be the war.

Aderhall is breached quickly and occupied.
Aymon of Aderhall dies in the defence.
His son, Evran of the Ader, cannot fight Harthold’s column in the open. He petitions the neighbouring lords and does not trust the answer.
He gathers the surviving men and goes north — onto Isern supply lines and holdings.
Rolund’s riders go with him. The lord of Berthall keeps the hearth; the riders work the roads.
Audovald stays slow. Sellis keeps Elaynac’s counsel.
Loher of Chevalac, neighbour to Aderhall, waits. He commits only after the raids prove they can bleed a host.

Harthold orders every bridge on the Ader thrown down except the crossing north of Aderhall.
There he raises a fortified crossing — later the site of a major castle. Working name: Adergate.
Along the Ader he plants fortified waystations.

He keeps to the water. Momentum stays with him.
From the crossing-reach the Ader carries the host 165 miles before it narrows toward Berthall.
Berthall is picked because it is hilly wooded country, not open riding-green: foot, barge, and bows against a raised hillfort.

The siege of Berthall is long.
Evran and Rolund’s riders sting the siege lines and the Ader logi-chain without pause.
River vessels sink. Waystations are razed. The river is blocked.
Syllvani woodsmen join Rolund of Berthall against the besiegers. They take payment in spoils from raided river transports.
This is not official Plateau aid. The Plateau remains stingy. These are woodland bands claiming cousin-right and taking their pay from Isern barges.
The Kannavi are instrumental in this phase of defending the Ader route.
They are in their element on the wooded banks: protecting waystations and barges, shooting from cover, and fighting the small war the river demands.

Berthall is a raised hillfort ringed in stone and timber bulwarks.
Isern siegecraft burns the timber battlements. The stone must be taken with blood. In time it is.
Rolund of Berthall and Evran expected this.
Before the siege closed, Rolund had already sent the populace far and wide. Herd-people move faster than a purely agrarian town.
After he had bled the Isernvolk as far as the hold would allow, he and the remaining defenders break out and leave Harthold the bloody prize.

Berthall is empty of spoils. Rolund’s people took what a mobile hall can take.
When the siege ends and river traffic slacks, the Syllvani raids all but stop. Those who were committed take their spoils back to the Plateau.

Harthold charges the Kannavi with garrisoning the captured stretch. Hallvard Ask of Westwood — second son of the Westwood ruling house — holds that charge.
They see the prize as poor recompense. Quietly they commit to the bare minimum that still honours the compact.
They do not march on Vayrac. Their compact is the Ader, not the open heartland.
When they learn Harthold’s fate they relinquish the holdings without a fight and carry the river-lessons north.
Harthold’s own vassals and host are in poor morale. He is pressed either to cut losses and leave, or to commit harder and win them the spoils they came south to take.

Evran and Rolund are hard-worn. The long guerrilla has spent them. Their people are scattered but intact.
They resent three hosts on their ground: Isernvolk on the river, Kannavi garrisoning the captured stretch, and Loher of Chevalac roaming as an opportunist. Technically three. The Syllvani bands have already taken their spoils home.

Loher of Chevalac starts committing as Berthall falls. His warbands pick their battles opportunistically. They are not working closely with Evran and Rolund.

The first Spikoar appears on the Ader.
During barge-defence against mounted riding parties, Kannavi and river bargemen drive metal spikes into their oars. The thing works. It catches on quickly along the river. The form is refined and keeps the harsh name: Spikoar.

--------------------------------------------------
HARTHOLD’S FOLLY
--------------------------------------------------
The victory at Berthall is Pyrrhic. Empty stone. No cattle, no gold. The hinge that turns a competent river war into a named disaster.

Kannavi sit the captured stretch and the wooded banks. They will not walk inland for an ash-ring. Their compact is the Ader.
Harthold’s vassals funded iron and tenants on a promise of southern plunder. Berthall yielded none. Morale craters.
Ultimatum: leave the water and take the wealth of Vayrac, or the host disbands.
He abandons the secure Ader line to keep his political base. That is the folly.
He does not sail the Virelle / Audava. That highway-water is Vayrac’s. This campaign lives on the Ader until he steps off it.

The moment the heavy columns leave Berthall’s wooded hills for the open riding-greens, they lose their anchors.
Audovald of Vayrac has been waiting for exactly this. He commits the largest fresh cavalry reserve of the capital-seat.
Evran’s veteran road-riders close the rear and cut Harthold off from the river berths.
Loher picks at the edges. He is not in their plan.

On the open plain the Isern levy still carry spears not yet lengthened against true heavy lance. This is the first time they meet full Cortani heavy horse in the open. Spikoars remain barge and river tools here, not yet standing-line doctrine. Doctrine is what they write after Folly.
The levy is flanked, broken, and run down.

Harthold rallies fragments of host and baggage onto higher ground. Seasoned soldiers and men-at-arms hold against repeated charges.
Audovald, sensing the hour, offers ransom terms. Some in the host argue to take them.
In the lulls Evran rides the Isern line: yield; then threat; then promise of trophies from their bodies.
Harthold answers that he will fight the Cortani lordlings one after another himself, if his men walk and the ransom is guaranteed.
While he speaks he sees his own vassals going down to accept terms of their own volition.
He reads the wall. He charges with closest retainers and men-at-arms.

Evran and Rolund take that last stand.
Audovald sweeps the bounty of captured Isern vassals.
Harthold’s remaining host is annihilated. They do not surrender.

Lost ground is recaptured in weeks. Kannavi river-garrisons relinquish the holdings without a fight when they learn Harthold’s fate.
The field is remembered in modern Coritania as Harthold’s Folly.

Taught Isern lesson: the Ader is a road; a riding-green is not. Do not leave the water to buy a vassal’s purse.
Levy spears are lengthened after Folly. Spikoar becomes standing-line doctrine after Folly, not during it.

End Act 1.

--------------------------------------------------
HARTHOLD’S BARROW
--------------------------------------------------
After the last stand Evran and Rolund’s remaining host bury the dead on the hill.
A small army lies there. The ground is blood-soaked. The place does not go quiet.

Cortani burial custom
The ordinary dead go into communal underground catacombs.
Persons of note take individual barrows.
On this hill both are used. Catacombs for the host. A barrow for the king and his inner circle, minus the valuables already stripped.

The complex grows on the battle-hill and persists into the contemporary age.
Modern name: Harthold’s Barrow.
It is a no-go zone. Haunted. Witnesses say wraiths fight the doomed last stand every night.
