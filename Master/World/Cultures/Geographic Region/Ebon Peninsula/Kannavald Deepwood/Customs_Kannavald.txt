# Customs_Kannavald

Region
Kannavald Deepwood
People of the lake ring, the burns, and the deep fir. Speech and habit sit between Norse and German mark-speech; the wood and the water matter more than the pedigree.

--------------------------------------------------
FOOD AND STOCK
--------------------------------------------------
Calories come from fish, hardy garden plots, forage, small stock, and grain brought in from milder country. This is not a wheat basin.

Common household: vegetable patch, chickens, a pig or two, seasonal nuts, berries, mushrooms. Peat for the hearth where edict allows, so timber stays for trade and building.

Cattle are uncommon. They appear mainly about Kolby-on-Vatn, Hornby, and the Twaford shore. Elsewhere sheep, goats, and swine.

Swine under the canopy are a skilled trade. Drivers work sounders through the undergrowth, keep them out of reserved stands, and bring them home. Open-pasture shepherding of the soft lowlands is a foreign picture here.

--------------------------------------------------
FOREST LAW
--------------------------------------------------
The wood is held. Unsanctioned felling and poaching are crimes against the holders of the region, not private sports.

Forest Wardens are the rulers’ men-at-arms in the trees. They stop illegal cuts, stop poaching, watch for natural and unnatural trouble, and hold the edges against neighbours. They are policy with a spear.

On the northern border, where a denser neighbour presses the tree-line, the same authority may tolerate hard men who take from northern traffic. That tolerance is rim-work and deniable. If it grows loud, reprisal follows.

--------------------------------------------------
THE WOODS-BELLS
--------------------------------------------------
Bells in Kolby and the lake villages are not only for fire or festival. A known pattern means the Deepwood is wrong — Still Stones stirred, a mist that does not fit, a night that fails the usual tests.

If you hear the bells in the trees, leave the wood. Go home. Do not finish the cut. Do not check the trap.
Barges and shore-boats relay the ring around Kolvatn so the upper burns and the fen-edge hear it.
After the signal, doors are barred and children counted. No one stays out who can help it.

Who may start the ring is a matter of local law; once it travels the lake, the whole ring is under the same rule. A false ring is a serious offence.

--------------------------------------------------
THE STILL STONES
--------------------------------------------------
Also called the Black Circle, the Hollows, the Quiet Watchers. Ancient stones in a deep clearing. Local law is absolute: do not approach.
Misfortune in the region is often blamed on someone who did. The woods-bells are what you do when the wood seems to answer. You do not go to the clearing to see.

--------------------------------------------------
ROADS AND COMPANY
--------------------------------------------------
Never travel the wood or the lake-road alone.

Skinwights do not keep company. Natural beasts will not walk easy beside an abomination. A person with a dog, a pony, a herd, or a noisy cart is provisionally human. A smiling figure alone under the canopy is the worst thing a Deepwood mind expects to meet.

Locals move in twos or with stock. On meeting a lone stranger they go quiet and ready iron. A foreigner walking solo is not greeted; they are tested. To a Kannavald man, a smiling lone stranger on the road — or worse, under the firs — is terror before it is a person.

Proverb: Only a wight walks smiling and alone.

--------------------------------------------------
THE FEN
--------------------------------------------------
Kolmire is work and warning. Bog-iron and peat are taken under rule and guard; the Sunkern Burg is not a place of ordinary errands. The old pillar-road south of the Burnford–Kolby trail is known and avoided by the sensible.

Fenwights on the reed margins are ordinary fear. Drivers on the Rookburn keep timber out of the swamp and watch the west bank for more than snags.

--------------------------------------------------
LAKE MANNERS
--------------------------------------------------
Toll and turn at bridges and water-gates are expected. Kolby sets much of the working pace; the Twins hold the Sund. Upper pits and kilns live on the lake road — ore and charcoal down, grain and tools up.

Hospitality is real but narrow: company, a roof, and clear purpose. Open-handed wandering without goods or kin reads as threat or folly.

--------------------------------------------------
NOTES FOR PLAY
--------------------------------------------------
These customs are default for anyone raised on Kolvatn’s ring. Foreigners are not told twice.
Wardens, bells, and the lone-road rule will shape almost every journey through the Deepwood.
