--------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THIS IS
--------------------------------------------------

The Rock Collectors take the plate off a range-holding
Wracstan. Spoken: rock troll, stone troll, Wracstan giant.
Early Isern on the Ebon Peninsula. "The vengeful boulder."

A hunt is attrition on ground the crew has already built.
The animal is not opposed in a pull, and it is not killed
in the opening. It is made to turn, at a cost, until the
turns fail. Then it is locked, blinded, and opened at
the throat.

A chance meeting on a shelf is not this file.
The cave is not entered.

--------------------------------------------------
1. QUARRY
--------------------------------------------------

A range-holding adult. About eleven feet and 1,800 kg.
Calcified plate on the back and shoulders. The belly
is the gap. The plate is the pay.

Found by crushed pine, wet granite, unnatural shale,
torn trees, and rank scat. Hunted on False Moon nights,
when it leaves the cave to beat the ridges.

A young one under 1,500 kg is not this hunt. That is
a taking, priced on Economy_Unveiling_Beasts.txt.
This file is the adult.

--------------------------------------------------
2. CREW
--------------------------------------------------

Twenty. Thirty if the hide will pay for losses.
Ten is the foolhardy minimum.

Offices are stations, not ranks. They swap.

  Six maypole runners
    Fittest and most experienced at the start.
    Three in circuit. Three in relief.
  Eight pikemen
    In pairs. The lane is never without the scoop.
  Four ridge-men
    Reserve. Bodkin arrows into soft tissue when
    the shot is there. They feed gear when a line
    bursts.
  Two piton-tenders
    The pins, the stockpile, the next hook.
  One leader
    Calls only the phase changes: nets, first lock,
    throat. After the roar, nothing else can be
    ordered.

The best crews keep a common interval without speech.

--------------------------------------------------
3. GEAR
--------------------------------------------------

  Barbed iron javelins
    About fifteen yards of heavy-core hemp.
    Each line ends in a four-pronged hook.
  Eyelet-pitons
    Driven weeks ahead. Not in the opening.
  Ultra-long awl-pikes
    A slick, open-curved wing under the tip.
    The wing lofts a rope and sheds it.
  Bodkin arrows
    Soft tissue only. Not the plate.
  Small hooked throwing nets
    Torso coils only. They clamp rope.
    Not across a runner. Not used to blind.
  Blinding net
    Separate. Last.
  Throat spikes
    After the net. Bypass the back-hide.

Hemp is carried because a man can sprint with it.
It is never asked to stop the first lunge.
A proper crew keeps a stockpile. Snapped ropes
and pulled pins are expected.

--------------------------------------------------
4. GROUND
--------------------------------------------------

Open, flat, with room to run. Built in silence,
weeks ahead. Lanes cleared by day while the quarry
sleeps in the stone. Pitons driven into sound
fissures, braced with pine roots, covered with
moss and dirt against the smell of fresh iron.
The whole crew does this labour. They return
for the False Moon.

A pin in bad rock is a failed pass. A pin that
pulls after the matrix holds is a lost line.

--------------------------------------------------
5. OPENING
--------------------------------------------------

Three javelins into the shoulders. The barbs are
meant to toggle under the plate, not to kill.
The animal answers with a roar, a lunge, and a
tree torn out as a club.
After the roar, the interval is the order.

--------------------------------------------------
6. THE MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------

Runners do not tie off. They pay the line just
slack of the rock, sprint opposing circuits, and
throw the tail back into the coils so a later
wrap can catch it. Holding the tail makes the
man the anchor.

A pikeman who finds a trailing line catches it
in the crescent wing, lofting the rope over the
head or a limb. The wing is an open curve. A
sudden tension sheds the rope. The pike stays
free. The wrists stay whole.

A line still in a runner's hand is his until he
throws it. A line on the stone belongs to the
nearest pike.

Crossing wraps matter. One direction can unwind.
Opposed coils, once keyed, tighten if the animal
reverses.

While the matrix is on the body it is drag, not
restraint. Every spin slides hemp over rough plate.
Small hooked nets, cast from the ridge onto the
torso coils only, bite the rope so a reversal
cannot shed the wrap.

--------------------------------------------------
7. THE TURN
--------------------------------------------------

When the animal faces a man, that man retreats
on the cleared lane. When it shows its back, the
others poke the plate with a shallow awl. It rings
the hide and comes away. The sting forces a new
turn, and the turn lays another coil. He leaves
as they strike, not after. The plate is the
commodity. The goad is not left in it.

A charge that ignores the sting and comes through
the semicircle is not answered with a stand.
Run. The pikemen clear the lane. The runners match
the pace and keep slack so the lines do not snap
on the granite. The circle shifts across the ridge
by sight, until the grade or the drag forces a turn.

--------------------------------------------------
8. LOSSES AND THE RELAY
--------------------------------------------------

A burst line or a pulled pin is replaced. Ridge-men
and rotations feed fresh gear into the matrix.
Rest is denied by the relay. Fatigue is the aim.
Peak force in one blow fades more slowly than the
rate of blows. A tired animal is still lethal.
Runners swap when the legs go. A pikeman who takes
a circuit gives up his pike. The lane is never
without the scoop.

--------------------------------------------------
9. THE LOCK
--------------------------------------------------

Only after several ropes have tangled, and only
after the gap between charges has opened, is the
first tail clicked into a piton. One lock, then a
pause while the cocoon cinches, then the next.
A runner who cannot reach stone throws the hook
back into the coils, binding elbow to ribs or
ankle to thigh.

--------------------------------------------------
10. THE THROAT
--------------------------------------------------

When the arms are pinned, the legs buckle, and
the breath is ragged, the blinding net goes over
the head. The pikemen bypass the back-hide and
drive the throat spikes. Damage after that point
is a fault. The plate is why the crew was paid.

--------------------------------------------------
11. LABOUR BAND
--------------------------------------------------

Skilled Hazardous Labour. Economy_Labour.txt.
35–65 bc a day. Not a specialist retainer.
The elite huntsman rate (90–140 bc) is a
different office. This crew does not draw it.
The leader stays in the band, at the top.
Stations are not ranks. They are paid the
same day. The fittest start on the rope.
They are not paid a second wage for it.

  Floor     35 bc
  Common    50 bc
  Top       65 bc
  1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc.

13. WHAT THE PLATE PAYS
--------------------------------------------------

Estimate. Not on Economy_Unveiling_Beasts.txt.
A live river troll, broken, is 8–25 gc.
That is the beast. This guild does not sell
the beast. It sells the plate.

The guild owns the pikes, the pitons, and
the ground. A repeat hunt does not buy them
again. Spent on the night: labour, food,
javelin-lines, hemp, and the nets.
Common marginal cost is about 123 gc.
The first hunt, kit included, is 147 gc 10 sc.

Plate
  Dressed back and shoulder plate.
  1 gc for each 10 kg of live mass.
  Floor the kilograms to the nearest 10.

  1,200 kg    120 gc    light adult, Size 7
  1,800 kg    180 gc    the sheet adult
  2,050 kg    205 gc    top of the band

  120 gc does not clear a first kit.
  The guild takes a light adult only when
  the pikes are already owned and the crew
  is at the floor wage. The 1,800 kg plate
  clears the Common marginal hunt and leaves
  about 57 gc before losses.

Other trophies, Common, off the same adult
  Tusks, the ivory          12 gc
    A pair. Not a hold of elephant teeth.
  Claws                     2 gc
  Belly hide                6 gc
    Lesser. Ties and small work.
    Not the plate.
  Teeth, loose              1 gc
  Other, Common             21 gc

A clean 1,800 kg kill, plate and the rest,
is about 201 gc at the stall. The guild's
cut is not written. Damage after the throat
spikes is a fault, and it comes off the plate,
not off the tusks.

--------------------------------------------------
14. THE MONOPOLY
--------------------------------------------------

The guild does not sell the plate on an open
stall. Raw plate is the commodity they hold.
A buyer who is not on their list does not
get a slab.

They work with a short list of trusted
artisans. Armourers, mostly. Cuir bouilli
that takes the rock-troll hide upgrade
comes off that list. The artisan does not
buy the plate outright. The guild keeps a
share of the finished piece.

  Share, Common
    Guild           one third of the finished
                    ticket, after the artisan's
                    labour and the other inputs.
    Artisan         the rest.
  A piece that fails in the shop pays no
  share. The plate comes back if it can
  be saved. It does not go to a second shop.

Tusks, claws, and belly hide may leave
the guild. The plate does not. A tusk sold
in a market is not a breach. A slab of
back-plate in a stranger's yard is.

They protect it fiercely. A hunter who is
not of the crew, a carter moving plate
without their mark, an armourer off the
list: the guild treats that as theft of
the commodity. They do not post a price
for the offence. Crews that can take a
troll can take a wagon. Lords who want
the plate deal with the guild, or they
do not get a sound hide.


--------------------------------------------------
14. THE FINISHED PIECE
--------------------------------------------------

The guild does not sell the kill. It finishes it.
Raw plate, raw tusk, and raw belly hide do not
leave the house. A hunter who parts with them
is no longer of the guild.

What they make
  Back and shoulder plate, dressed and closed,
  for cuir bouilli and for ties. The rock-troll
  hide upgrade in Armour.txt is this shop.
  Tusk ivory, cut. Not a second ivory guild.
  Belly hide, for straps and small work.
  Claws and loose teeth, as fittings.
  They do not make the harness. An armourer
  buys the closed plate or the ties. He does
  not buy the kill, and he does not tan it.

The monopoly
  One house finishes Wracstan. Regional names
  for the beast do not make a second shop.
  A crew that is not theirs does not sell plate
  in a guild town. A tanner who tries the hide
  loses the hide, and then the yard.
  They are fierce about it. The hunt is the
  cost. The closed plate is the reason the
  cost is paid. Another shop on that plate
  is a theft of the hunt.

Price
  The section 13 figures are the raw stall,
  and the raw stall is not offered.
  Closed plate from an 1,800 kg adult: 240 gc.
  That is the raw 180 gc plus the house's finish.
  Ivory fittings from the pair: 20 gc.
  Belly straps, a set: 10 gc.
  A fault on the plate still comes off the
  closed price. It does not become a raw sale.
