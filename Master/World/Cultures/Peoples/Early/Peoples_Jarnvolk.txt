# Peoples_Jarnvolk

Umbrella / parent
Isernvolk offshoot. Early category. Polar stock. Not peninsula Isern. Not Adernvolk.

Formal name
Jarnvolk (locked).

Common names
Jarns / Jarnfolk (Isern and southern speech).
The lost wing (old Isern mourning-name; they do not know it still lives).

What this file is
The first Isern offshoot. Jarnvard’s rear wing of the trans-oceanic crossing, spent onto Dauðavík and forced into a polar life the Ebon line never had to learn.
Same root stock as the Isernvolk. Speech leans harder into the Scandi base; Anglo-Germanic is the quieter cousin, not the house-voice.
They make a better ship, not a super one.

--------------------------------------------------
GM META — ORIGIN
--------------------------------------------------
Same Second Wave Ingenari stock as the Isernvolk.
The split is not blood-law. It is a storm, a current, and a brother who put ashore.
Osric held the Ebon Latitude Line and landed the peninsula.
Jarnvard could not recover the heading. About 2,400 miles north of the intended track he took the ice-rim rather than die on broken spars.

Osric’s people counted Jarnvard’s fleet dead in the ice and mourned them.
Jarnvard trusted his brother to have taken the remaining hulls to a better shore than the bight.
That trust sours. Generation by generation it becomes resentment of the soft southerners on the mainland — men who kept the temperate line and, in Jarn telling, left them to the ice.
First contact, when it comes, is not a reunion. It is a ghost that learned to speak.

--------------------------------------------------
DEEP NAME VS SURFACE NAME
--------------------------------------------------
Deep / mythic: Jarnvolk.
Surface: Jarns, Jarnfolk, men of Dauðavík.
Speech: Scandi-heavy. Names, holds, and ship-words sit on the Norse side of the old Isern mix. Anglo-Germanic remains intelligible at a stretch, not the tongue of the bight.
Land-name: Dauðavík — the Dead-Bight / the Killing Gulf.
From the sea: a graveyard in the northern mist. Ice will grind you.
From the shore: dead volcanic rock walls that kill the gale, and killing-hot water under the fields. Where the ice dies.

--------------------------------------------------
GEOGRAPHY AND CULTURE
--------------------------------------------------
Seat: Dauðavík. Polar / near-polar island. Working size: about 1,400 miles by 600, irregular, roughly 420,000 square miles of tundra, basalt cliff, fault-wood, and hydrothermal ground.
Climate: long night, low sun, hard wind. Not a world without day. Winters are the test.

Culture of shortage.
Population is capped by what the warmed soil will grow — kail, turnip, what a trench-field will hold.
Wonder-timber exists and is hard to take, hard to work, slow to yield a hull.
Most textiles and many minerals are scarce. The answer is not a finished store at home. The answer is a ship south: raid or trade.

They dig in or sit behind stacked basalt. Early linings are oil-cured gut and bladder from whale and seal. Later windows are mica (Muscovy glass) where the rock gives it. Blown glass is late, if at all.

--------------------------------------------------
BOTTLENECKS (LOCKED)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Agriculture. Geothermal trenches keep soil soft. They do not make a fat country. Heads of household are few.
2. The wood. Svart-Viðr is what grows. Ordinary timber will not. On Dauðavík the rare good is southern oak and pine; in the south the rare good is Svartwood. Hard to harvest, hard to shape, limited stands. Every Jarn hull is Svartwood because there is nothing else to plank a ship with. That hull is heavy and cumbersome. A better ship. Not a super one. A keel is still a generation-argument.
3. Cloth, many ores, ordinary comforts. Sourced abroad. This is why they appear on southern lanes at all.

Every Jarn hull is Svart-Viðr. Flagship and snekkjur alike. There is no mixed oak-and-black fleet on the bight.
The cost is weight. The ships are heavy. They do not sail like a pine cog.
That is why two Osricford cogs are a prize: ordinary timber, deep holds, the wood Dauðavík cannot grow.
They still need men, food, and a reason to leave the bight. They do not own the ocean.

--------------------------------------------------
THE FAULT-WOOD
--------------------------------------------------
Locked name: Svart-Viðr.
Working name: Svartwood.
Flora file: Flora_Polar.txt. Dauðavík only.
Pre-collapse engineered conifer on the volcanic arc. Inhabitants do not know it as a machine. They know a dark-rib forest that follows the hot ground, stands that are warmer inside than the blizzard, timber that works like stubborn iron-wood.

Jobs the grove is allowed to do:
- Trace the faults. Warm, humid stands in a hard climate.
- Take up foulness from the vents in bark and sap.
- Yield heavy, unfriendly timber that makes a better ship if you can get a plank off it.

It does not make them fireproof, reef-proof, or a law of the sea.
Tap-root warmth under a house is a local trick where a stump sits on live ground. Not central heating for the island.

--------------------------------------------------
SHARED CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------
- They are Isern blood that the ice did not finish
- Jarnvard chose the shore and the living over the true line
- A hull is wealth
- What the island will not grow, the sea must bring
- Osric sailed away. That story is a wound, not a footnote

--------------------------------------------------
RELATION TO OTHER STOCKS
--------------------------------------------------
Isernvolk of the Ebon Peninsula: lost brothers.
Isern song: the rear wing died in the ice. They mourn.
Jarn song: Osric found the better shore. First it is trust. Later it is spite for soft southerners.
Until the second war they do not know they are aimed at living men.
Second-war contact: pathfinders and west-board probes, then Sindri at the Vayrac berths. Still not a reunion. A ghost with a pay-chest.
Kannavi, Cortani, Adernvolk: not this people’s war. Southern names arrive as cargo, captives, or rumour.
Plateau Syllvani: distant First-Blood cousins at most. No compact.

--------------------------------------------------
BRANCHES / LOCAL STOCKS
--------------------------------------------------
One people. Hold-by-hold flavour along the bight and the fault-ribbons.
House files later if a lasting seat is locked.

--------------------------------------------------
PERSONS OF NOTE
--------------------------------------------------
- Jarnvard — younger brother of High King Osric. Commands the rear wing. Puts ashore at Dauðavík. Founder in Jarn memory.
- Osric — kept the easterly line. Full entry: Peoples_Isernvolk.txt.
- Sindri Vindvari — Windcaller. Second-war warlord. Sails past the intended peninsula diversion and burns the Vayrac berths. Takes two Osricford cogs. The Three Hundred are his chronicle-count. This is first loud contact with the living Isern south.
