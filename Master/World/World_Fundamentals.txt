# World_Fundamentals.txt
# Basic planetary and civilisational overview of Virath

WORLD NAME
Virath

PRIMARY MOON
Lorn (the true moon)

SECONDARY CELESTIAL BODY
The False Moon (appears irregularly; see The_Unveiling.txt)

SIZE
Pole-to-pole distance (surface meridian): 15,360 miles.
Equator circumference: 27,300 miles.
Larger than Earth, still a human-habitable terrestrial world.
Pole-to-pole here is the surface run from pole to pole, not the through-axis diameter.

AXIAL TILT
24.5 degrees.
Engineered in the deep past by Ingenari machines in collaboration with the Pionari (see THE DEEPER PAST). Inhabitants do not know this.
Seasons are a little sharper than Earth's 23.4° tilt. Still four seasons: Thaw, Highsun, Harvest, Longnight.

PLANETARY CHARACTER
Virath is a terrestrial world broadly comparable to Earth in gravity, atmosphere, and temperature range. Humans can live and thrive across most of its surface without extraordinary aid. Seasons, weather, and day length function in familiar ways.

Despite this familiarity, Virath contains far more extreme topography and climate zones than a typical settled world:
- Vast, near-endless tundras and polar wastes
- Immense deserts of sand, stone, and salt
- Sheer, near-impassable mountain ranges
- Dense, lightless jungles and old-growth forests that resist mapping and settlement
- Deep, cold oceans with abyssal trenches

These extremes shape culture, trade routes, warfare, and the practical limits of empire.

GEOLOGY
Virath is a cooling crust. In the deep past it was violently active: rifting, stacking, magma, and a sky that still threw stone. That age carved the cave-nets (old lava tubes, emptied chambers, karst) and raised the extreme fault-walls. When the Pionari first worked the world, most continents had already settled. Isolated arcs remain live. Dauðavík is one. The Ebon Spire is not. The Bowls leak; they do not erupt.
The Ebon Spire is the tallest peak on Virath: an inactive black shield volcano, Olympus-class in bulk. Footprint is huge. Fire is dead. See Geographic_Region_Ebon_Peninsula.txt.

Human chronicles do not reach this. Recorded history is a few thousand years. Mountain-time is not hall-time.

Age of landforms (north-west, oldest first)
- The Appulse. A giant impact. The Appolasian Plateau is its basin. The ring-hills and crags are the oldest standing topography in this quarter of the world. A structure this large can steer mountains, rivers, and climate for hundreds of millions of years. It stops looking like a fresh wound long before it stops existing. The Plateau is worn, stubborn, and first.
- The volatile tectonic age. Fault-scarps, the Rivenstrand Walls, the Marchwall, the Orosian Spine and its clusters. Younger than the Appulse. Sharper. The “upstart” ranges. Most of this fire is dead. The walls remain.
- Quiet continents. Caves are fossil plumbing. Heat is local.
- Late small falls. The Bowls on the Rhennod foot. Young pits on an old lip. Cousin of the Appulse or a later stone — not locked.

A giant impact does not need to stay sharp to stay in charge. Vredefort-scale wounds on a wet world still own the map after the knife-ridges of a later orogeny have grown up beside them. The Appolasian crags are old bone. The Spine and the Marchwall are the new argument.

DOMINANT SPECIES
Humanity is the dominant and most widespread intelligent species on Virath.
Other fully sapient races exist, though they are generally fewer in number, more geographically limited, or less politically central than humans.
Their exact natures, origins, and relations with humanity vary widely by region and culture.
Mutants, beastmen, and other abominations also appear, but these are regarded as corruptions or curses rather than natural peoples.

TECHNOLOGY
The highest consistently achievable technology across Virath is roughly equivalent to Earth’s 16th century. This level is not uniform:
- Some wealthy coastal and riverine states maintain advanced metallurgy, plate armour, complex fortifications, early firearms (rare and expensive), and sophisticated shipbuilding.
- Many inland, highland, or frontier regions remain closer to high medieval or even earlier material culture.
- Gunpowder exists but is uncommon, unreliable, and often treated with suspicion.
- No functioning relics of higher technology are understood or operable by present-day peoples.

HISTORY (KNOWN)
Recorded history stretches back only a few thousand years at most, and even the oldest chronicles are fragmentary. All cultures share a distant memory of a great fracture known as the Unveiling (see The_Unveiling.txt). Before that event, knowledge dissolves into myth.

THE DEEPER PAST (UNKNOWN TO INHABITANTS)
This sequence is GM-only. Living peoples have myth, not this order.

Pionari first. An explorator vessel found Virath already fit for habitation — promising, almost ready, only light work needed. They lacked the mass and the kit to finish a world. They sent home for it.

Those who answered were the Ingenari: heavy engineers and the military sent to secure the claim. Years in transit.

While they waited, the Pionari put down on the Appolasian Plateau and began seeding Terran life.

The Ingenari arrived. With the Pionari they turned the axial tilt to widen the temperate belts. With their machines they raised the first true cities and the first infrastructure. Lesser Terran administrators came with them: the Ordinari.

In that first century the Unveiling was already present. The human count was still a thin specialist contingent. Manifestations were subtle. Men found rational names for them.

Then the Multitude came — Novari in the later books — millions fleeing calamity on the homeworld. That weight of souls fed the Unveiling. What can fairly be called demons first took form.

By then the entrenched first-comers — Pionari, Ingenari, and the first Ordinari — had laboured near a century on a home they would not hand to Terran overreach and a horde of interlopers. They were ready to hold it.

The Aperti watched with cold detachment.
In that same span they mapped what they took to be the mechanics of the Unveiling. They measured that the old-world religions carried by the Multitude were leaving a print on Virath — visible only to those who knew the signs.
Aperti working count of the Multitude’s inherited faith-mass (not a living census):
- The Nazarene creed (later halls will not know the word Christianity) — about 29–31%
- The Submission (later halls will not know the word Islam) — about 24–26%
- The Middle Path (later halls will not know the word Buddhism) — about 15%
- The Eternal Law (later halls will not know the word Hinduism) — about 6–7%
Other affiliations, in their reading, never reached the mass needed to steer the Immaterium.
Those four currents are a foundation under many contemporary cults and churches. The living forms are near-unrecognisable. Names, law, and rite have been broken by the Dark Age and rebuilt from scraps. Do not print Earth-names in a 16th-century hall-book.
This is Aperti observation, not a proof offered to the marks. The Aperti file, when written, holds the cold version. The_Unveiling.txt holds the phenomenon.


The Multitude and later Ordinari forced a place in the less desirable country. Low hostility remained. A cold war. Then an uneasy peace.

Then the False Moon first eclipsed the sun.

Records from that hour grow sparse, then broken, then gone. The human count falls. Fewer souls, and the Unveiling recedes. A remnant on the order of ten thousand is left, scattered across the globe.

The old “one colony ship, five to ten thousand settlers” line is retired. That number is the *aftermath*, not the landing.
Megastructures and ash-choked works remain. No present people operate them. This origin is unknown to the halls. It survives, if at all, as quarreling legend.

CULTURAL DEVELOPMENT
Enough time has passed since the collapse for distinct ethnic groups, languages, religions, and cultures to form and diverge widely. Regional identities are strong. Universal institutions are rare.

GENERAL TONE OF THE WORLD
Virath is a hard, lived-in world of steel, blood, trade, faith, and weather. The supernatural exists but remains mostly at the edges — rare, feared, and poorly understood. Most days are governed by the same concerns that have always ruled human life: harvest, winter, war, honour, debt, and survival.
