# Economy_Primary_Goods.txt
# Primary Goods – Base Unit: 1 kg (or single natural item)

HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Prices shown are base market values and are subject to ±50% (or greater) fluctuation by region, season, supply, and demand.
Base unit is 1 kg wherever practical. Larger lots are provided for convenience.

These goods form the economic foundation of Virath.

---

Agriculture
Crops planted, tended, and taken from the field or grove.
Comes from settled farmland.
Acquired by farm labour through the year.
Used as food, fibre, oil, and specialist trade.

  Grain
  The staple seed-crop of settled land: wheat, barley, rye, or local equivalents.
  Comes from ploughed fields in temperate and warmer regions.
  Acquired by farm labour through the agricultural year.
  Used as bread-corn, ale-corn, fodder, and the baseline of most diets.
  Base: 3 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (40 kg) → 1 sc 20 bc | Cart load (800 kg) → 10 sc
  Wt/Vol reference: 1 kg ≈ 1.3 L

  Olives
  Oil-fruit of warm coasts and hills.
  Grown in groves; picked by seasonal labour.
  Pressed for oil or eaten cured.
  Base: 4 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (30 kg) → 1 sc | Cart load (600 kg) → 10 sc

  Grapes
  Fresh or pressed fruit of the vine.
  Grown in warm, settled hill-country.
  Picked by seasonal labour; used for eating or wine.
  Base: 6 bc per kg
  Common lots: Crate (20 kg) → 1 sc | Cart load (500 kg) → 12 sc 10 bc

  Date Fruit (dried)
  Sweet dried fruit of hot, dry lands.
  Grown in irrigated groves; picked and sun-dried.
  Eaten as food and a high-value southern staple.
  Base: 9 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (25 kg) → 2 sc 5 bc

  Flax
  Fibre-plant of damp fields.
  Grown and retted by farm labour, then broken and scutched.
  Used for linen, twine, and oil-seed.
  Base: 7 bc per kg
  Common lots: Bundle/sack (20 kg) → 1 sc 10 bc

  Raw Silk
  Unwoven fibre of the silkworm.
  Comes from specialised warm-country rearing.
  Acquired by sericulture, not ordinary farmwork.
  Used for high cloth.
  Base: 2 sc per kg
  Common lots: Bundle (5 kg) → 10 sc

  Spice
  Dried aromatic goods of hot and distant lands.
  Grown or gathered in specialised regions, then packed for long trade.
  Used in cooking, medicine, and as a mark of wealth.
  Base: 3 sc per kg
  Common lots: Small chest (5 kg) → 15 sc

  Dyes
  Colouring stuffs of plant, insect, or mineral origin.
  Grown, gathered, or imported according to type.
  Acquired by specialist harvest or trade.
  Used by dyers and cloth-makers.
  Base: 1 sc 10 bc per kg
  Common lots: Pot (5 kg) → 7 sc 10 bc


Animal Produce
Goods taken from living beasts, or from beasts that must be killed.
Comes from herds, yards, woods, and the hunt.
Acquired by husbandry, slaughter, or trapping.

  Live / Household
  Taken without slaughter.

    Butter
    Churned fat of milk.
    Comes from cattle and similar dairy herds in pastoral country.
    Acquired by daily dairy work.
    Used for cooking, eating, and as a stored fat.
    Base: 12 bc per kg
    Common lots: Small cask (20 kg) → 1 sc | Larger cask (50 kg) → 2 sc 10 bc

    Cheese
    Pressed, aged milk-curd.
    Made in pastoral and mixed-farming country.
    Acquired by dairy labour after milking.
    Used as stored protein and trade food.
    Base: 14 bc per kg
    Common lots: Wheel/sack (15 kg) → 1 sc 15 bc | Larger lot (40 kg) → 2 sc 10 bc

    Wool
    Shorn fleece.
    Comes from sheep in pastoral country.
    Acquired by shearing.
    Used for cloth, felt, and stuffing.
    Base: 5 bc per kg
    Common lots: Fleece sack (25 kg) → 1 sc 5 bc | Cart load (400 kg) → 8 sc 10 bc

    Eggs
    Fresh eggs of domestic fowl.
    Comes from household flocks.
    Acquired by ordinary yard-labour.
    Used as daily food; rarely a long-range trade good.
    Base: 1 bc each (≈ 20–25 eggs per kg)
    Common lots: Basket (100 eggs) → 1 sc

    Honey
    Wild or kept sweet.
    Taken from woodland hives or kept skeps.
    Acquired by bee-keepers and forest gatherers.
    Used as sweetener, drink-base, and store food.
    Base: 14 bc per kg
    Common lots: Jar/cask (15 kg) → 2 sc 10 bc

    Beeswax
    The comb-wax of the same hives.
    Taken when honey is cut.
    Used for candles, sealing, and fine craft.
    Base: 16 bc per kg
    Common lots: Block (10 kg) → 1 sc 15 bc

  Slaughter / Hunt
  Requires the beast to be killed, or taken as a carcass. Includes meat, hide, fur, and fat.

    Meat (smoked/salted)
    Preserved flesh of livestock or game.
    Comes from slaughter or hunting, then salted or smoked.
    Used as travelling food and winter store.
    Base: 15 bc per kg
    Common lots: Sack (20 kg) → 1 sc 5 bc | Cart load (350 kg) → 21 sc 15 bc

    Hides (raw)
    Unworked skins of cattle and large game.
    Taken at slaughter or from the hunt.
    Acquired by flaying and a first salting or drying.
    Used by tanners for leather.
    Base: 10 bc per kg
    Common lots: Bundle (15 kg) → 1 sc 10 bc

    Fur
    The dense winter coat of forest and highland beasts.
    Taken by trappers.
    Used for warm clothing, trim, and high-value trade.
    Base: 1 sc 10 bc per kg
    Common lots: Bundle (10 kg) → 15 sc

    Tallow
    Rendered animal fat.
    Comes from slaughter and hunting.
    Acquired by boiling down carcass fat.
    Used for candles, soap, and cheap cooking fat.
    Base: 9 bc per kg
    Common lots: Cask (25 kg) → 2 sc 5 bc

    Fish (smoked/dried)
    Preserved catch from sea, lake, or river.
    Taken by net, line, or trap, then smoked or dried.
    Used as staple protein and a durable trade food.
    Base: 8 bc per kg
    Common lots: Sack (25 kg) → 2 sc | Cart load (400 kg) → 13 sc 10 bc

  Ivory
  Hard animal material used for fine work, handles, and ornament.

    Walrus Tusk
    The great ivory of cold seas.
    Taken by northern hunters from walrus.
    Used for carving, inlay, and prestige goods.
    Base: 2 sc 10 bc per kg
    Common lots: Single large tusk (8–12 kg) → 20–30 sc

    Antler / Horn
    Shed or taken antler, and horn of deer, goat, and similar beasts.
    Comes from hunting country and forest margins.
    Acquired by hunting, gathering sheds, or slaughter.
    Used for handles, combs, glue, and small tools.
    Base: 1 sc per kg
    Common lots: Bundle (10 kg) → 10 sc


Wilderness Foraging
What is taken from wood, fen, shore, and waste without the plough.
Comes from wild ground.
Acquired by gathering, seasonal picking, and shore-combing.
Used as food, packing, medicine, and small high-value finds.

  Moss / Lichen
  Soft growth of the deep forest floor, wet bark, and swamp margins. Includes peat-moss for packing and tanner’s moss for curing.
  Comes from heavy woodland and fen-edges.
  Acquired by gathering; little skill, much stooping and carrying.
  Used to pack fragile goods, stuff walls, and dress hides.
  Base: 4 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (20 kg) → 80 bc | Cart load (400 kg) → 6 sc 10 bc

  Bog Berries
  Sour small fruit of wet moss and fen.
  Comes from Kolmire-type ground and lake margins.
  Picked in season, then eaten fresh or dried.
  Used as winter store, drink-base, and a minor export.
  Base: 8 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (15 kg) → 1 sc | Basket (5 kg) → 40 bc

  Forest Berries
  Ordinary wild fruit of the wood: bramble, currant, and the like.
  Comes from woodland edges and burns.
  Picked in season by anyone who can walk.
  Used as food and cheap preserve; rarely worth a long haul.
  Base: 5 bc per kg
  Common lots: Basket (8 kg) → 40 bc | Sack (20 kg) → 1 sc

  Dried Fungi
  Edible or useful mushrooms, dried for keeping.
  Comes from the deep-wood floor after rain.
  Gathered by those who know the safe from the dead.
  Used as food, some dye, and a little medicine.
  Base: 12 bc per kg
  Common lots: Bundle (5 kg) → 60 bc | Sack (12 kg) → 6 sc

  Wild Greens / Roots
  Eaten leaves, shoots, and roots of waste and wood.
  Comes from any wild country in season.
  Gathered as household food, not as a staple trade.
  Used to stretch the pot when grain is dear.
  Base: 3 bc per kg
  Common lots: Bundle (10 kg) → 30 bc

  Medicinal Herbs
  Known useful plants, dried or bound.
  Comes from specific banks, clearings, and fen-edges.
  Gathered by those with the knowledge.
  Used by physicians, wise-women, and household medicine.
  Base: 1 sc per kg
  Common lots: Bundle (2 kg) → 2 sc | Small chest (5 kg) → 5 sc

  Amber
  Fossil tree-resin, hard and clear enough for ornament.
  Found washed up on wooded shores, or dug from old fen-ground.
  Acquired by shore-combing and careful digging; never a sure harvest.
  Used by jewellers and long-range traders.
  Base: 4 sc per kg
  Common lots: Small lot (1 kg) → 4 sc | Choice piece by quality

Forestry
Wood, and what is taken from the standing tree besides timber.
Comes from forest and woodland.
Acquired by woodcutters, charcoal-burners, and tappers.

  Timber (mixed)
  Unsorted building and firewood. Species not specified or not worth specifying.
  Felled in any workable wood.
  Used for ordinary building, fuel, and cheap joinery.
  Base: 4 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,200 kg) → 20 sc

  Pine / Fir
  Light, resinous softwood of cold and highland forest.
  Fast to fell, easy to float, prone to rot if left raw.
  Used for common building, planking, scaffolding, and as the chief source of resin.
  Base: 5 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,100 kg) → 22 sc 10 bc

  Birch
  Pale, even wood of northern and mixed forest.
  Burns hot; the bark is a good tinder and wrapping.
  Used for firewood, small joinery, turned work, and bark-goods.
  Base: 6 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,000 kg) → 25 sc

  Alder
  Wet-ground wood of river, fen, and lake-margin.
  Holds up in water better than most cheap timber.
  Used for piles, sluices, clogs, and work that stands in the damp.
  Base: 6 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,000 kg) → 25 sc

  Beech
  Hard, close-grained wood of temperate forest.
  Takes a clean cut and a good finish; splits well for fuel.
  Used for furniture, tools, flooring, and better firewood.
  Base: 9 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (950 kg) → 35 sc 10 bc

  Elm
  Tough, twisted wood that does not split easily.
  Stands wet better than oak in some uses.
  Used for hubs, keels, pipes, and any work that must take strain and damp together.
  Base: 10 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (950 kg) → 39 sc 10 bc

  Ash
  Springy, straight-grained hardwood.
  Takes shock without snapping.
  Used for tool-handles, oars, shafts, and frames.
  Base: 12 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (900 kg) → 45 sc

  Oak
  The chief heavy hardwood of lasting work.
  Slow to grow, slow to rot, hard to work.
  Used for ships, halls, gates, barrels, and any frame meant to outlast the builder.
  Base: 14 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (900 kg) → 52 sc 10 bc

  Yew
  Dense, elastic, dark wood. Scarce and slow.
  The best common bow-wood; waste is still valued.
  Used for bows, small fine work, and costly joinery.
  Base: 1 sc 10 bc per kg
  Common lots: Billet lot (20 kg) → 30 sc

  Charcoal
  Wood burned down to a hot, clean fuel.
  Made in forest kilns and pits from whatever is cheap and dry enough.
  Acquired by charcoal-burners.
  Used by smiths, smelters, and anyone needing a strong fire.
  Base: 6 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (15 kg) → 90 bc | Cart load (300 kg) → 7 sc 10 bc

  Resin / Pitch
  Sticky sap and the boiled tar made from it.
  Tapped chiefly from pine and fir, or rendered from their roots and waste.
  Acquired by woodland labour and simple boiling.
  Used to seal boats, bind torches, and weatherproof timber.
  Base: 12 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cask (20 kg) → 1 sc | Cart load (400 kg) → 20 sc


Earths and Salts
Bulk stuffs dug, cut, or evaporated from ground and water.
Comes from pits, pans, and fens.
Acquired by digging, cutting, boiling, or drying.
Used as fuel, preservative, and raw building-earth.

  Clay
  Raw earth for pots, brick, and daub.
  Dug from riverbanks, lake margins, and clay-pits.
  Acquired by digging and hauling.
  Used by potters, builders, and tilers.
  Base: 2 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,000 kg) → 8 sc 10 bc

  Salt
  The chief preservative.
  Taken from pans, mines, or coastal works.
  Acquired by boiling, cutting, or evaporating.
  Used to cure meat and fish, and as a universal kitchen good.
  Base: 18 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (20 kg) → 1 sc 10 bc | Cart load (400 kg) → 30 sc

  Peat
  Cut and dried fen-earth used as fuel.
  Taken from bogs and lake-margin mosses.
  Acquired by cutting, stacking, and drying.
  Used as a cheap local fire where wood and charcoal are too dear to burn.
  Base: 2 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (800 kg) → 6 sc 10 bc


Minerals
Raw ores and mineral stuffs taken from the ground.
Comes from veins, washes, pits, and volcanic ground.
Acquired by miners, diggers, and washers.
Used by smelters, smiths, and specialists once reduced.

  Iron Ore
  Mined rock bearing iron.
  Comes from highland veins and workings.
  Used to make bloom, bar, and finished iron.
  Base: 8 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (50 kg) → 1 sc 15 bc | Cart load (800 kg) → 26 sc 15 bc

  Bog Iron
  Low-grade iron taken from wet ground rather than hard rock.
  Forms in fens, lake margins, and old waterlogged soils.
  Acquired by digging and washing, then smelted locally.
  Base: 5 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (50 kg) → 1 sc 10 bc | Cart load (800 kg) → 16 sc 10 bc

  Copper Ore
  The common ore of bronze and common plate.
  Comes from green-stained veins in hills and old workings.
  Used for bronze, pots, fittings, and roofing once smelted.
  Base: 14 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (40 kg) → 2 sc 5 bc | Cart load (700 kg) → 40 sc 10 bc

  Tin Ore
  The scarcer half of bronze.
  Comes from specific stream-washes and hard-rock veins.
  Used with copper to make bronze; little other common use in the raw.
  Base: 1 sc 2 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (25 kg) → 27 sc 10 bc | Small lot (10 kg) → 11 sc

  Lead Ore
  Heavy, common ore.
  Comes from widespread highland veins, often beside silver.
  Used for pipes, roofs, shot, seals, and cheap casting.
  Base: 7 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (50 kg) → 1 sc 10 bc | Cart load (900 kg) → 26 sc 5 bc

  Silver Ore
  Rock bearing silver.
  Comes from uncommon highland veins, often with lead.
  Used for coin, plate, and fine work once smelted.
  Base: 1 sc 5 bc per kg
  Common lots: Small sack (10 kg) → 12 sc 10 bc

  Gold Ore
  Rock bearing gold.
  Comes from rare veins and washings.
  Used for coin and high ornament once refined.
  Base: 6 sc per kg
  Common lots: Small sack (5 kg) → 30 sc

  Coal
  Black mineral fuel.
  Comes from seams and shallow pits.
  Used where woodland charcoal is dear, and in some heavy firing.
  Base: 3 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (40 kg) → 1 sc | Cart load (800 kg) → 10 sc

  Sulphur
  Pale, stinking mineral of volcanic and rotten ground.
  Comes from vents, old flows, and rare pits.
  Acquired by scraping and careful packing.
  Used by smiths, fowlers, and those who make fire-powders and medicines.
  Base: 1 sc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (15 kg) → 15 sc

  Saltpetre
  Crystalline mineral of dry earth, stables, and old cellars.
  Comes from nitre-beds and scraped deposits.
  Used in preserving, medicine, and fire-work.
  Base: 1 sc 8 bc per kg
  Common lots: Small sack (10 kg) → 16 sc

  Alum
  Astringent mineral salt.
  Comes from specific shales and works, not from ordinary rock.
  Used by dyers and tanners to fix colour and harden hide.
  Base: 18 bc per kg
  Common lots: Sack (20 kg) → 1 sc 10 bc


Stone and Volcanic Products
Cut, broken, or gathered rock for building, grinding, and craft.
Taken from quarries, cliffs, volcanic highlands, and river-wash.
Acquired by quarrymen, gatherers, and hauliers.

  Common Stone
  Ordinary building stone: limestone, sandstone, and unmarked local rock.
  Comes from lowland and hillside quarries.
  Used for walls, cottages, and heavy fill.
  Base: 3 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,500 kg) → 18 sc 15 bc

  Granite
  Hard, coarse stone for lasting work.
  Comes from highland bosses and deep cuttings.
  Used for keeps, quays, millstones, and foundations that must not fail.
  Base: 6 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,400 kg) → 35 sc

  Slate
  Splitting stone for roofs and flags.
  Comes from specific beds, not from any cliff.
  Used for roofing, flooring, and thin facing.
  Base: 8 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,000 kg) → 33 sc 10 bc

  Marble
  Fine, workable stone that takes a polish.
  Comes from uncommon beds in hills and old massifs.
  Used for halls, tombs, and finished facing.
  Base: 1 sc 5 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (800 kg) → 50 sc

  Basalt / Pumice
  Dark volcanic rock and the light abrasive stone washed from it.
  Comes from volcanic highlands and the runoff rivers below them.
  Acquired by cutting the hard rock or gathering the river-stone.
  Used for grinding, hide-work, scouring, and dark building.
  Base: 5 bc per kg
  Common lots: Cart load (1,200 kg) → 25 sc

  Obsidian
  Volcanic glass.
  Comes from old flows and the broken flanks of volcanic peaks.
  Acquired by careful gathering and splitting, not ordinary quarrying.
  Used for keen edges, ceremonial blades, and small high-value work.
  Base: 2 sc per kg
  Common lots: Bundle (5 kg) → 10 sc
