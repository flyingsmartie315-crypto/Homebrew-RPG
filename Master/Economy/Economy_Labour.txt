# Daily Wages of Virath

IMPORTANT NOTE ON WAGES
The daily wage figures listed below do not necessarily mean the worker receives that amount in actual coin at the end of each day.
They represent the total economic value derived from the labour.

For rural commoners (especially farmhands, herdsmen, and village labourers) the majority of this value is typically received in kind — food, produce, board, grazing rights, a share of the harvest, or other household provisions — rather than as brass coins in hand.
Cash payment, when it occurs at all, is often only a small portion of the total value.

Urban workers are more likely to receive a higher proportion of their wage in actual coin, as they must purchase most of their food and necessities.

All wages are base rates and are supply-dependent.

NOTE ON LISTS
The job lists below are illustrative, not exhaustive. Real societies contain far more occupations than can be listed here. Use the definitions and wage bands to place any unlisted role.

COMMON LABOUR
Defined as physical work that requires no formal apprenticeship, only basic tools or none at all, and can be performed by most healthy adults after minimal instruction.
It is the foundation of rural and urban economies and is typically paid at the lowest rates.

Additional Note:
Workers in this class frequently move between different jobs. A rural farmhand or village labourer will often perform many different Rural Common Labour tasks over the course of a year according to the season (sowing, reaping, ditching, woodcutting, etc.).
Urban Common Labour roles tend to be more settled, with individuals remaining in the same type of work for longer periods.

SKILLED HAZARDOUS LABOUR (35–65 bc)
Defined as work that demands real practical skill, environmental knowledge, and regular acceptance of physical danger, but is not a traditional workshop craft.
These roles often involve being entrusted with valuable equipment, vessels, animals, or commodities that the worker does not personally own. Responsibility for the safe handling and return of such property is a core part of the job.

Examples:
- Rough-water / deep-sea fisherman
- Caravan guard
- Sailor / seaman (coastal or ocean)
- Forest warden / ranger
- Professional deep miner
- Long-distance teamster / caravan driver
- River pilot (difficult waters)
- Professional watchman (rough districts)
- Whaler / dangerous sea hunter
- Border patrol (non-military)
- Experienced steeplejack / high climber
- Salvage diver (where applicable)
- Camel / caravan master (responsible for animals and cargo)
- Lighthouse keeper (isolated and hazardous postings)
- Ice cutter / winter river worker
- Professional fire-fighter (city or shipboard)
- Elephant / heavy animal handler (where applicable)
- Powder magazine / explosives handler (rare)
- High-scaffolding or roof worker on major buildings
- Marsh / swamp guide (dangerous terrain)
- Tomb or ruin guard (remote and hazardous sites)

SPECIALISTS
Defined as individuals whose work requires rare expertise, significant personal risk, specialised knowledge, or a combination of high skill and danger that places them clearly above ordinary skilled trades.
They are fewer in number, often mobile or retained by wealthier clients, and command a clear premium over standard skilled craftsmen.
Many specialists are paid by contract, month, or retainership rather than a strict daily rate.

SCALING MULTIPLIERS

Proficiency:
- Apprentice: 0.25×
- Journeyman: base
- Experienced: 1.25×
- Local Renown: 2×
- Region / Kingdom reputation: 5×
- World-renowned: 10×

Client Status:
- Commoner: base
- Bourgeoisie: 2×
- Nobility: 5×

Note: Extreme multipliers (especially World-renowned × Nobility) apply only to exceptional high-prestige commissions, not ordinary daily wages.
