# Economy_Finished_Arms.txt
# Fighting pieces. Not tools.

HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Arms are sold by the piece.
The metal in the weapon is never cheaper than the grade named in the inputs.
Hafts and shafts use selected blanks, not boards.
Waste is in the bought mass, not a second ticket.

Cost by trade-rate, not by hat.
If one craftsman does two trades under one roof, book each block
at that trade's wage. Do not invent extra men. Do not pay the
higher wage for the lower trade. Parallel days shorten the calendar.
They do not cheapen the sum.

Wait is not labour.
Seasoning, loft-years, and drying sit as storage and a small premium on the stave.
They are not bowyer-days and they are not smith-days.
Same rule as tan-pits in Economy_Processing_Matrix.txt.

Stay inside the categories below. Named examples are types within a category, not separate files.
Core/Weaponry.txt Common cost is the mid of the band.

--------------------------------------------------
BOWS
--------------------------------------------------

Seasoning is not labour.
A stave sits in a loft for years. That is secure storage and a little tied coin.
It is not a bowyer standing over the stick.

Floor
  stave (already dry) + loft premium (1–8 sc) + string/nocks + tiller-days × wage.
Do not multiply bowyer-days by the years in the rack.
Yew costs more because the stick is scarce, not because someone watched it for three years.

Low-Poundage Bow
Type: Low-Poundage Bow
Examples: light bow, hunting self-bow
Inputs:
  - 1 selected bow-stave (ash, elm, or lesser yew), already dry
  - loft premium 1–3 sc
  - string
Labour Component Split (Total 2–4 man-days):
  - Tiller:             2-4 days Basic skilled bowyer          @ 30 bc
Time: loft years behind the stave; not booked as days
Modifiers:
  - Fine: better stave and tiller
Output: 1 piece
Base price: 12–25 sc
Core Common: 18 sc

Medium-Poundage Bow
Type: Medium-Poundage Bow
Examples: cavalry bow, skirmish self-bow
Inputs:
  - 1 selected bow-stave, already dry
  - loft premium 2–5 sc
  - string
  - sinew / glue / horn only if composite
Labour Component Split (Total ~5 man-days):
  - Tiller:             5 days Basic skilled bowyer            @ 30 bc
  - Composite only:     +3 days Higher skilled + sinew work    @ 65 bc
Time: loft years on the stave, not on the man
Modifiers:
  - Self-bow: the band below
  - Composite: extra materials and time; push toward 1–3 gc
Output: 1 piece
Base price: 25–50 sc (self). Composite 1–3 gc.
Core Common (self): 38 sc

High-Poundage Bow
Type: High-Poundage Bow
Examples: heavy self-bow (70–100 lb); longbow / warbow
Inputs:
  - 1 selected stave, already dry
      Yew stave ~5 kg as Forestry (about 12 sc 6 bc)
      Substitute ash / elm stave, cheaper
  - loft premium 4–8 sc
  - string, nocks
Labour Component Split (Total ~5 man-days):
  - Tiller:             5 days Higher skilled bowyer           @ 65 bc
        5 days x 65 bc = 27 sc 1 bc
Time: loft years. Yew scarcity is the stave price, not extra weeks.

Check (yew longbow, 5 tiller-days)
  Yew stave                 12 sc 6 bc
  Loft premium              4-8 sc
  Tiller                    27 sc
  String / nocks            a few sc
  Floor                     about 2 gc 5 sc to 2 gc 10 sc
Do not retail yew under that floor.

Modifiers:
  - Substitute stave (ash, elm): the 1-2 gc band
  - Yew longbow / warbow: starts at 2 gc 10 sc
  - Fine selected yew: 3-5 gc. Not a harness.
Output: 1 piece
Base price:
  Substitute heavy self     1-2 gc
  Yew longbow               2 gc 10 sc - 4 gc
  Fine selected yew         3-5 gc
Core Common:
  Heavy (substitute)        1 gc 10 sc
  Longbow (yew)             2 gc 10 sc
