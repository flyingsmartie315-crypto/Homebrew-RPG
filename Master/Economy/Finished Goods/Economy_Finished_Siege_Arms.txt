# Economy_Finished_Siege_Arms.txt
# Material, labour, and logistical cost of siege engines.
# Status: DRAFT.
# Derived from Economy_Primary_*, Economy_Processed_*,
# Economy_Labour.txt, and Economy_Rules.txt.
# Companion: Siege_Weapons.txt (rules and base cost).

HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Engines are not sold by the piece in ordinary shops.
The figures below are the material + labour value of a
finished engine in good order, ready to be emplaced.
Transport, site preparation, and local scarcity sit on top.

Cost by trade-rate, not by hat.
Book each block at that trade’s wage.
Parallel days shorten the calendar. They do not cheapen the sum.
Wait (seasoning of heavy timber, curing of rope) is storage
and a small premium, not extra labour days.

Stay inside the categories. Named engines are types.
Iron Grade on metal parts follows the same rules as
Economy_Finished_Arms.txt.
Timber is selected structural blanks or heavy scantling,
not boards or firewood.

--------------------------------------------------
RAMPART CROSSBOW (MAUERARMBRUST)
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Heavy wall-sniper / braced crossbow
Inputs:
  - 1.2–1.6 kg high-carbon oil-tempered spring steel (laminated prod)
  - 3–4 kg dense seasoned hornbeam or yew (stock)
  - 0.8–1.2 kg iron (collar, recoil hook, trigger housing, fittings)
  - Heavy composite linen cord (2,000+ strands, waxed and tallowed)
  - Revolving nut (ivory or cast bronze) + steel lever
  - Dual-crank windlass (separate, iron and timber)
  - Leather sling and brass-lined groove
Labour Component Split (Total ~6–9 man-days):
  - Prod forging and temper:  2–3 days Higher skilled smith      @ 65 bc
  - Stock shaping and finish: 1.5–2 days Higher skilled carpenter @ 40 bc
  - Lock, hook, and fittings: 1.5–2 days Higher skilled smith     @ 65 bc
  - Cord and assembly:        1–1.5 days Specialist labour        @ 35 bc
  - Windlass:                 0.5–1 day Smith / carpenter         @ 50 bc
Modifiers:
  - Poor steel or green stock: −25% cost, unreliable snap and warp risk
  - Fine laminated prod + ivory nut: +40%
Output: 1 crossbow + separate windlass
Base price: 18–35 gc
Logistical notes:
  Man-portable by one soldier via sling when uncocked.
  Assistant carries windlass and bolt quiver.
  Requires stone rampart or swivel frame to fire safely.
  Bolts are heavy all-iron armor-piercing (separate cost;
  see Finished Arms for heavy bolts). Not a field weapon.
  Common on permanent walls and tower posts.

--------------------------------------------------
MANGONEL / ONAGER
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Torsion stone-thrower
Inputs:
  - 8–12 m³ selected oak or ash scantling (frame and arm)
  - 40–60 kg iron (fittings, axle, shot-cup reinforcement)
  - 30–40 kg heavy rope or sinew (torsion bundles)
  - Stone shot or cast balls as ammunition (separate)
Labour Component Split (Total ~12–18 man-days):
  - Frame and arm:        6–8 days Higher skilled carpenter   @ 40 bc
  - Iron fittings:        3–4 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Rope work and wind:   2–3 days Basic skilled labour       @ 25 bc
  - Assembly and test:    1–3 days Engineer / artillerist     @ 80 bc
Modifiers:
  - Poor timber or light rope: −20% cost, −1 Cycle reliability
  - Fine iron and seasoned oak: +30%
Output: 1 engine, transportable in sections
Base price: 40–80 gc
Logistical notes:
  Moves on a heavy cart or in sections.
  Needs firm ground and space to wind.
  Shot is local stone or pre-cast; not part of the engine cost.

--------------------------------------------------
BALLISTA
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Direct-fire torsion bolt-thrower
Inputs:
  - 4–6 m³ selected timber (frame and stock)
  - 25–35 kg iron (prods, trigger, fittings)
  - 15–20 kg heavy rope or composite prod material
  - Bolts (separate; see Finished Arms for heavy bolts)
Labour Component Split (Total ~8–12 man-days):
  - Frame:                4–5 days Higher skilled carpenter   @ 40 bc
  - Iron work:            3–4 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Assembly and sighting:1–3 days Engineer                   @ 80 bc
Output: 1 engine
Base price: 25–50 gc
Logistical notes:
  Lighter than a mangonel. Two men can shift it short
  distances once emplaced. Preferred for walls and
  counter-battery work.

--------------------------------------------------
TREBUCHET (COUNTERWEIGHT)
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Gravity stone-thrower
Inputs:
  - 25–40 m³ heavy structural timber (frame, arm, counterweight box)
  - 80–120 kg iron (axle, fittings, sling hardware)
  - 50–70 kg rope
  - Counterweight mass (stone, earth, or lead; often local)
Labour Component Split (Total ~40–60 man-days):
  - Heavy frame:         20–25 days Higher skilled carpenter  @ 40 bc
  - Iron and axle:        8–10 days Higher skilled smith      @ 65 bc
  - Rigging:              5–8 days Basic skilled labour       @ 25 bc
  - Design and erection:  7–12 days Engineer                  @ 80 bc
Output: 1 engine (usually built on site)
Base price: 120–250 gc (or equivalent local labour + timber)
Logistical notes:
  Rarely transported whole. Built where it will stand.
  The counterweight and the throwing arm dominate time.
  A major siege commitment. See also Architecture costs
  for the working platform.

--------------------------------------------------
BATTERING RAM
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Covered or open ram
Inputs:
  - 6–10 m³ heavy timber (frame and beam)
  - 30–50 kg iron (head shoe, fittings, wheels or rollers)
  - Hide or timber cover if protected
Labour Component Split (Total ~6–10 man-days):
  - Beam and frame:       4–6 days Carpenter                 @ 40 bc
  - Iron head:            2–3 days Smith                     @ 65 bc
  - Cover (if any):       1–2 days Labour / leather          @ 25–30 bc
Output: 1 ram
Base price: 15–30 gc
Logistical notes:
  Often improvised from local timber and a smith’s shoe.
  The covered version approaches the cost of a small mantlet
  wall plus the ram.

--------------------------------------------------
SIEGE TOWER
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Mobile covered approach tower
Inputs:
  - 40–70 m³ structural timber
  - 60–100 kg iron (fastenings, wheels or rollers)
  - Hides or wet hides for fire protection (optional)
Labour Component Split (Total ~50–80 man-days):
  - Structure:           30–40 days Carpenter                @ 40 bc
  - Iron and mobility:   10–15 days Smith                    @ 65 bc
  - Covering and finish:  5–10 days Labour                   @ 25 bc
  - Design:               5–10 days Engineer                 @ 80 bc
Output: 1 tower (almost always built on site)
Base price: 80–180 gc
Logistical notes:
  More building than weapon. See Economy_Finished_Architecture.txt
  for comparable timber structures. Movement requires a
  prepared approach and many bodies. Fire is the usual end.

--------------------------------------------------
MANTLET / PAVISE WALL
--------------------------------------------------
Type: Mobile cover
Inputs:
  - 1–2 m³ timber or heavy boards
  - 8–15 kg iron (frame, wheels or handles)
  - Hide facing optional
Labour Component Split (Total ~2–4 man-days):
  - Frame and face:       1.5–2.5 days Carpenter             @ 40 bc
  - Iron:                 0.5–1 day Smith                    @ 65 bc
Output: 1 mantlet
Base price: 5–12 gc
Logistical notes:
  Common, often made in quantity. Can be carried or
  wheeled. The cheapest way to give a crew hard cover.

--------------------------------------------------
GENERAL LOGISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS
--------------------------------------------------
- Ammunition (stone, bolt, fire-pot) is never included
  in the engine price. It is bought or gathered separately.
- Transport of a finished engine adds cart-days and
  escort. A mangonel in sections needs one heavy cart.
  A trebuchet is not moved; it is built.
- Site preparation (level ground, protective ditch,
  approach ramp) is Engineering labour, not part of
  the engine ticket.
- Local timber of the right scantling is the usual
  constraint, not coin. A mark with good oak and a
  smith can raise engines faster than it can import them.
- Captured engines are worth their remaining structural
  integrity. A burnt frame is firewood and scrap iron.
