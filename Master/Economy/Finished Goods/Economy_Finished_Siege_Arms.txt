HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Engines are not sold by the piece in ordinary shops.
The figures below are the material + labour value of a
finished engine in good order, ready to be emplaced.
Transport, site preparation, and local scarcity sit on top.

Cost by trade-rate, not by hat.
Book each block at that trade’s wage.
Parallel days shorten the calendar. They do not cheapen the sum.
Wait (seasoning of heavy timber, curing of rope) is storage
and a small premium, not extra labour days.

Stay inside the categories. Named engines are types.
Iron Grade on metal parts follows the same rules as
Economy_Finished_Arms.txt.
Timber is selected structural blanks or heavy scantling,
not boards or firewood.

--------------------------------------------------
TENSION ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------

RAMPART CROSSBOW (MAUERARMBRUST)
Type: Heavy wall-sniper / braced crossbow
Inputs:
  - 1.2–1.6 kg high-carbon oil-tempered spring steel (laminated prod)
  - 3–4 kg dense seasoned hornbeam or yew (stock)
  - 0.8–1.2 kg iron (collar, recoil hook, trigger housing, fittings)
  - Heavy composite linen cord (2,000+ strands, waxed and tallowed)
  - Revolving nut (ivory or cast bronze) + steel lever
  - Dual-crank windlass (separate, iron and timber)
  - Leather sling and brass-lined groove
Labour Component Split (Total ~6–9 man-days):
  - Prod forging and temper:  2–3 days Higher skilled smith      @ 65 bc
  - Stock shaping and finish: 1.5–2 days Higher skilled carpenter @ 40 bc
  - Lock, hook, and fittings: 1.5–2 days Higher skilled smith     @ 65 bc
  - Cord and assembly:        1–1.5 days Specialist labour        @ 35 bc
  - Windlass:                 0.5–1 day Smith / carpenter         @ 50 bc
Modifiers:
  - Poor steel or green stock: −25% cost, unreliable snap and warp risk
  - Fine laminated prod + ivory nut: +40%
Output: 1 crossbow + separate windlass
Base price: 18–35 gc
Logistical notes:
  Man-portable by one soldier via sling when uncocked.
  Assistant carries windlass and bolt quiver.
  Requires stone rampart or swivel frame to fire safely.
  Bolts are heavy all-iron armor-piercing (separate cost).
  Not a field weapon. Common on permanent walls and tower posts.

GREAT TOWER CROSSBOW (BALLISTA DE TURRE)
Type: Massive frame-mounted wall crossbow
Inputs:
  - 2.5–3.5 kg high-carbon oil-tempered laminated spring steel (prod)
  - 12–18 kg heavy seasoned oak (permanent carriage and stock)
  - 2–3 kg iron (collar, fittings, winch hardware, bolts)
  - Heavy composite linen or sinew cord (scaled up from rampart)
  - Reinforced revolving nut (bronze or iron) + long lever
  - Integrated or paired dual-man heavy winch
Labour Component Split (Total ~14–20 man-days):
  - Prod forging and temper:   4–6 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Carriage and stock:        5–7 days Higher skilled carpenter   @ 40 bc
  - Lock, fittings, winch:     3–4 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Cord, assembly, mounting:  2–3 days Specialist / engineer      @ 50–80 bc
Modifiers:
  - Poor steel or green oak: −20% cost, high warp and snap risk
  - Fine laminated prod + bronze nut: +35%
Output: 1 mounted engine (carriage permanent; bow assembly transportable at limit)
Base price: 45–80 gc
Logistical notes:
  The bow assembly alone is the absolute one-man carry limit (~45 lbs).
  Full assembly stays bolted to the fortress.
  Requires a prepared stone or timber mount.
  Bolts are heavy armor-piercing (separate).
  Anchors major defensive positions; not field-mobile.

OXYBELES
Type: Early tension frame-bow
Inputs:
  - Composite bow limbs (horn, sinew, wood)
  - 4–6 kg timber frame
  - 1–1.5 kg iron fittings
Labour: ~8–12 man-days
Base price: 30–55 gc
Notes: Lighter than Great Tower. Still requires a brace or frame.

--------------------------------------------------
BOLTS — TENSION ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Ammunition is separate from the engine.
Sold by the dozen or the score in fortress stores.
Not ordinary crossbow bolts.

Rampart Bolt (Mauerbolzen)
Inputs:
  - 0.15–0.25 kg hardened carbon steel (long narrow bodkin head)
  - Dense ash or hornbeam shaft blank, tapered
  - Leather or horn vane stock + copper wire binding
Labour: ~0.2–0.25 man-day per bolt (smith head + specialist fletching)
Base price: 10–18 sc each
Notes: 12-inch. Diamond or square bodkin optimised for plate and mail.
       Leather/horn vanes bound with copper wire.
       Dedicated to the Rampart Crossbow. Quiver of 8–12 typical for a post.

Great Tower Bolt (Turmbolzen / Bolt-Spear)
Inputs:
  - 0.6–0.9 kg iron or steel (socketed jacket or solid shaft + chisel head)
  - Dense hornbeam core (if composite) or full forged shaft
  - Sheet brass or copper for riveted vanes
Labour: ~0.4–0.5 man-day per bolt (smith + fitter)
Base price: 35–55 sc each
Notes: 16–18 inch. Barbed or solid-pyramid chisel head wider than the shaft.
       Metal vanes cold-riveted and angled for spin.
       Closer to light artillery than a quarrel.
       Dedicated to the Great Tower Crossbow. Stored in fortress magazines.

TORSION ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------

EUTHYTONE
Inputs: timber frame, iron fittings, heavy rope or sinew bundles
Labour: ~8–12 man-days
Base price: 20–40 gc

BALLISTA
Type: Direct-fire torsion bolt-thrower
Inputs:
  - 4–6 m³ selected timber (frame and stock)
  - 25–35 kg iron (prods, trigger, fittings)
  - 15–20 kg heavy rope or composite prod material
  - Bolts (separate)
Labour Component Split (Total ~8–12 man-days):
  - Frame:                4–5 days Higher skilled carpenter   @ 40 bc
  - Iron work:            3–4 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Assembly and sighting:1–3 days Engineer                   @ 80 bc
Output: 1 engine
Base price: 25–50 gc
Logistical notes:
  Lighter than a mangonel. Two men can shift it short
  distances once emplaced. Preferred for walls and
  counter-battery work.

SCORPION
Inputs: light timber, iron fittings, smaller torsion bundles
Labour: ~5–8 man-days
Base price: 12–25 gc
Notes: Man-portable in sections.

ONAGER
Type: Torsion stone-thrower
Inputs:
  - 8–12 m³ selected oak or ash scantling (frame and arm)
  - 40–60 kg iron (fittings, axle, shot-cup reinforcement)
  - 30–40 kg heavy rope or sinew (torsion bundles)
  - Stone shot or cast balls as ammunition (separate)
Labour Component Split (Total ~12–18 man-days):
  - Frame and arm:        6–8 days Higher skilled carpenter   @ 40 bc
  - Iron fittings:        3–4 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Rope work and wind:   2–3 days Basic skilled labour       @ 25 bc
  - Assembly and test:    1–3 days Engineer / artillerist     @ 80 bc
Output: 1 engine, transportable in sections
Base price: 40–80 gc
Logistical notes:
  Moves on a heavy cart or in sections.
  Needs firm ground and space to wind.
  Shot is local stone or pre-cast; not part of the engine cost.

SPRINGALD
Inputs: timber housing, iron fittings, torsion bundles
Labour: ~7–11 man-days
Base price: 22–45 gc
Notes: Often wall- or ship-mounted.

--------------------------------------------------
COUNTERWEIGHT ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Damage, range, and reload live on the payload
(see Siege_Weapons.txt matrix). The figures below
are the engine structure only.
Ammunition is never included.

MANGONEL (man-powered / traction)
Inputs:
  - 3–5 m³ light timber
  - 15–25 kg iron fittings and rope hardware
  - Heavy pulling ropes
Labour: ~6–9 man-days
Base price: 18–35 gc
Notes: Simplest form. No substantial ballast box.
       Fast to build and move.

Tier 1: Traction / Light Trebuchet
Type: Hybrid traction-counterweight, mobile
Inputs:
  - 6–10 m³ selected oak or ash (frame, arm, small ballast box)
  - 30–45 kg iron (axle, fittings, rope hardware)
  - 20–30 kg heavy rope (pulling lines and sling)
  - Small ballast (local stone or clay)
Labour Component Split (Total ~10–14 man-days):
  - Frame and arm:        5–7 days Higher skilled carpenter   @ 40 bc
  - Iron fittings:        2–3 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Rigging and test:     2–3 days Engineer / labour          @ 40–80 bc
Output: 1 engine, transportable in sections on two heavy wagons
Base price: 35–60 gc
Logistical notes:
  Footprint 4 m × 3 m. Setup or pack-down ~30 minutes with
  Engineering Test. Hybrid man-rope assist on small ballast.
  Fastest counterweight cycle. Local stone for most shot.

Tier 2: Fortified Siege Trebuchet (The Couillard)
Type: Heavy on-site counterweight
Inputs:
  - 20–30 m³ structural timber (frame, arm, large pivoted box)
  - 70–100 kg iron (axle, fittings, winch hardware)
  - 40–60 kg rope
  - Ballast mass (iron ore, wet clay, or local stone; often gathered)
Labour Component Split (Total ~30–45 man-days):
  - Heavy frame and box:  15–20 days Higher skilled carpenter  @ 40 bc
  - Iron and axle:         6–8 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Rigging and erection:  6–10 days Engineer + labour         @ 40–80 bc
Output: 1 engine (built on site; not movable once anchored)
Base price: 90–160 gc (or equivalent local timber + labour)
Logistical notes:
  Footprint 8 m × 6 m. 2–3 days construction from structural logs.
  Workhorse for wall and gate reduction. Outside most
  wall-crossbow range when properly sited.

Tier 3: Fortress Breaker (The Warwolf Class)
Type: Colossal gravity engine
Inputs:
  - 60–100 m³ heavy structural timber (beams thicker than oak trunks)
  - 150–250 kg iron (axles, fittings, dual capstan hardware)
  - 80–120 kg rope
  - Ballast cradle for up to 15 tons lead/rubble (mass often local)
Labour Component Split (Total ~120–180 man-days):
  - Monolithic frame:     60–80 days Master carpenters          @ 50 bc
  - Iron and capstans:    20–30 days Higher skilled smith       @ 65 bc
  - Design, balance, erection: 25–40 days Master engineers     @ 100 bc
  - Logging and haul:     included in the above or separate camp
Output: 1 engine (site-built only)
Base price: 300–550 gc (or multi-week local commitment)
Logistical notes:
  Footprint 15 m × 12 m earthwork. 4–6 weeks, active logging camp,
  hard Engineering Test to balance torque. Failure risks collapse.
  Beast-of-burden assistance standard for the dual giant capstans.
  Strategic piece for breaking historic deadlocks. Not mobile.

--------------------------------------------------
BLACK POWDER ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Early gunpowder pieces. Powder, shot, and barrels are
specialist and scarce. Prices reflect that scarcity.
Misfire and burst risk sit outside the cost ticket.

FALCONET / SAKER
Inputs: bronze or iron barrel, timber carriage, iron fittings
Labour: ~12–18 man-days (founder + carpenter + smith)
Base price: 40–70 gc
Notes: Lightest practical cannon.

CULVERIN
Inputs: longer bronze or iron barrel, reinforced carriage
Labour: ~20–30 man-days
Base price: 80–140 gc

DEMI-CANNON / BOMBARD
Inputs: heavy cast barrel (bronze or iron), massive timber bed
Labour: ~40–70 man-days (often on-site assembly)
Base price: 150–300 gc
Notes: Wall-breaker. Bombards frequently built or finished on site.

MORTAR
Inputs: short heavy barrel, timber bed, iron fittings
Labour: ~15–25 man-days
Base price: 60–110 gc
Notes: High-angle. Shells or stone shot separate.

RACK OF FIRE
Inputs: timber frame, multiple light tubes or rocket guides, iron fittings
Labour: ~10–16 man-days
Base price: 50–90 gc
Notes: Experimental. Rockets or fire-pots are the payload and
       are not included. High risk of premature ignition.
