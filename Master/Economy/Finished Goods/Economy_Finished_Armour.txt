HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Armour is sold by the piece. A pair is one listing where Armour.txt says pair.

Enc 10 = 1 kg. Finished mass is Base Enc / 10 after Forms.
The metal, cloth, or hide bought is never the finished mass.
Waste is in the input line.

Listed prices here are Common, low-carbon steel (or riveted mail).
Core Iron Grade then multiplies a plate/segmented ticket:
  Wrought iron   x0.80    (Wrought Iron)
  Low-carbon     x1.00    (Steel)
  Hardened       x1.25    (Fine Steel)
  Crucible       x2.50    (Crucible Steel)
Butted mail is not a grade. It is a worse join: x0.6 cost, x1.2 Enc, AC 2/2/2.

Stay inside the categories. Named Models in Armour.txt are types, not separate files.

--------------------------------------------------
THREE SHOPS
--------------------------------------------------

Mail, segmented, and plate are not the same trade with a different ticket.
They are three ways of spending days.

Wages used here
  Ring-hand / apprentice     16 bc/day    (0.25 x Higher skilled 65)
  Basic skilled              30 bc/day    tailor, leatherworker
  Higher skilled armourer    65 bc/day
  Master armourer           155 bc/day    (mid of 130-180)

1. MAIL -- the sweatshop
  Modular, repetitive, low-risk once the wire exists.
  An experienced smith draws the wire, cuts the rings, and forges
  collar, latches, and any solid bits.
  Weaving and riveting go to a circle of apprentices.

  Split of booked days:
    10% Higher skilled armourer
    90% apprentice / ring-hand

  Mail stays dear because the calendar is long and the bar is heavy
  (waste 1.80), not because every hour is an armourer's hour.

  Where: any decent town or castle garrison that keeps a few
  apprentices and a smith who can draw wire.
  Repair: a garrison can close rings. It cannot start from bloom
  without that smith.

2. PLATE -- the art academy
  A breast is geometry, heat, and a sheet you can ruin.
  Dishing a uniform curve without thin spots is not apprentice work.
  If the lad overheats the plate, the steel is lost.

  Split of booked days:
    100% Higher skilled armourer on Common work
    Master armourer on Fine / closed / fitted work
    Apprentice may pump bellows and hold. Not on the hammer line.

  Plate looks "cheap per kilo" because it is fewer days than
  30,000 rings. The premium is the brain, not the hours.

  Where: capital, great foundry-town, or a named armourer's hall.
  A hamlet cannot buy or truly repair solid plate.
  A market town may straighten a ding. It may not re-dish a breast.

3. SEGMENTED -- the middle bench
  Shears, holes, rivets, and a foundation coat.
  The smith cuts and shapes the plates. Hands can punch, rivet,
  and stitch the coat.

  Split of booked days (default):
    40% Higher skilled armourer
    60% apprentice or basic skilled (leather / rivet)

  Coat of plates leans toward the smith (50 / 50).
  Brigandine and scale lean toward the hands (30 / 70) once the
  plates exist.
  Laminar sits with the smith (50 / 50): the bands have to fit.

  Where: a market town with an armourer and a bench.
  Not every garrison. Not only the capital.


Textile and leather stay Basic skilled. A household can quilt an
aketon. A household cannot rivet a hauberk or dish a breast.

--------------------------------------------------
MAILLE
--------------------------------------------------

Sweatshop. Riveted is the shop baseline.
Bought mass is finished mass x 1.80 in Wrought Iron or Steel.
Wire is drawn from the bar. Rings are cut, overlapped, riveted.

Labour on every mail row:
  10% Higher skilled (wire, cut, collar, latches)
  90% apprentice ring-hand
Calendar is still four to eight weeks on a hauberk.
Do not book those weeks at 65 bc/day.

Worked floor, short/short riveted hauberk, 6 weeks (36 days):
  Steel 13.5 kg x 6 sc                 81 sc
  Armourer 3.6 days x 65 bc            19 sc 5 bc
  Ring-hands 32.4 days x 16 bc         43 sc 2 bc
  Charcoal, rivets, shop               a few sc
  Floor                                about 7-8 gc
Band above that is overhead, long sleeves/hem, and a tighter shop.

Type: Mail

Inputs:

Every fabrication day is Higher skilled. Fine / closed / fitted pulls Master.

BOOTS
--------------------------------------------------

A listing is a pair.
Base Material is mandatory (Textile layer).
Armour Material is optional and only on Cuffed or Riding.
Cobbler is Basic skilled. Splint or greave add-on uses the segmented or plate shop for that add-on only.

Type: Turnshoes, Ankle, Cuffed, Riding, Poulaine, Clog
Inputs:
  - Leather, Suede, or Felt & leather as the Base table
  - last, thread, tallow
Labour: Cobbler, a day to three
Output: 1 pair
Base price (leather base, Common):
  Clog / slipper     6-10 sc     Core 8 sc
  Turnshoes          8-14 sc     Core 11 sc
  Poulaine           12-18 sc    Core 15 sc
  Ankle boots        12-20 sc    Core 16 sc
  Cuffed boots       16-28 sc    Core 22 sc
  Riding boots       18-32 sc    Core 25 sc

Armour Material on Cuffed or Riding only, added:
  Padded         +6-10 sc     Core +8 sc
  Cuir bouilli   +14-22 sc    Core +18 sc
  Splinting      +12-20 sc    Core +16 sc
  Greaves        +18-30 sc    Core +24 sc

Plate greaves on the shin are their own Modular piece.
They do not stack AC with boot greaves.

--------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------

- It does not stack AC.
- It does not price a shield (not in the armour catalogue).
- It does not let T0 rivet mail or dish plate.
- It does not let an apprentice dish a breast.
- It does not apply Iron Grade twice.
- It does not recost Sea-serpent or troll-hide upgrades. Those are Unveiling goods on top of this floor.
