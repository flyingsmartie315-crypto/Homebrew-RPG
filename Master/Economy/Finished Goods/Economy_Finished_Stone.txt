Stone — Finished
Bespoke and commission pieces.
These are not stock tickets. Price, time, and even the final form
are set by the commission: size, stone, detail, craftsman, and patron.

A city may commission a ten-foot statue of the king.
A wealthy house may order a carved stone table.
A patron may want a bust, a tomb effigy, a fountain basin,
or a pair of heraldic supporters.
Each is its own agreement.

--------------------------------------------------
INPUT
--------------------------------------------------
- Intact Unit direct from the quarry (Primary_Stone), or
- Processed blank (squared carving blank, millstone blank, facing blank)
  from Economy_Processed_Stone.txt.
The craftsman chooses. A master may prefer the raw block;
a busy yard may start from a dressed blank.

--------------------------------------------------
WHAT SETS THE PRICE
--------------------------------------------------
No fixed Base per kg.
The commission price is the sum of:
- Stone (intact premium or processed blank)
- Labour days of the named craftsman and any assistants
- Detail and risk (undercutting, thin sections, portrait likeness)
- Transport and erection if the piece is large
- Patron’s standing and the yard’s reputation

A small bust in local freestone is one order of cost.
A ten-foot royal statue in marble, crated and set in a square, is another.
The same craftsman may charge differently for the same nominal size
if one piece is a quick tabletop and the other is a likeness that must satisfy a court.

--------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLES (SCALE ONLY)
--------------------------------------------------
These are illustrations, not price locks.

Bust or small relief
  Modest stone, days to weeks. Single craftsman.

Carved table, basin, or architectural ornament
  Selected blank, one to several weeks. May leave the yard finished.

Tomb effigy or life-size figure
  Large intact or heavy blank. Weeks to months. Often erected on site.

Major public statue or monument
  Multiple blocks or one exceptional piece. Months.
  Transport, scaffolding, and setting are part of the commission.
  Stone cost is real but usually the smaller fraction beside labour and risk.

--------------------------------------------------
RULES
--------------------------------------------------
- Do not stock these as ordinary shop goods.
- Do not apply the 2.5–6× processed guideline as a hard rule.
  Craftsmanship and commission dominate.
- Failed or abandoned pieces may be broken back to bulk or rubble;
  the patron normally still owes the labour spent.
- Siege, architecture, and ordinary building continue to draw
  dressed stone from Economy_Processed_Stone.txt.
  This file is only the commissioned finished object.

--------------------------------------------------
NOTES
--------------------------------------------------
Short by design. Variability is the point.
When a specific commission appears in play, price it from
the stone ticket + the craftsman’s day rate + the agreed detail,
not from a table in this file.
