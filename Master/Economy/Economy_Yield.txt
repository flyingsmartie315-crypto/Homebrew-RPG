# Economy_Yield.txt
# How much a settlement takes in a year.
# Crops: Economy_Agriculture.txt
# Beasts: Economy_Livestock.txt
# Site scores: Geography_Rules.txt and Bestiary_Rules.txt
# Prices: Economy_Primary_Goods.txt

This file does not invent hectares.

Field take uses the people and the ground they work.
Margins sit beside the field. They are not a second plough.

Margins in this file
- Burgage garden (house plot)
- Forage and trap (wood, fringe, custom)
- Water take (fish, and named sea-work)
- Yard and herd (poultry, pig, sheep, goat, cattle)

Do not mash them into one “food” number.
A lake town can be short of barley and still eat.


--------------------------------------------------
ORDER
--------------------------------------------------

Field crop
1. Domain
2. Biome
3. Site scores
4. HSI match → Suitability S
5. S → Yield factor Y
6. Who is on the land → field-hands F
7. What they plant → hand-share
8. Take = F × share × class yield × Y

Stop a crop at the first failed gate. Failed Domain or Biome → that crop is 0.

Margins (after the field, every year)
9. Burgage
10. Forage and trap
11. Water take
12. Yard and herd

Hands used for forage, nets, and stock are not taken out of F.
They come from the household share that is not behind a plough.


--------------------------------------------------
1. DOMAIN
--------------------------------------------------

Read Domain from the crop.
Almost all entries in Economy_Agriculture.txt are Domain: Land.

If the worked ground is not that Domain, take is 0.
Do not run the rest.


--------------------------------------------------
2. BIOME
--------------------------------------------------

Read Biome tags from the crop.
Read the Biome of the ground the fields sit on, not the water the docks sit on.

- Settlement names a Linked Feature where the fields are → use that feature’s Biome
- Else use the Local Region Biome
- A matching Sub-biome allows the crop only on those features, not on the whole country

No overlap → take 0.
Do not run HSI.


--------------------------------------------------
3. SITE SCORES
--------------------------------------------------

Settlements do not yet print a seven-number line.
Build one.

Start from:
- the Linked Feature the fields sit on, if named
- else the Local Region Harvest line

Harvest is the printed score. Annual yield uses Harvest.
Do not apply a seasonal template to the year’s bushels.
Use a seasonal template only when asked if a crop is in the ground, failing, or being lifted in a named season.

Then replace Human Density with the settlement’s own figure.
Region Human Density averages empty wood and water. Crops need the people who live here.

Settlement Human Density
- Ruin / empty site: 0
- Hamlet, or fewer than 200 people: 2
- Village, or 200–1,500: 3
- Town, or 1,500–8,000: 4
- City, or more than 8,000: 5
- Castle / Order seat with working fields: 3
- Castle / Order seat with no fields: 2

If the settlement file later prints its own score line, use that line and do not derive.

Population figure is the midpoint of the printed range.
About 4,000–6,000 → 5,000.


--------------------------------------------------
4. HSI MATCH
--------------------------------------------------

Same method as Bestiary_Rules.txt.
Compare the crop HSI to the site scores from step 3.

For each axis the crop actually uses:
- Site score inside the crop range = 1
- 1 step outside = 0.5
- 2 or more steps outside = 0
- Dash (—) on the crop = omit that axis from the sum

S = total points ÷ axes used.

Human Density on a crop line means cleared, worked land and standing people.
Flora is cover on the worked ground.
Fauna is pest and graze pressure around the crop, not the crop itself.
A site Fauna above the crop range is bite and trampling, not “rich country.”


--------------------------------------------------
5. YIELD FACTOR
--------------------------------------------------

Wildlife can persist at a thin match. A field cannot.
Do not use the creature presence bands as bushels.

S          Y     Meaning
< 0.30     0     Not a field crop here
0.30–0.49  0.35  Thin. Catch crop or desperate row
0.50–0.69  0.60  Poor floor. Hard year is normal
0.70–0.84  0.85  Ordinary take
≥ 0.85     1.00  Full take for this crop on this ground

Kitchen-garden exception:
If 0.20 ≤ S < 0.30 and the crop is ROOT, COLE, SOFT FRUIT, or a bulb, a house may still grow a little on the burgage.
That take is not field yield. Do not add it here.


--------------------------------------------------
6. FIELD-HANDS
--------------------------------------------------

Not everyone in the count holds a plough.

Field fraction (share of the population tied to worked land)

Type                         Fraction
Hamlet                       0.85
Village                      0.75
Town                         0.30
City                         0.12
Castle / Order seat          0.45
Ruin / hazard                0

Read the Economy block and adjust. Stack. Floor 0.05, cap 0.90.

- Mining, kiln, timber-hub, dock-first, “food imported”: −0.20
- “Not a grain capital” / hardier foodstuffs only: −0.10
- Named self-supply, vale fields, market-garden hinterland: +0.10
- Siege, plague, or a logged flight of hands: set by the Campaign Log

Field-hands F = Population midpoint × field fraction × 0.40

The 0.40 is the working share inside those households.
Children, the old, house-work, and by-work are the rest.

F is the hands that can be put on fields, orchards, and dye-rows this year.
It is not soldiers and it is not the dock.


--------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT THEY PLANT
--------------------------------------------------

Do not put every passing crop on every acre.

First, drop any crop with Y = 0.

Then:

Rarity 7–10
Grown if Y ≥ 0.35. These are the staple candidates.

Rarity 5–6
Grown if Y ≥ 0.60, or if the Economy block names them.

Rarity 1–4
Grown only if the Economy block names them (estate, order garden, specialist row).

Among the crops that remain, split F:

Default hand-share (farming village or hamlet)
- Best staple grain or, if no grain passes, best ROOT: 0.60
- Pulse: 0.10
- ROOT / COLE not already used as the staple: 0.15
- OIL AND FIBRE: 0.10 if any such crop has Y ≥ 0.60; else 0
- ORCHARD / VINE / SOFT FRUIT: 0.05 if any has Y ≥ 0.70; else 0

Town or city
Same shape, fewer hands. Do not raise orchard share just because the market wants fruit.
If the Economy block names one crop as the point of the place (hops, grapes, flax, dye), give that crop 0.30–0.50 of F and shrink the staple.

Castle / Order seat
Follow the Economy block. Eyrenal-style vale: staple + garden + hay. No dye estates unless written.

If several crops share a class, give the land to the one with the higher Y.
Tie → the one with the higher Rarity (more commonly kept).

Shares must sum to 1.00 of F, or less if the Economy block says the rest of the hands are hay, fallow, or woodland.


--------------------------------------------------
8. CLASS YIELD
--------------------------------------------------

Kilograms per field-hand per year at Y = 1.00.
That hand’s year is on this class. Split hands before you multiply.

Class                         kg / hand / year
GRAIN                         600
PULSE                         280
ROOT                          900
COLE                          700
OIL AND FIBRE                 160
ORCHARD fruit / nut           350
SOFT FRUIT                    220
VINE (grape, hop as bine)     400
SPICE                         12
DYE                           40
Mulberry / Raw Silk           8

GRAIN and PULSE figures are barn take after ordinary seed is held back.
ROOT and COLE are wet weight at lift.
ORCHARD assumes standing trees, not a first-year whip.

A hop bine uses the VINE line for cone-weight, not for ale.
Silk is cocoons, not leaves.

Hay and woodland graze are not a crop class.
They show up as the pasture factor under Yard and herd.


--------------------------------------------------
TAKE
--------------------------------------------------

Take (kg / year) = F × hand-share × class yield × Y

Report by crop, not as one mashed “food” number.
Convert to sacks, crates, and cart-loads only when a price or a road is needed.
Those pack figures are on Economy_Primary_Goods.txt.


--------------------------------------------------
WORKED: KOLBY-ON-VATN, BARLEY AND WHEAT
--------------------------------------------------

Population midpoint: 5,000
Type: Town
Economy: timber hub, docks, forges; hardier foodstuffs only; not a grain capital

Field fraction: 0.30 − 0.20 − 0.10 = 0.05 → floor 0.05
F = 5,000 × 0.05 × 0.40 = 100 hands

Site
Local Region Kannavald Deepwood, Harvest, fields on the forest-edge floor not the lake.
Biome: Boreal
Printed region: Temperature 2 | Rainfall 4 | Human Density 2 | Flora 4 | Fauna 2 | Topography 3 | Hydrology 3
Settlement Human Density for a town of 5,000: 4
Site line used: Temperature 2 | Rainfall 4 | Human Density 4 | Flora 4 | Fauna 2 | Topography 3 | Hydrology 3

Wheat
Biome: Temperate, Mediterranean, Steppe
No Boreal → stop. Take 0.
Kolby does not grow wheat.

Barley
Biome: Temperate, Boreal, Oceanic, Steppe → pass
HSI: Temperature 1–4 | Rainfall 2–4 | Human Density 3–5 | Flora 3–4 | Fauna 2–3 | Topography 0–2 | Hydrology 2–3

Temperature 2 inside → 1
Rainfall 4 inside → 1
Human Density 4 inside → 1
Flora 4 inside → 1
Fauna 2 inside → 1
Topography 3 is 1 outside 0–2 → 0.5
Hydrology 3 inside → 1

S = 6.5 ÷ 7 ≈ 0.93 → Y = 1.00

Rarity 9 → staple candidate.
Town with “hardier foodstuffs only”: barley (or rye/oats) takes the staple share 0.60.

Barley take = 100 × 0.60 × 600 × 1.00 = 36,000 kg / year
= 900 sacks of 40 kg
= 45 standard cart-loads by weight

Five thousand people cannot live on 36 tonnes of barley.
The margins below are why Kolby is still a town.

--------------------------------------------------
9. BURGAGE
--------------------------------------------------

Every living household may keep a plot.
Households H = Population midpoint ÷ 5

Burgage grows ROOT, COLE, bulbs, and a little SOFT FRUIT only.
It does not grow market grain.

Burgage factor
- Hamlet / village: 1.00
- Town: 0.70
- City: 0.35
- Castle with named gardens: 0.80
- Ruin: 0
- Walled town with no outskirts written: 0.50

If the best ROOT or COLE crop at this site has Y ≥ 0.35, burgage uses that Y.
If no such crop passes the field gate but S ≥ 0.20, use Y = 0.35.
Otherwise burgage = 0.

Take = H × 100 kg × burgage factor × Y

This is house food. Do not cart it unless the Economy block names market-garden.


--------------------------------------------------
10. FORAGE AND TRAP
--------------------------------------------------

Use the Local Region scores, not the settlement Human Density.
Forage is the empty wood and fringe. A town HD of 4 does not make the Deepwood a garden, and it does not empty it either.

Region Human Density is the pressure on the wild take.
Customs, Forest Law, taboo, and “Never Alone” are lids. If a culture file closes the wood, apply the law cut.

Plant forage (nuts, berries, greens, fungi, mast)
Base: 25 kg per person per year at full factor.

Flora factor
0–1 → 0
2 → 0.35
3 → 0.70
4–5 → 1.00

Pressure cut (region Human Density)
0–1 → ×1.00
2 → ×0.85
3 → ×0.55
4 → ×0.25
5 → ×0.05

Law cut
- Open common / no written limit: ×1.00
- Forest Law, licence, or watched wood: ×0.70
- Taboo or closed season most of the year: ×0.40

Plant take = Population × 25 × Flora factor × pressure × law

Trap and small-hunt (meat; hide follows the carcase)
Base: 8 kg meat per person per year at full factor.

Fauna factor
0–1 → 0
2 → 0.50
3 → 0.80
4–5 → 1.00

Pressure cut (region Human Density)
0–1 → ×1.00
2 → ×0.75
3 → ×0.40
4 → ×0.10
5 → ×0

Same law cut as plant forage.

Trap take = Population × 8 × Fauna factor × pressure × law

Fur and hide from this take follow Economy_Livestock.txt slaughter parts on GAME, in rough proportion to the meat.
Do not run a second hunt on top for the same mouths.

This margin is house food and a little trade-fur.
Named commercial hunting (a warrener, a fur post) exists only if the Economy block says so. Then raise trap take ×1.5 and send a third of it off-site.


--------------------------------------------------
11. WATER TAKE
--------------------------------------------------

Fish is not a field crop.
Read the water feature the settlement actually works: lake, river, strand, harbour.
If none is linked and Hydrology on the land line is 0–1, water take is 0.

Water factor
Hydrology (or the water feature’s Hydrology)
0–1 → 0
2 → 0.35
3 → 0.70
4–5 → 1.00

Fauna on that same water feature
0–1 → ×0.20
2 → ×0.60
3 → ×0.85
4–5 → ×1.00

Use one method, not both:

Household nets
When the Economy block does not name docks, fleets, or fishers as work.
Take = Population × 15 kg × water factor

Working fishery
When the Economy block names fishing, docks, smoke-houses, or a fleet.
Fish fraction
- Hamlet / village: 0.15
- Town: 0.10
- Named fishing port or fish-staple place: 0.20
Fish-hands = Population × fish fraction × 0.40
Take = fish-hands × 800 kg × water factor

800 kg is mixed catch per working fisher-year, lake or river.
Named ocean work: 1,200 kg per hand, same factor, using the oceanic feature.
A whale or a leviathan is a voyage. Do not fold it into this annual line.

Do not add household nets on top of a working fishery for the same water.
The working number already feeds the town and leaves a surplus.


--------------------------------------------------
12. YARD AND HERD
--------------------------------------------------

Stock numbers come from households and ground, not from a hidden census.
H = Population midpoint ÷ 5

Read live output and slaughter weights from Economy_Livestock.txt when you need a part-by-part split.
This section only needs yearly offtake.

Pasture factor (large stock)
Flora 0–1 → 0.15
Flora 2 → 0.60
Flora 3 → 1.00
Flora 4 → 0.70 sheep and goat, 0.40 cattle, 1.00 pannage pig
Flora 5 → 0.40 sheep and goat, 0.20 cattle, 1.00 pannage pig

Then:
- Topography 4–5: cattle ×0.50, goat unchanged, sheep ×0.70
- Rainfall 0–1 with no irrigation written: all graze-stock ×0.40
- Economy names sheepwalk, cattle, or dairy: large stock ×1.50, requires pasture factor ≥ 0.60
- Economy names mine, kiln, or “food imported”: large stock ×0.60
- Town / city already use the thinner head-counts below. Do not stack a second town cut.

Head-count per household before pasture factor

                 Chicken   Pig   Sheep or goat   Cattle
Hamlet/village   6         1     4               0.40
Town             3         0.5   0.5             0.05
City             1         0.2   0               0
Castle / vale    4         0.8   2               0.25

Pick sheep on open or mixed floor (Flora 2–3, Topography 0–2).
Pick goat on steep, thin, or browse-wood (Topography 3–5, or Flora 4–5).
A place may keep a little of both. Do not give every house 4 sheep and 4 goats.

Chickens ignore pasture factor.
Pigs use pannage (Flora 4–5) or scraps (town). Pasture factor for pigs is 1.00 unless Flora is 0–1 (then 0.50).

Yearly take per head kept
Figures are midpoints from Economy_Livestock.txt, as offtake, not a slaughter of the whole herd.

Chicken: 100 eggs + 1.0 kg meat
Duck or goose: only if Economy or a water feature names them. Duck 80 eggs + 1.5 kg; goose 20 eggs + 3 kg
Pig: 30 kg pork (herd turning, not every pig killed)
Sheep: 2.0 kg wool + 30 kg milk + 8 kg mutton
Goat: 180 kg milk + 6 kg meat
Cattle: 170 kg milk + 20 kg beef
  (about one in three head is a milker; oxen are labour, not a meat crop)

Draft horses, mules, and donkeys are labour. Do not harvest them.

Take = H × heads per household × pasture factor × yearly take per head

Report milk, eggs, wool, and meat separately.
Slaughter by-parts (hide, tallow, bone) only when a craft or a cart needs them. Pull those weights from Economy_Livestock.txt against the meat offtake, not against the live herd.

--------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------

- It does not price the take. Use Economy_Primary_Goods.txt.
- It does not assign tithe, rent, or who owns the barn. That is Economy_Rules.
- It does not grow a crop that failed Domain or Biome because the market wants it.
- It does not use region Human Density for a town’s fields.
- It does not use town Human Density for forage.
- It does not turn Rarity into kilograms. Rarity only decides if the crop is planted.
- It does not slaughter the live herd to book a year’s meat.
- It does not count a whale, a gryphon, or a royal hunt as annual yield.
