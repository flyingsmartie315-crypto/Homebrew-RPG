--------------------------------------------------
DAIRY — PROCESSED
--------------------------------------------------
Butter and cheese are processed goods.
They must cost at least their milk input plus labour.

Butter
Made from milk. Roughly 20 kg milk → 1 kg butter.
At milk 1.5 bc/kg the input is 30 bc before labour and loss.
Base: 35–40 bc per kg
Bulk: 900 kg/m³
Packing: cask
Labour: churning, working, salting. T0–T1.
Notes: Clears the input floor. Old 12 bc price is retired.

Cheese
Made from milk. Roughly 8–10 kg milk → 1 kg cheese.
At milk 1.5 bc/kg the input is 12–15 bc before labour, rennet, and loss.
Base: 18–20 bc per kg
Bulk: 700 kg/m³
Packing: sack / crate
Labour: setting, pressing, salting, short cure. T0–T1.
Notes: Clears the input floor. Old 14 bc price is retired.

Both remain subject to regional ±50%.
Long-cured or named cheeses may sit higher.

--------------------------------------------------
LEATHER — PROCESSED
--------------------------------------------------
Hides made usable.
Input is raw hide from Primary Animal Products (or the slaughter line of Livestock).
Different species and processes produce different properties and prices.
Generic “leather” at 40 bc/kg remains the ordinary mixed or unspecified ticket.
Named grades sit above or below it.

Common / Mixed Leather
Tanned hide, species not distinguished.
Made from raw hides, tannin, and time.
Used for shoes, belts, tack, and jacks.
Base: 40 bc per kg
Bulk: 400 kg/m³
Packing: bundle
Bundle (20 kg) → 66 sc 8 bc
Larger lot (50 kg) → 166 sc 8 bc
Cart load (800 kg) → 2,666 sc 8 bc
Heavy-cart load (1,500 kg) → 5,000 sc
Notes: Default when the hide is ordinary cattle, horse, or mixed.

Cattle Hide Leather
Thick, strong, takes heavy use.
Used for soles, harness, shields, buckets.
Base: 45–55 bc per kg
Notes: Higher than mixed. Preferred for structural leather.

Calfskin
Thinner, finer grain.
Used for gloves, light shoes, bookbinding, fine tack.
Base: 70–90 bc per kg
Notes: Premium. Less yield per hide.

Sheep / Goat Leather
Softer, more flexible. Goat is tighter.
Used for clothing linings, light bags, gloves.
Base: 30–40 bc per kg
Notes: At or below the mixed ticket. Not for heavy harness.

Pigskin
Tough but less even. Takes tooling.
Used for saddles, cheap shoes, work gloves.
Base: 35–45 bc per kg

Deer / Game Leather
Soft when brain- or smoke-tanned. Variable.
Used for clothing, light bags.
Base: 40–60 bc per kg
Notes: Depends on process. Bark-tanned is stiffer.

Horsehide
Dense and durable.
Used for heavy boots, harness, protective gear.
Base: 50–65 bc per kg

Rawhide (minimally processed)
Stretched and dried, not fully tanned.
Used for lashings, drumheads, temporary bindings.
Base: 15–25 bc per kg
Notes: Cheaper. Stiffens when wet, then dries hard.

Oil-tanned / Soft Leather
Extra labour and oil or fat.
Used for garments, gloves, supple tack.
Base: +30–50% over the species base
Notes: Process premium, not a separate species.

Heavy Bark-tanned
Long tan, thick result.
Used for soles, armour components, buckets.
Base: +20–40% over the species base
Notes: Process premium.

--------------------------------------------------
RULES
--------------------------------------------------
- Raw hide price is Primary. These tickets are after tannin, labour, and time.
- Species and process may stack (e.g. oil-tanned calfskin).
- Finished Arms, Armour, and Clothing that specify a leather grade
  should name the species or process, not only “leather.”
- Waste and trim are normal. A finished piece weighs less than the hide bought.
- Regional ±50% still applies. Scarce game or fine calf raises the top of the band.
