# Base unit: 1 kg, or 1 litre where noted. 1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc.

Woodwork
Timber sawn, split, hewn, or selected for use.
About a third to half of a log is lost to slab, bark, and sawdust.
The input is always a Forestry log or selected trunk of that species.
A piece too short or shaken for the named use drops to mixed planks, or to firewood.
Packed planks and beams: Bulk 500 kg/m³, loose-stacked.
Shingles: Bulk 350 kg/m³, bundled.

FORMS

Planks / Boards
Input: A sound log, bark on or roughly barked.
Labour: Saw-pit or mill. Two men and a saw, or a water-saw. Then stacking to season.
Use: Floors, walls, hull-skin, doors, chests, common furniture.
Property: Even thickness. Short offcuts are not shafts.

Roof shingles
Input: Straight-grained log, usually pine or oak.
Labour: Split with froe and mallet, then dressed thin.
Use: Roofs, sometimes wall-hang.
Property: Thin, overlapping, sheds water.

Spar / pole blank
Input: A young, straight pine or fir, or a clean top-log.
Labour: Felled whole, barked, knots dressed off.
Use: Scaffold-poles, light spars, ladder-stiles.
Property: Still one stick.

Hewn beam
Input: A large, reasonably straight log.
Labour: Scored and hewn square with axe and adze.
Use: Posts, ties, plates, hall and barn frames.
Property: One tree, squared.

Turning blanks
Input: Birch or beech, clean and even.
Labour: Cross-cut and split or sawn to a short block, then seasoned.
Use: Bowls, spoons, legs, handles.
Property: Short grain is acceptable.

Wet-work blanks
Input: Alder from river or fen.
Labour: Cut to length and kept from checking.
Use: Clogs, sluice-linings, cheap damp joinery.
Property: Stands wet. Not a structural timber.

Hub / pipe blanks
Input: Elm, the twisted, knot-free heart of a good log.
Labour: Selected and cross-cut.
Use: Wheel-hubs, pump-pipes.
Property: Interlocked grain. Hard to split.

Shaft / oar / handle blanks
Input: Straight-grained ash, one stick.
Labour: Selected and seasoned. Turning to the haft comes later.
Use: Axe and pollaxe shafts, spear-hafts, oars, tool-handles.
Property: Long spring. A board will not do.

Cooper’s staves
Input: Oak, split from the log rather than sawn when possible.
Labour: Split, dressed to a taper, seasoned.
Use: Casks, barrels, vats.
Property: Holds liquid when the grain runs right.

Knee / compass-timber blank
Input: An oak that grew a natural bend.
Labour: Found more than made.
Use: Ship-knees, frames.
Property: The strength is the grown curve.

Svart-Viðr / Svartwood
Dauðavík only. Input is the Forestry log of the named grade.
Bark off under wet-wrap (Fault-moss). Dry sawdust is a Hazard.
Water-saw preferred. T1 whip-saw is slower and sicker.
Not a super-material. Rot- and worm-hard. Fire-ugly.
Grade is geothermal proximity and mineral take, not a second species.

Rim / pale — 600 kg/m³. Hull cuts. A skin of strakes and deck may be this.
Mid — 720 kg/m³. Waterline and ordinary frames.
Heart / vent — 850 kg/m³. Keel, stem, stern, tiller, durable haft. Not a hull-lot.

Packing: wrapped stack
| Form | Rate | Lots |
|------|------|------|

| Heart hewn beam | 32 bc/kg | 100 kg → 266 sc 8 bc |
Do not charcoal any grade. Offcuts are foul fuel or wrapped waste.
A southern stall that has heart has a Jarn hull or a wreck. Rim strakes may be cargo.
