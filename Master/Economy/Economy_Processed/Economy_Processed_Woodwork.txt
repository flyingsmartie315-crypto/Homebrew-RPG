# Economy_Processed_Woodwork.txt
# Processed goods — Woodwork
# Law: Economy_Processed_Goods.txt (currency, bulk, packing)
# Making: Economy_Processing_Matrix.txt
# Input: Economy_Primary_Forestry.txt intact yield types
# Status: DRAFT — linked to Forestry species model
# Base unit: 1 kg, or 1 litre where noted. 1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc.

Woodwork
Timber sawn, split, hewn, or selected for use.
About a third to half of a log is lost to slab, bark, and sawdust.
The input is an intact unit from Economy_Primary_Forestry.txt
(or the bulk kg remainder when no intact unit was produced).
A piece too short, checked, or shaken for the named use
drops to mixed planks or to firewood.

Packed planks and beams: Bulk 500 kg/m³, loose-stacked.
Shingles: Bulk 350 kg/m³, bundled.

--------------------------------------------------
YIELD TYPE → WOODWORK FORM
--------------------------------------------------
The Forestry model produces intact outputs by species and DBH.
Those outputs feed the forms below.
If the tree yielded only bulk kg, only the lower forms
(mixed planks, firewood, rough boards) are available.

Structural log
  → Hewn beam, long plank stock, ship timber
  Primary input for posts, ties, plates, and hall frames.

Beam blank
  → Hewn beam or heavy plank
  Squared or sawn to section.

Stave blank
  → Cooper’s staves
  Split (preferred) or sawn from oak or white oak.
  Short or flawed blanks lose the cooperage premium.

Mast / spar
  → Spar / pole blank or finished mast
  Pine or fir. Clean, straight, minimal knots.
  Master-mast carries the premium already noted in Forestry.

Haft / handle blank
  → Shaft / oar / handle blanks
  Ash preferred. One stick, long spring.

Knee / compass
  → Knee / compass-timber blank
  Natural bend oak. Found more than made.

Turning blank
  → Turning blanks
  Birch or beech, short and even.

Wet-work blank
  → Wet-work blanks
  Alder. Stands damp.

Hub / pipe blank
  → Hub / pipe blanks
  Elm heart.

Bulk kg / mixed
  → Planks / boards (common), shingles, firewood
  No intact premium.

--------------------------------------------------
FORMS
--------------------------------------------------

Planks / Boards
Input: Structural log, beam blank, or bulk kg of sound wood.
Labour: Saw-pit or mill. Two men and a saw, or a water-saw. Then stacking to season.
Use: Floors, walls, hull-skin, doors, chests, common furniture.
Property: Even thickness. Short offcuts are not shafts.
Yield note: Best stock comes from structural log or beam blank.
            Bulk kg produces ordinary or short boards.

Roof shingles
Input: Straight-grained log or beam blank, usually pine or oak.
Labour: Split with froe and mallet, then dressed thin.
Use: Roofs, sometimes wall-hang.
Property: Thin, overlapping, sheds water.

Spar / pole blank
Input: Mast / spar yield (pine or fir) or clean top-log.
Labour: Felled whole, barked, knots dressed off.
Use: Scaffold-poles, light spars, ladder-stiles, masts.
Property: Still one stick.
Yield note: Direct from Forestry mast/spar column.
            Master-mast premium applies when the model marks it.

Hewn beam
Input: Structural log or beam blank.
Labour: Scored and hewn square with axe and adze.
Use: Posts, ties, plates, hall and barn frames.
Property: One tree, squared.
Yield note: Requires the intact structural or beam output.
            Cannot be made from bulk kg alone.

Turning blanks
Input: Turning blank yield (birch or beech) or short clean blocks.
Labour: Cross-cut and split or sawn to a short block, then seasoned.
Use: Bowls, spoons, legs, handles.
Property: Short grain is acceptable.

Wet-work blanks
Input: Alder intact or bulk from river or fen.
Labour: Cut to length and kept from checking.
Use: Clogs, sluice-linings, cheap damp joinery.
Property: Stands wet. Not a structural timber.

Hub / pipe blanks
Input: Elm hub/pipe yield or selected heart.
Labour: Selected and cross-cut.
Use: Wheel-hubs, pump-pipes.
Property: Interlocked grain. Hard to split.

Shaft / oar / handle blanks
Input: Haft / handle blank (ash preferred).
Labour: Selected and seasoned. Turning to the haft comes later.
Use: Axe and pollaxe shafts, spear-hafts, oars, tool-handles.
Property: Long spring. A board will not do.
Yield note: One stick from the Forestry haft column.
            A sawn board is not a substitute.

Cooper’s staves
Input: Stave blank (oak or white oak), split from the log when possible.
Labour: Split, dressed to a taper, seasoned.
Use: Casks, barrels, vats.
Property: Holds liquid when the grain runs right.
Yield note: Direct from Forestry stave column.
            White oak preferred for tight work.
            Short or checked blanks drop to ordinary oak price.

Knee / compass-timber blank
Input: Knee / compass yield (natural bend oak).
Labour: Found more than made.
Use: Ship-knees, frames.
Property: The strength is the grown curve.
Yield note: Rare. Only when the Forestry model or a specific tree produces it.

--------------------------------------------------
PRICES BY SPECIES (DRAFT LINK)
--------------------------------------------------
Base prices remain those in the species tickets of
Economy_Primary_Forestry.txt, adjusted for:
- loss (one-third to one-half to slab, bark, sawdust)
- labour of the form
- intact premium when the input was a named yield type

Mixed / Pine (ordinary plank or spar from bulk or light spar)
Bulk: 500 kg/m³ (planks, beams, spars) / 350 kg/m³ (shingles)
Packing: loose pile / bundle
Typical uplift: 2.5–4× primary kg after loss and labour.

Oak structural / beam / stave
Higher uplift when the input is an intact structural log,
beam blank, or stave blank.
Cooper’s staves from white oak carry an additional premium.

Ash haft
Premium for true one-stick haft blanks.
Sawn substitutes are priced as ordinary ash board.

Master-mast
Uses the 1.5× already noted on the Pine / Fir ticket
plus spar labour.

--------------------------------------------------
RULES
--------------------------------------------------
- Input authority is Economy_Primary_Forestry.txt.
- If the species model did not produce the required intact yield,
  the higher form cannot be made. Drop to the next lower form
  or to bulk.
- Loss of one-third to one-half is already assumed in the uplift.
- Seasoning time is wait, not labour days (same rule as other processed goods).
- Siege engines, architecture, and ship work that specify
  “structural timber”, “staves”, or “mast” must be costed
  from the matching yield type, not from mixed kg.
