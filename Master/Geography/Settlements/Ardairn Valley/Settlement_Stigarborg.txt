# Stígarborg

Type
Town

Location
Local Region: Ardairn Valley (common: Westmark)
Geographic Region: Ebon Peninsula
Position: Dam-castle at the head of Daelsteig Valley. 30 miles north-east of Osricford as the crow flies. Between Arnmont (west) and the Dalhamar foothills (east).

Overview
The keep sits on the dam. That is the point.
Melt off Bergon Massif above — not the Keldau as such — plus the moisture-trap of the Horns is stopped here, held as Laufirn, and sent down the Flōdweg — a 45-mile aqueduct — into Osricford. The valley below the wall is 175 square miles. The castle is inhabited because if this stone fails, the capital drinks the Värdå and the Värdå is not a well.
Names locked. Vale: Daelsteig (Dæl-Steig). Mere: Laufirn (hlūtr/lauter + firn). Keep: Stígarborg.

Control
Crown / Zonarch water-wrights and a resident keep-lord. More vital than pretty. Not a villa. Not Laedburg’s new money. An old watch that became a dam.

Population
Working figure: 400–600 in the keep and dam-works.
The 175-square-mile vale below holds scattered hamlets that are not this file.

Economy
Water. Toll on the Flōdweg is political, not a market.
Some timber and hunt from the vale. The keep does not live off grain.

Defences / Access
The dam is the wall. The keep is the house on the wall. Destroy the dam and you murder a city; that is the defence and the hostage.
Access: vale-road 30 miles toward Osricford; the Flōdweg itself (service path); tracks into Dalhamar and onto Arnmont.

Linked Features
- Daelsteig — 175 sq mi vale below the dam
- Keldau — NE head-tributary of the Herrenau. 16 miles SE to the merge that forms Herrenau
- Laufirn — artificial reservoir behind the dam
- Flōdweg — 45-mile aqueduct to Osricford
- Herrenau — formed at the merge below this vale
- Osricford — Settlement_Osricford.txt
- Bergon Massif — the melt that fills Laufirn
- Arnmont (west) — live north veins use this vale as the kinder road
- Dalhamar foothills (east)

Notes
Aqueduct length is the route, 45 miles. Crow-flight to the city is 30. Same rule as Värdå path-miles.
Second Herrenau head-stream is not named.
Keldau is the vale’s running water to the Herrenau merge. The mere’s wealth is Bergon ice coming down off the centre of the range.
Daelsteig / the Keldau road is also the transit for Arnmont’s northern mines. The vale-slope is a better road than the crags. Ore comes this way when it does not want the hollow-mountain tracks.
16th-century masonry can hold this. Do not make it a steel dam. Do not make the Flōdweg a pipe you cannot see.
Campaign hooks: a crack in winter ice; a man who can shut the city off; melt-year the mere climbs the wall.
