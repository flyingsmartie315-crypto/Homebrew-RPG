# Hornby

Type
Village

Location
Local Region: Kannavald Deepwood
Geographic Region: Ebon Peninsula
Position: On the Hornbeck above its mouth into Lake Kolvatn; bridge on the working road up from the lake

Overview
A lake-road village on the dark Hornbeck under the Westwood slopes. Water and timber set the pace: floats and small craft from the Kolvatn, wood from the west-bank forest, and the bridge that ends the south-shore haul from the fen country. Hall, store, and dock-yard stand fortified in the centre; no circuit wall. Quieter than Kolby, less hard than Askgruv — a working hinge between the lake and the Westwood.
Spurhall, the stone keep on the burn-spur, is the mark of the place from the water and the road.

Control
[Local Kannavald authority or Kolby’s reach — lock house later]

Population
About 350–600
Boatmen, timber-hands, a few farm and shore folk. Swells when the lake is open and the floats are running.

Economy
Timber and lake-trade. Wood out of the Westwood and Hornbeck slopes; charcoal where the pits pay. Small craft and barge-work on the lower Hornbeck and the Kolvatn. Fishing and shore-take. Grain and goods mostly by water from Kolby or the south-shore road. Not a mine-town.

Defences / Access
No circuit wall. Hall, store, and river-dock in Kannavald main-building style.
**Spurhall** — a prominent stone keep on a raised spur above the Hornbeck — is the strong point of the village. It commands the bridge and the water. The rest of the centre sits as fortified homesteads under its eye.
The bridge on the Hornbeck is the land gate; the mouth is the water gate.

To Kolby-on-Vatn (water)
- Down the Hornbeck about 14 miles to the lake mouth.
- Across Kolvatn about 28 miles east to the tip of the Kolmire, or the shorter working line north-east to Kolby’s water 22 miles.
- Kolby sits on the north-west of the lake from the fen-tip route used by Askgruv traffic.

To Kolby-on-Vatn (road)
- Down-Hornbeck road about 13 miles to the mouth.
- About 40 miles east along the southern edge of Lake Kolvatn toward the Askflow / Kolmire tip, then the north-of-fen roads to Kolby — or the living lake-shore tracks as held locally.

To Askgruv (water)
- Hornbeck to the lake, east across Kolvatn to the tip of the Kolmire (~28 miles from mouth to fen-tip on the west–east line), then up the Askflow about 34 miles to Askgruv.

To Burnford (road)
- South-shore way toward the Askflow: reverse of Burnford’s Hornby road — to the Askflow bridge, north to the Rookburn join, then up the east-bank Rookburn road to Burnford.

To Skaardhold
- South-east about 60 miles on the winding road through dense firs.
- Westwood Peak towers to the west; Stetbjorg Height to the east.
- The road runs the high wood toward Deepskaard Col; Skaardhold is the Kannavald watch on that pass.

To Oldfort Kilnstead
- North-east about 15 miles down the northern side of the Hornbeck to its mouth into Kolvatn.
- Another 12 miles up the flat road to the crossroads.
- From the crossroads, west into the woods on the shadowed covered trail, toward the rising smoke — about 13 miles into Oldfort Kilnstead.

To The Twins of Twaford
- From the same crossroads, 13 miles north to the head of the Kolvatn outflow — the Sund — and the towering Twins of Twaford.
- Kolby-on-Vatn is visible from the road on the western side of the Kolvatn.

Linked Features
- Hornbeck
- Westwood Heights
- Lake Kolvatn
- Underwood Shores (west, beyond the ridge)

Notes
Hornby is the Westwood’s lake door. The Hornbeck is darker, forest-fed water; floats and small craft matter more than deep barge-draught until the open lake.
Campaign hooks: timber boom held at the mouth, dispute with Kolby over lake tolls, something wrong washing down from the Westwood, a missing boat on the crossing to the fen-tip, Burnford drivers and Hornby hands in a fight over who works the south shore.
