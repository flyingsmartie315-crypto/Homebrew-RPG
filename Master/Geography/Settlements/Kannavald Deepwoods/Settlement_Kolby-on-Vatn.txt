# Kolby-on-Vatn

Type
Town

Location
Local Region: Kannavald Deepwood
Geographic Region: Ebon Peninsula
Position: North-west shore of Lake Kolvatn; chief town of the Deepwood lake ring

Overview
The working heart of Kannavald. Walls on the land side, docks on the water, and the smell of timber, pitch, and forge-smoke. Barges and river-craft pack the quays; warehouses hold sawn wood and charcoal; yards turn out boards and light hulls for the Sund and the lake. Ore and bog-iron feed the forges. Kolby does not romanticise the fen or the upper pits — it burns, cuts, smelts, and ships. On clear days the Sunkern Burg is a dark mark across the water and a reminder of what the lake country already lost.

Control
[Chief local power of the lake ring — lock house later]
Kolby’s law reaches the bridges and the water-tolls more readily than the deep wood.

Population
About 4,000–6,000
Boatmen, sawyers, smiths, shipwrights, fishers, labourers, and the households that feed them. Swells in open-water seasons; tight in Longnight.

Economy
Timber hub. Docks and warehouses full of wood; workshops and stacks of processed lumber bound down the Sund or across the lake.
Charcoal from Oldfort Kilnstead feeds the forges that keep the tools and fittings of this trade alive.
Metal: bog-iron from parties into the Kolmire, and ore from Askgruv, smelted and worked here. Work gangs — often convict labour under local edict — go by road or across Kolvatn into the swamps for bog-iron; the work is guarded and hated.
Peat cut from the Kolmire under local edict to spare the woods for timber rather than hearth-fuel.
Shipwrights skilled in light, nimble river-craft and in servicing the region’s barge fleet.
Fishing docks help feed the town.
Agriculture on the outskirts to the forest edge: hardier foodstuffs only. Kolby is not a grain capital.

Defences / Access
Walled on the land approaches. Water-gate and docks on Kolvatn. Hall, warehouses, and major yards in Kannavald main-building style — stone and brick ground, heavy timber, iron-bound doors — built to work and to hold.
Chief water-gate of the living lake country; the Twins hold the Sund beyond.

To The Twaford Twins (road)
- About 7 miles east along the northern shore of Kolvatn to the eastern side of the Sund and the Twins.

To Oldfort Kilnstead
- About 9 miles south-west by barge to the crossroads water; then the covered trail about 13 miles west into Oldfort (smoke marks the kilns).
(Charcoal comes the other way on this line.)

To Burnford (road)
- About 30 miles east on the undulating trail south of the open lake, Kolmire a couple of miles south.
- At about 15 miles from Burnford’s side: stone pillar and south sign into the fen (Kolholm road — broken after ~2 miles).
- Burnford is the Rookburn bridge village.

To Askgruv (water)
- Across Kolvatn about 24 miles to the tip of the Kolmire / Askflow mouth.
- Up the Askflow about 34 miles to Askgruv (Rookburn join at ~29 miles from Askgruv; Sunkern Burg west of that water).

To Hornby (water)
- Across Kolvatn toward the Hornbeck mouth (via fen-tip about 28 miles from tip to Hornbeck mouth on the Askgruv–Hornby line, or the direct working crossing when measured).
- Up the Hornbeck about 14 miles to Hornby.

To Kolholm (hazard)
- First fen about 6 miles north-west of town.
- Deeper swamp and ruins about 22 miles from Kolby.
- Visible across the water on clear days. No safe dock; salvage is unofficial and costly.

Linked Features
- Lake Kolvatn
- The Sund
- Kolmire Fens
- Askflow / Rookburn / Hornbeck (lake mouths)
- Kolholm / Sunkern Burg

Notes
Kolby is the market the pits, kilns, and bridges answer to. Convict bog-iron gangs and peat edicts are law here, not custom alone.
Campaign hooks: timber fleet held for toll, a forge short of Oldfort charcoal, bog-iron gang broken in the fen, Askgruv ore late or stolen, shipwrights pressed for a war-boom on the Sund, clear-day sight of the Burg and a dare that goes too far.
