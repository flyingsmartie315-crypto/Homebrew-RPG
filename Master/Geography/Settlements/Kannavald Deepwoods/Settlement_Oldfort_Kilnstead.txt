# Oldfort Kilnstead

Type
Village

Location
Local Region: Kannavald Deepwood
Geographic Region: Ebon Peninsula
Position: Deep wood west of Lake Kolvatn; old hillfort and kiln-yards; smoke marks the place from the lake road

Overview
A charcoal village on an old fort. The walls are ruin and memory; the work is fire, wood, and pack. Kilns sit in the clearing; the trail in is shadowed and covered. Hall and store are fortified homesteads in the Kannavald style. No circuit wall that still keeps a siege — only the old ditch and the smoke.

Control
[Local Kannavald authority or Kolby’s reach — lock house later]

Population
About 250–450
Colliers, cutters, a few pack-hands. Thin in Highsun when the woods are dry and watched; busier when the pits are drawn for the lake markets.

Economy
Charcoal. Wood from the deep stands around the old fort; pits and clamps in the yards. Charcoal moves to the crossroads and then by barge to Kolby, or by trail toward the Hornbeck and Twaford traffic. Not a farm village. Grain and tools come in from the lake road.

Defences / Access
No living circuit wall. Old hillfort ditch and bank; hall and charcoal-store in Kannavald main-building style.
The trail is the gate. Smoke is the signpost.

To Hornby
- About 13 miles east on the shadowed covered trail to the crossroads.
- From the crossroads, 12 miles down the flat road to the Hornbeck mouth, then about 15 miles up the northern side of the Hornbeck to Hornby.
(Reverse of Hornby’s road to Oldfort.)

To The Twins of Twaford
- About 13 miles east to the crossroads.
- From the crossroads, 13 miles north to the head of the Kolvatn outflow — the Sund — and the Twins of Twaford.
- Kolby-on-Vatn is visible from that road on the western side of the lake.

To Kolby-on-Vatn
- Trail to the crossroads (~13 miles).
- Charcoal loaded to barge at the Kolvatn; about 9 miles north-east by water to Kolby-on-Vatn.

Linked Features
- Lake Kolvatn
- Hornbeck (south of the crossroads)
- The Sund / Twaford (north)
- Westwood Heights (south and west, the wider wood)

Notes
Oldfort is pit and smoke. The fort is older than the kilns; diggers sometimes hit worked stone and stop.
Campaign hooks: a clamp fire that runs into the wood, charcoal short for Kolby’s smiths, something found under the old bank, the covered trail watched by the wrong eyes, a barge lost on the short water to Kolby.
