# Ebon Peninsula

Visual Reference
maps/Map_Geographic_Region_Ebon_Peninsula.jpeg

Overview
A west-facing peninsula of temperate marks, highland marches, and one remaining deepwood. The west opens to productive low country and a major river-mouth. The north and northeast rise into the Marchwall Range, which sits on the Rivenstrand Walls — sea-cliffs that cut the peninsula off from easy northern landfall. Centre-east the ground lifts through Highmarch toward the inactive Ebon Spire and the Svarthorn Massif. The east wall is the forested Rhennod Mountains, western lip of the Appolasian Plateau. The south is river country: Rhonmere at the Rhennod foot, the Ader running out as the live border with Coritania.

Location & Borders
Northwestern continent. West of the Appolasian Plateau. North of the Coritanian Heartlands. Open sea to the west and along the Rivenstrand.

Shared / Border Local Regions
- Rhennod Mountains — eastern wall of the peninsula; western lip of the Appolasian Plateau. One file. Listed here as shared border, not as a primary peninsula region.
- Ader line / Rhonmere country — live southern border with Coritania. The river is the division in the modern era. South-bank halls are Coritanian; north-bank works (Brückenburg and the waystations) sit on the peninsula side.

Local Regions
- Westmark — open western heartland. Iron, grain, livestock, sea trade. Capital port at the mouth of the Värdå. Seat of Isernvolk power and of first-landing memory.
- Ebormark — southern / vale country under the Spire massif. Eyrenal and the Eyrenhost. South face of Ebon Spire / Svarthorn.
- Kannavald Deepwood — remaining dense forest. Northern face of Ebon Spire / Svarthorn. Westwood Heights. Kannavi majority in the woods; Isern law on lake-shore and pits.
- Highmarch — elevated middle country between the eastern flanks of Ebon Spire / Svarthorn and the rising Marchwall Range. Passes, watch, highland herd.
- Rhennod Marches — working name for the eastern hill country: rolling ground between the heartland river and the Rhennod wall, Ader on its south. Confirm or replace when the pink / magenta local-region file is locked.

Notable Features
- Ebon Spire — tallest peak on Virath. Inactive shield volcano. Black rock. Shared faces: Kannavald (north), Ebormark (south), Highmarch (east). Full write-up in Notes.
- Svarthorn Massif — adjacent high massif. Lower than the Spire. Same shared-face rule.
- Marchwall Range — long northeastern range. Sits atop the Rivenstrand Walls.
- Rivenstrand Walls — northern / northeastern sea-cliffs.
- Rhennod Mountains — east wall. Highest peak: Rhön / Mount Rhön.
- Rhônweill — lake at the Rhennod foot. Local Frankish. Wider Coritania: le Rhônel. Syllvani: Llyn Rhön. Tale: Giant’s Pool. Outflow: the Ader.
- The Ader — southern border river with Coritania.
- Värdå — great west-flowing river. Old Färdå. Mouth at Osricford / Ocsgaard. Glacial melt; sea moisture only a partial trap. Sea to Holtau ~377 path-miles on the listed joins (meander length of the Värdå, not a straight shot from the coast).
- Herrenau — first join, 20 miles up. Isernhorns south-shelf. Villa-country of the capital.
- Íså — tributary of the Värdå in Westmark.
- The Sund — tributary of the Värdå in Westmark.
- Spireflow — tributary of the Värdå in Highmarch.
- Dreiau — three strands from boreal forest at the Marchwall base. Joins the Värdå at 305. Then 72 miles to Holtau.
- Erzau — joins the Appolasian Plateau river; that trunk enters Holtau on the east. Not the Spire NE burn. Not a Värdå join.
- Brocken Peak — with Svarthorn; the Glasau runs off it to the Värdå.
- Hornlein — Little Horn water to the Värdå in Westmark (183 from sea). Not the Little Massif until so locked.
- Ebonclock — working range-name for the mountains attached to the Ebon Spire (Kannavald mountains), excluding Lowstacks, Westwood Peaks, Westwood Heights, Watchers Peak, and Mistcap Crags.
- Holtau — 187 square miles on the upper Värdå. Second hub of the peninsula. Lake name locked. Takes Ostglas from the south; from the east, Avenhir after Erzau has joined it.
- Die Schulter — named mass of Ebormark (the Shoulder). Local_Region_Ebormark when written.
- Zackenkar — working name. Bowl-peak south-east of Die Schulter. Holds glacial ice. Seasonal melt swells the Zackenbeck.
  A cirque, not a caldera. Other name-options: Eiskar, Schulterkar, Kesselhorn.
- Kannavald Deepwood — last large canopy of a once-forested peninsula.
- Western sea-board — iron, grain, livestock, and sea trade concentrated in Westmark.


Ebon Spire (locked)
Tallest peak on Virath. Peer in bulk to an Olympus-class shield: a black mountain that owns the horizon, not a needle.
Inactive volcano. Black rock — basalt and scorched glass. No live fire. No plume. The Ebon name is the colour and the memory of fire, not a present furnace.
A shield this high on an Earth-like gravity world must be vast in footprint. The “Spire” is what it reads as from the marks. Close-to, it is a black country of its own: long rising ramps, old lava benches, a dead caldera-crown.
Shared landform. Detailed face-scores live in the local files when written:
- North — Kannavald Deepwood
- South — Ebormark (Eyrenal / Eyrenhost country; Die Schulter and Zackenkar sit on this mass, not above the Spire)
- East — Highmarch approaches
Do not give the Spire to one local region. Svarthorn is the neighbouring massif and is lower.
The cave-nets under the peninsula include old tubes in this rock. They are fossil. This cone does not feed Dauðavík’s live heat.
Climb: men will try. They have tried for centuries in its shadow.
Olympus-class here is bulk and blackness — a shield-country — not a 22-mile needle in thin air. Virath’s air is Earth-like at the marks. A true Olympus Mons summit (twenty kilometres and change) sits above any lung. No 16th-century host carries a breath that high.
What is climbable: the ramps, the benches, a false crown. Death-zone stone. Most turn back or stay. A few names enter the songs. The dead caldera-crown remains a claim, not a road.
Do not give bottled air to make the summit ordinary.


Notes
Travel is easy on the Westmark floors and river, harder in Highmarch, and slow in Kannavald. The Marchwall / Rivenstrand side is a wall more than a road. The Rhennod is the Plateau’s lip, not a peninsula range to be crossed lightly.
The peninsula was tamed from near-continuous forest after the Isern landing. Kannavald is what the taming did not finish.
Culture on this geography is Isernvolk marks with a Kannavi wood-minority (majority only in the Deepwood). East of the Rhennod the modern division is the Appolasian Plateau. South of the Ader is Coritania.

A Geographic Region does not take a Biome line or Environmental Scores.
It inherits the biomes of its Local Regions.
