# Economy_Primary_Goods.txt
# Primary Goods – Base Unit: 1 kg (or single natural item)

HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Prices shown are base market values and are subject to ±50% (or greater) fluctuation by region, season, supply, and demand.
Base unit is 1 kg wherever practical. Larger lots are provided for convenience.

Bulk is packed density for the good in its listed packing.
A vehicle is full at whichever comes first: weight or volume.
Standard cart: 800 kg or 2.5 m³
Heavy cart: 1,500 kg or 4 m³
River timber barge: 12,000 kg or 30 m³
A “cart load” is one standard cart.
A “heavy-cart load” is one heavy cart.
A “barge-load” is one river timber barge.

Packing
The Bulk figure already includes the listed packing.
Use the modifiers below only when the good is packed differently from its listed method.

Sacks
+2% weight. +5% volume.
Default for grain, salt, charcoal, dried meat, dried fish.

Baskets
+15–25% volume.
Default for eggs, berries, greens, moss, and anything that must breathe or not be crushed.

Casks / Crates
+8–12% weight.
Volume is the cask’s outside measure.
Default for butter, tallow, pitch, milk, and breakable dry goods.

Loose pile
No extra penalty.
Default for ore, coal, stone, bricks, clay, peat, and logs.

These goods form the economic foundation of Virath.

# Economy_Livestock.txt
# Livestock, Beasts of Burden, War Mounts, Game & Sea-Take

HEADER NOTE
All prices use the universal currency (1 gc = 20 sc = 240 bc).
Prices shown are base market values for healthy adult animals and are subject to significant regional and seasonal fluctuation (±50% or more).
Livestock requires ongoing space, feed, water, and care. Neglect rapidly reduces value or kills the animal.

Animals are listed with:
- Purchase price (healthy adult)
- Typical upkeep
- Space / housing needs
- Primary uses
- Slaughter yields, named as the Primary Goods meat types

Slaughter turns a live beast into the goods in Economy_Primary_Goods.txt.
Yields are working averages. Age, breed, and season move them.
Meat weights are as carcase meat, priced raw or preserved from that file.
Wool is taken in life and is not a slaughter yield.

How to price a kill
Add the parts at the Primary Goods rates.
Do not add a separate “carcase” price on top.
The live beast is worth more than this stack only while it still works, breeds, or gives milk and wool.

Live prices below are the current listed rates and do not yet match the slaughter stack. Hide and tallow still need the same treatment meat received.

# World_Calendar.txt
# The Common Reckoning of Virath

HEADER NOTE
The year is the same length and shape as a real terrestrial year.
Twelve months. Four seasons. The same number of days.
Only the names differ.
This is the trader’s and scholar’s calendar. Local cultures keep their own feast-names on top of it.

The natural moon is Lorn.
The False Moon is not used for reckoning.

---

THE YEAR

365 days in a common year.
366 in a leap year, every fourth year.
Weeks of 7 days, if a culture bothers to count them that way.

---

SEASONS

Thaw
The spring. Snow-melt, first plough, lambing.
Months: Budmoon, Lambmoon, Greenleaf

Highsun
The summer. Long days, hay, dry roads.
Months: Flowering, Highsun, Aftersun

Harvest
The autumn. Cutting, pressing, slaughter for winter.
Months: Reaping, Fallwine, Leafbare

Longnight
The winter. Short days, store-eating, closed high passes.
Months: Frostfall, Deepcold, Icewane

SEASON BOUNDS

HOW TO SCORE
--------------------------------------------------
1. Give every land-cover feature a Harvest score in all six measures.
2. Name one seasonal template for the region (or a different template on a feature that needs it).
3. When a season is dated, shift each feature’s Temperature, Rainfall, and Flora/Fauna by that template (+1 / 0 / −1).
4. Average the land-cover features. Round to the nearest whole number. Clamp 0–5.
5. Print that average as the region’s Environmental Scores.

A lake, a forest, and a mountain each keep their own character.
The region line is only the summary.
Kannavald would then list Ebon Spire, Kolvatn, the Deepwood, and so on as absolute scores, not ± off a basin average. The six numbers at the top become the mean of those.

This separation keeps environmental data stable while allowing the political map to shift through conquest, diplomacy, rebellion, or collapse.

# Environmental Scoring System for Geographic Regions

These scores provide a consistent way to describe the environment of any land or oceanic region on Virath.
They are used both for general regional description and for comparison against creature HSI values in the Bestiary.

Every scored place uses seven measures, written on one line:

Temperature: X | Rainfall: X | Human Density: X | Flora: X | Fauna: X | Topography: X | Hydrology: X

--------------------------------------------------
LAND REGIONS – Environmental Scores (0–5)
--------------------------------------------------

Temperature (heat / cold)
0 – Arctic / freezing
1 – Sub-arctic / cold
2 – Cool temperate
3 – Mild temperate
4 – Warm subtropical
5 – Hot (sub-Saharan equivalent)

Rainfall (precipitation / humidity)
0 – Arid desert
1 – Dry / sparse
2 – Semi-arid
3 – Temperate / balanced
4 – Humid / wet
5 – Rainforest / torrential

Human Density (settlement level)
0 – Uninhabited wilderness
1 – Sparse / isolated
2 – Low / scattered
3 – Moderate (villages)
4 – High (towns)
5 – Dense (cities)

Flora (plant cover)
0 – Barren / lifeless
1 – Limited / scarce
2 – Sparse / occasional
3 – Moderate / common
4 – Abundant / thriving
5 – Teeming / overrun

Fauna (animal life)
0 – Barren / lifeless
1 – Limited / scarce
2 – Sparse / occasional
3 – Moderate / common
4 – Abundant / thriving
5 – Teeming / overrun

Topography (elevation / steepness)
0 – Flat plains
1 – Gentle slopes
2 – Undulating hills
3 – Rugged terrain
4 – Steep cliffs
5 – Sheer mountains

Hydrology (water features)
0 – None / parched
1 – Sparse trickles
2 – Limited streams
3 – Moderate rivers
4 – Abundant lakes
5 – Flooded / swamps

--------------------------------------------------
OCEANIC REGIONS – Environmental Scores (0–5)
--------------------------------------------------

When scoring oceanic regions, the same seven categories are used with adapted meanings.

Temperature (water temperature)
0 – Polar / permanently frozen surface or near-freezing
1 – Freezing cold
2 – Cold currents
3 – Temperate seas
4 – Warm waters
5 – Tropical hot

Rainfall (storm frequency / humidity over water)
0 – Extremely dry air / almost no precipitation
1 – Minimal storms
2 – Light fog / occasional rain
3 – Moderate rains
4 – Frequent storms
5 – Constant high humidity / monsoon conditions

Human Density (maritime activity)
0 – No human presence (unexplored or avoided waters)
1 – Isolated routes
2 – Sparse traffic
3 – Moderate trade
4 – Busy shipping lanes
5 – Crowded ports / heavy traffic

Flora (marine plant and reef cover)
0 – Barren / sterile
1 – Limited / scarce
2 – Sparse weed or poor reef
3 – Moderate growth
4 – Abundant kelp / rich reef
5 – Teeming / overrun

Fauna (marine animal life)
0 – Essentially lifeless
1 – Barren depths
2 – Sparse fish
3 – Moderate schools
4 – Abundant life
5 – Teeming ecosystems

Topography (water depth)
0 – Intertidal / exposed flats at low tide
1 – Shallow waters
2 – Coastal shelf
3 – Moderate depths
4 – Deep sea
5 – Abyssal depths / trenches

Hydrology (sea state / surface conditions)
0 – Dead calm / mirror-like
1 – Calm seas
2 – Mild swells
3 – Moderate waves
4 – Rough currents
5 – Extreme storms / violent seas

--------------------------------------------------
USAGE NOTES
--------------------------------------------------

- Scores are relative tools for consistency, not absolute scientific measurements.
- A region is normally described with all seven scores.
- Flora and Fauna are scored separately. A closed canopy can sit at Flora 4 with Fauna 2. A steppe can sit at Flora 2 with Fauna 4.
- When comparing a region to a creature’s HSI, use the method defined in Bestiary_Rules.txt. Match Flora and Fauna on their own axes.
- Extreme scores (0 or 5) should be used deliberately; most settled regions will cluster in the middle ranges.
- Unveiling phenomena, megastructures, or local anomalies can create exceptions that override normal environmental expectations.

--------------------------------------------------
GEOGRAPHY HIERARCHY & PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------------------

Virath’s geography is organised in three tiers:

1. Geographic Region
   - Large-scale physical area roughly comparable to Western Europe in size.
   - Defined purely by natural geography, climate patterns, and major landforms.
   - Political and cultural ownership is not inherent to the region.

2. Local Region
   - Smaller physical subdivision within a Geographic Region.
   - Each Local Region carries its own full set of Environmental Scores.
   - Still defined by physical characteristics rather than politics.

3. Settlement
   - Individual cities, towns, villages, fortresses, and other permanent human sites.
   - Political control, cultural identity, and faction ownership are recorded at this level.
   - Settlements link upward to the Local Region (and therefore Geographic Region) they physically occupy.

Core Principle
Geographic and Local Regions are physical realities. They do not change ownership.
Political control is fluid and is determined by who holds the settlements within them.
As a result, any Geographic or Local Region may be wholly controlled, partially controlled, or actively contested by multiple factions at the same time.

This separation keeps environmental data stable while allowing the political map to shift through conquest, diplomacy, rebellion, or collapse.

--------------------------------------------------
HOW TO SCORE
--------------------------------------------------

1. Give every land-cover feature a Harvest score in all seven measures.
2. Name one seasonal template for the region (or a different template on a feature that needs it).
3. When a season is dated, shift each feature’s Temperature, Rainfall, Flora, and Fauna by that template (+1 / 0 / −1).
4. Average the land-cover features. Round to the nearest whole number. Clamp 0–5.
5. Print that average as the region’s Environmental Scores.

A lake, a forest, and a mountain each keep their own character.
The region line is only the summary.

--------------------------------------------------
HEIGHT AND PLACE
--------------------------------------------------
Height is a label for the Local Region as a whole.
Place is a clause in each feature’s description.
Neither is a 0–5 score. Neither is averaged. Neither is used for HSI.

--------------------------------------------------
HEIGHT (region line)
--------------------------------------------------
One optional line. Name the region’s seat in the world, not the steepness of its ground.

highland basin
A high, enclosed or half-enclosed floor, ringed by higher ground.
Kannavald Deepwood.

high range
A mountain mass as the main body of the region, not a basin inside one.

highland
High country that is not a closed basin and not a single range.

plateau
A raised table. The floor is high and relatively even.

upland
Hills and high ground above the neighbouring low country, short of a true highland.

lowland
Broad country near the regional base level. Valleys, plains, wide river-floors.

coastal plain
Low, open land against the sea.

coastal highland
High ground that meets the sea. Cliffs, raised benches, seaward ranges.

rift / trench
A deep cut below the surrounding land. Use rarely.

Do not mark a basin floor as lowland if that floor still sits high in the world.

--------------------------------------------------
PLACE (feature clause)
--------------------------------------------------
Every Notable Feature description includes one of these.
It says where the feature sits relative to the land around it.

floor
The low ground of the region or of a hollow inside it.
Lakes, fens, valley bottoms, basin woods, river-courses.

bench
A step or shoulder: higher than the floor, lower than the rim or peak.
Terraces, mid-slopes, hanging woods, lesser coastal heights.

pass
A gap that lets travel through higher ground.
Cols, defiles, water-gates if the water is the way through.

rim
The enclosing edge of a basin, plateau, or highland.
The ring of heights, the lip of a crater, the upper forest around a lake.

peak
The high point. A summit, spire, or dominant height.
Use for the mountain itself, not for the range as a whole unless the range is treated as one feature.

shore
Where land meets standing water or the sea.
Lake-strand, fen-margin, coastal plain against the ocean.

--------------------------------------------------
HOW TO PICK
--------------------------------------------------
- One place-word per feature.
- The range is usually rim or peak. The lake is floor or shore. The col is pass.
- A river in a basin is floor. A working coast is shore.
- Point sites still take a place-word (the Still Stones sit on a floor).
- If two words fit, pick the feature’s job: Deepskaard is a pass, not a bench.

--------------------------------------------------
HOW TO SCORE
--------------------------------------------------

1. Give every land-cover feature a Harvest score in all seven measures.
2. Name one seasonal template for the region (or a different template on a feature that needs it).
3. When a season is dated, shift each feature’s Temperature, Rainfall, Flora, and Fauna by that template (+1 / 0 / −1).
4. Average the land-cover features. Round to the nearest whole number. Clamp 0–5.
5. Print that average as the region’s Environmental Scores.
6. Optionally add a Height line for the region as a whole. It is a label, not a score.

A lake, a forest, and a mountain each keep their own character.
The region line is only the summary.
Kannavald only needs one added line under the scores:

Height: highland basin

# Kannavald Deepwood

Overview
A mountain-ringed highland basin of dense boreal forest, black volcanic rock, and standing water. The canopy is heavy, the light is poor, and most travel follows the rivers that drain from Ebon Spire into Lake Kolvatn. Settlement clings to the water; beyond the lake-shore and the upper burns the Deepwood is empty, wet, and quietly wrong.

NOTES
--------------------------------------------------
Access
By the Sund, by Deepskaard Col, by the west coast, or over the mountains.

Goods
Timber, charcoal, hides, and ore move downriver to the lake.

Population
Land area: 29,391 square miles.
Permanent population: about 16,000 (range 12,000–22,000).
Overall density: roughly 0.5 people per square mile.
Almost all of that population sits on the water. River and lake corridors, a small fraction of the land, hold the town, villages, and working landings. A thin margin of forest is used for charcoal, trapping, and small mines. The rest of Kannavald — the great majority of the Deepwood — has no permanent settlement.

Working split
- Kolby-on-Vatn: 4,000–6,000
- Named villages and the Twaford crossing: 3,000–5,000 combined
- Scattered charcoal hamlets and isolated households: 5,000–7,000
- Remainder: seasonal camps, traplines, and mine-heads

The extra land beyond those corridors is empty forest. Distance between named places should feel long.

Hydrology (water features)
0 – None / parched
1 – Sparse trickles
2 – Limited streams
3 – Rivers / inlets / channels
4 – Lakes / major through-water
5 – Flooded / swamps / wet coast
