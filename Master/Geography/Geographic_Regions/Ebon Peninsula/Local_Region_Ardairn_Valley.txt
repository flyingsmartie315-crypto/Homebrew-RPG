# Local_Region_Ardairn_Valley
# Modern name: the Ardairn Valley. Common offhand: Westmark.

Overview
The open, productive west of the Ebon Peninsula. Iron, grain, livestock, and the sea. First-landing memory and the Isern crown sit at a two-mile river-mouth: Osricford on the north bank, Vardgaard / Little Oc on the south. Barge only. Most people live on the plough-and-stock floor. Worked woodland feeds the yards. The Isernhorns and the Rivenstag Alps are a rim. The Rivenstrand reach is gale-coast — watch-posts, not farms.

Biome: Temperate
Sub-biomes: River, Coast, Highland, Forest
The floor is temperate mark-country. Forest is the eleven worked stands. Highland is the Horns, the Rivenstag Alps, and the cliff-reach.

Isernhorns
Westmark mountain range. 6,603 square miles. Name locked. Not the Marchwall.
Umbrella: north trio, Bergon Massif, Tahrfall, Arnmont, Dalhamar foothills (2,315 sq mi), and Sætann Wall / the Shattered Seawall all sit under this name.
Second-tallest peak on the Ebon Peninsula (Ebon Spire is first, and tallest on Virath).
Inactive volcano. Distinctive silhouette: a pale head and two horns. From the Ardairn floor it reads as a crowned thing, not a black shield.
The head is the old plug / ice-crown: Īshoubit.
The horns are the flanking ridges of the broken rim: Westarīs (west), Āustarīs (east).
Lower southern shelf feeds the Herrenau.
Centre of the range: Bergon Massif (from *bergō). Cluster of higher peaks with glacial reserves between them. Not the north trio.
West face to the sea: Tahrfall (*tahrą + *fallaną). Common: the Weeping Walls. 54-mile sheer cliff-line. Bergon ice goes over the edge into the sea. Not the shallow Western Sea-Board.
Named heights (locked, metres above sea)
- Īshoubit: 9,845. North head. Second peak of the peninsula; only the Ebon Spire stands higher.
- Westarīs: 7,055
- Āustarīs: 6,897
- Bergon Massif high point: Mount Vaterberg, 6,123. “Father of the massif.”
Tahrfall wall
- Lowest sea-cliff: Tahskar, 1,250 m
- Tallest sea-cliff: Sanderstīg, 2,026 m. Alexander (mad, famous steeplejack) + *stīganą (to climb).
9,845 m is a death-zone stone. Men will try. Most will not stand on Īshoubit. The Weeping Walls are a climb of a different kind — Sanderstīg is a name earned, not a road.

Arnmont
From *īsarną (iron) + mont. Worn: Arnmont.
The hollow mountain — dual sense: mined-out veins and ancient vast cave-channels.
550 square miles. South of Tahrfall, over Osricford. High point 1,569 m.
Ancient dark volcanic rock. South faces: empty veins, long dug out. North faces: still worked (copper, leftover silver, iron).
Fossil lava-tubes and cave-net. Same under-stone family as the peninsula; not Dauðavík live heat.
Live north-face ore prefers the Keldau / Daelsteig road to the crag-tracks.
Not Īshoubit’s pale ice-cone. Part of the Isernhorns umbrella — the south dark body of the same range.

Eisenberg
Official name: Das Isern-Zentralrevier.

The Skatobrand
3,573 square miles. Locked.
The ragged, cold northern scarp of the Westmark peninsula. Bound by the biting northern sea along 410 miles of highly irregular coast, and the towering, sheer northern walls of the Isernhorns (Westarīs and Īshoubit) to the south. The entire reach sits permanently under the looming mountain-shadow, receiving minimal direct high-sun. Fetch, heavy squalls, and black volcanic shelves.
Directly west of the Galekyst Flats. 14-mile shared border running from the Westarīs to the Rivenstrand Walls.
Biome: Coast / Highland
Temperature: 2 | Rainfall: 4 | Human Density: 0–1 | Flora: 2 | Fauna: 2 | Topography: 4 | Hydrology: 4

Internal Features:
- The Dark-Tail: The narrow, westernmost peninsula arm stretching into the sea. Barren, windswept, and dominated by ancient, dormant volcanic peaks. The shoreline here fractures into jagged coastal skerries, black-sand inlets, and tide-pools choked with basalt debris. No farm country; sparse watch-posts only.
- The Iron-Skerries: A long tail of half-drowned granite and volcanic teeth breaking the northern current for nearly 12 miles off the tip of the Dark-Tail. Deadly to navigation without a local pilot.
- The Horn-Apron: The deep, sheltered eastern basin of the reach. Here, the sheer mountain walls of the Isernhorns curve northward, forming a massive geographic lap that blocks the worst of the arctic sea-gales. Nurtured by glacial meltwater run-off and protected from the shearing winds, the basin shelters dense, primeval stands of mountain spruce and boreal pine. The canopy is thick, damp, and heavy with deep moss turf.

Lesser / Scattered / Seasonal:
- Cliff-side watch-towers keeping eyes on the northern sea-lanes.
- Seasonal logging camps on the fringes of the Horn-Apron.
- Kelp-gathering huts along the black volcanic beaches.

NOTES
--------------------------------------------------
Access
Sea into the two-mile mouth. The Värdå inland. Roads on both banks; the banks do not join except by barge at the mouth.
Highmarch and the Spire lie inland and up. Kannavald is not this floor.
The Rivenstrand reach is entered by cliff-paths and is left the same way, if the wind allows.

Goods
Out: grain, stock, iron, worked metal, hides, hulls.
In: salt, southern wine and cloth, horses, long timber.
The north-bank drydocks eat woodland timber and throw ships.

Population
Mouth locked: 50,000 (Osricford + Vardgaard).
Region working total: ~975,000 (range 850,000–1,100,000). Not locked.

Working density
- Open floor 27,071 × ~32 / sq mi → ~870,000 (includes the mouth)
- Woodland 3,594 × ~22 / sq mi → ~80,000
- Horns + Rivenstag Alps + Rivenstrand → ~25,000

Working split
- Capital, two banks: 50,000 locked. Own orbit. Not one of the 16–20.
- Market / pit / river towns: 16–20. Typical 2,000–5,000. ~70,000.
  Each town is a hub. Villages of consequence attach to a town — mill-right, market-day, tax, and levy all run through that hub.
  Working load: about 5–7 attached villages per town (~90–110 villages across the mark, plus ~10–15 wood villages on the timber towns).
- Attached villages: typical 200–400. Named on the town file until a village needs its own Settlement_ file.
- Lesser hamlets and pit-rows: still ~720,000 open + ~70,000 wood. They attach even more loosely — a market-day and a reeve, not a charter.
- Horns / Massif / Rivenstrand: ~25,000. Posts. They answer a keep or a Zonarch, not a market town.

Castles / keeps (working)
- Major white keeps / Zonarch seats / river-gates / crown forts: 10–12, including the Osricford rise
- Lesser towers on the open floor: ~25 — often the same site as a hub-town, or the attached village that holds the tower
- Wood-edge towers: a few on the 1,362-mile stand
- Rivenstrand: watch-posts, not castles
- Mountain parcels: pass-watches

Settlement filing
Write Settlement_ files for the capital pair and for the 16–20 hub towns.
List attached villages on the hub file under Notes / Linked Features.
A village gets its own file when play sits there, or when it has a keep, a pit, or a fight worth a page.
Do not open a hundred village files to start the mark.

Naming
Modern name: the Ardairn Valley. Said (ard)(dern).
From *aiwin- (ever / lasting). Sharpened to Ard + dair; -n for the mouth.
Common offhand: Westmark.
Early canopy-names still sit under it: Scatodal, Untarscato, Ēwald, Langwald, Thakscato, Hulisterwald, Deop-untar-Eben.
Do not use Ebonmark.
Clash to watch: Ardairn sits near Ader / Adernvolk in the ear. Different thing. Different river.
