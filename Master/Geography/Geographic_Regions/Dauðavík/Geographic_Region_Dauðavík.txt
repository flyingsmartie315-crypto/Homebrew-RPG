Overview
A large polar island in the high north, about 1,400 miles east–west by 600 miles north–south, irregular, roughly 420,000 square miles. Most of the floor is tundra, ice-waste, and bare volcanic rock. Live ground is not the white interior. It is the western inland sea, the north-wall volcanoes, and the vein-drainage that runs off that wall into the central basin. Climate is long night, low sun, and hard wind, broken locally by geothermal faults. From the open sea the island reads as a killing gulf. From inside the western bight it is dead-rock walls that take the gale, and killing-hot water under selected fields.

Location & Borders
Isolated polar island north of the Ebon Latitude Line. About 2,400 miles north of the Isern crossing-track that made Osricford. No land border with any other Geographic Region. All approaches are polar sea.

Shared / Border Local Regions
None. The island does not share a land-barrier Local Region with another Geographic Region. The border is water.

Local Regions
Working names only. No Local_Region file locked yet. Geographic order west to east.

- The Bight — western inland sea, skerries, inner channels, and the rock walls that kill the gale. First putting-ashore country.
- West Wall — outer west mountains and fjord-coast.
- North Wall — the high volcanic wall and the dominant north-central cone. Source of the south-running veins.
- Vein Country — fault-drainage from the North Wall into the central basin. Live hydrothermal ground. Svart-Viðr ribbons belong here, not as blanket canopy.
- Inner Basin — central sea or great lake with arms and islands. Open-to-bight or closed melt-basin is not yet locked.
- South Shore Range — short SW–NE hills on the south lip of the Inner Basin.
- East Limb — long, poorer taper. Fewer veins. Hungrier floor.

Notable Features
- Dauðavík / the Dead-Bight / the Killing Gulf — the western inland sea that names the island. Grave from the outer fetch. Shelter inside the channels.
- North Wall volcanoes — polar volcanic arc. Dominant cone on the north-central rim.
- Vein-drainage — many parallel south-flowing watercourses off the North Wall. The map-hook for fault-ribbons.
- Svart-Viðr / Svartwood — Dauðavík only. Dark conifer in ribbons on live faults. See Flora_Polar.txt. Not a blanket forest.
- Inner Basin — large central water. Salt-arm vs closed lake still open.
- East Limb — low polar floor and scattered coastal hills.
- Hydrothermal trenches and fumarole ground — under selected vein-fields. Soft soil in a hard climate. Thin crust kills.
- Polar sea — pack, current, and the road Jarn hulls use when they leave the bight.

Notes
Travel on the white interior is slow and empty. Travel that matters is along the bight, the inner channels, and the vein-ribbons.
Svart-Viðr follows hot ground. Do not draw it across the open tundra.
An entire hull of Svart-Viðr is too heavy. The wood is a strategic member, not a landscape of black ships.
Working size and first-pass map aspect are close, not surveyed. Confirm the scale bar before settlements.
