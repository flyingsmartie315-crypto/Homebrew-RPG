# Siege_Weapons.txt
# Crew-served engines. Operating span exceeds a normal Turn.
# Status: DRAFT.
# Companion files:
#   Core_Combat.txt          ordinary Actions and Turn structure
#   Weaponry.txt             personal arms (engines are not rows there)
#   Economy_Finished_Siege_Arms.txt   material and labour cost
#   Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt   relevant Skills (Artillery, Engineering)

PURPOSE
Siege engines are not weapons a single character swings.
They are machines that require a crew, a prepared position,
and time measured in multiple Rounds or full Turns of work
before they can loose once.

A normal Turn (one Full or two Half Actions) is too short
for most of their cycle. The rules below replace the ordinary
Action economy for the engine itself.

--------------------------------------------------
0. KEYWORDS
--------------------------------------------------

Engine
  The machine. It has no WS or BS of its own
  unless a specific entry says otherwise.
  The crew uses the Artillery Skill (or Engineering for
  setup and repair). Light wall-pieces may use BS
  when braced and Pre-Loaded.

Crew
  Minimum number of able bodies required to operate
  at the listed rate. Fewer bodies slow the cycle.
  Excess bodies do not speed it past the listed minimum.

Cycle
  The full sequence from “ready to load” to “loosed and
  ready to load again.” Stated in Rounds.
  One Round = one Turn for the crew members dedicated
  to the engine.

Setup
  Time to emplace, brace, and first-aim a cold engine.
  Stated in Turns or minutes of narrative time.
  After Setup the engine is Emplaced.

Emplaced
  Braced, aimed at a sector, and able to begin a Cycle.
  Moving an Emplaced engine breaks Emplacement.
  It must be Set up again.

Load / Wind / Aim / Loose
  Stages inside a Cycle. The GM may call for Skill
  rolls at Aim or Loose. Failure delays the Cycle
  by one Round or spoils the shot.

Braced Weapon (X)
  New quality shared with certain specialist arms.
  The weapon cannot be aimed and fired until it has
  been braced on a suitable mount (stone rampart,
  swivel frame, or equivalent).
  Listed Reload assumes full crew complement.
  Firing without a brace suffers an X penalty to hit
  and inflicts 1d10 × (X/5) blunt damage with Impact
  to the shooter’s torso (chest, armpit, collarbone).
  Moving, setting up, and reloading use the Engineering
  Skill. A single trained man may fire a Pre-Loaded
  braced piece using BS as normal once it is Emplaced.

--------------------------------------------------
1. OPERATING RULES
--------------------------------------------------

Dedication
  Each Round a crew member spends on the engine is a
  Full Action for that character. They may not take
  other Full or Half Actions that Round.
  Free Actions remain available if they do not leave
  the engine.

Minimum Crew
  Below the listed minimum the Cycle length doubles
  (round up). At half or fewer the engine cannot Loose
  until the crew is restored.

Accuracy
  Base is the Artillery Skill of the crew leader
  (or the average if no clear leader).
  Range bands and cover apply as for ordinary missiles
  unless a specific engine says otherwise.
  Emplaced and properly Aimed: no extra penalty.
  Hastily Aimed or moving: −20 or worse.

Damage
  Engines do not use the ordinary Cut / Pierce / Blunt
  profile of personal weapons.
  Damage profile, range, and most special effects
  live on the projectile (bolt, stone, shot, or payload).
  Location and Soak still apply to individual targets
  when the shot hits a person or creature.
  Against structures use the breach rules in Core_Combat
  or the GM’s structural hit points.

Reload and Wind
  These are part of the Cycle. They are not ordinary
  Reload Actions from Weaponry.txt.

Movement
  Most engines are static once Emplaced.
  Light pieces may be manhandled a short distance
  (stated per engine) at the cost of breaking Emplacement.

--------------------------------------------------
2. TENSION ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Bow-type machines. Damage, range, and AP live on the bolt.
The engine provides the draw, the brace, and the crew rules.

Rampart Crossbow (Mauerarmbrust)
  Crew: 2 (1 Master Gunner / 1 Loader-Assistant)
  Setup: 2 Turns to brace on rampart or swivel
  Cycle / Reload: 6 (assumes full crew; Engineering Skill)
  Range: determined by bolt
  Damage profile: determined by bolt
  Qualities: Fast (10), Pre-Loaded, Braced Weapon (40)
  Special: Portable by one man via sling (22–26 lbs / 9.9–11.8 kg).
           Impossible to shoulder-fire. Must be braced on stone
           crenellation (recoil hook) or dedicated swivel frame.
           A single trained man may fire a Pre-Loaded piece using
           BS as normal once Emplaced. Moving, bracing, and
           reloading use Engineering. Without brace: −40 to hit
           and 1d10 × 8 blunt Impact to the shooter’s torso.
           Effective against plate at 350–400 yards with proper bolt;
           maximum flight over 600 yards.
  Base cost: 18–35 gc
  Notes: The smallest true siege piece. Bridges infantry
         arms and artillery. Compact enough for tower stairs.
         Dual-crank windlass is separate and carried by the assistant.
         Recoil hook transfers the ~1,800 lb draw directly into
         the wall. Laminated spring-steel prod, hornbeam or yew
         stock, heavy composite linen cord, revolving nut lock.
         Damage and AP live on the Rampart Bolt.

Great Tower Crossbow (Ballista de Turre)
  Crew: 3 (1 Master Gunner / 2 Assistant Loaders)
  Setup: Permanent mount (bolted oak carriage); 4 Turns to ready if dismounted assembly is seated
  Cycle / Reload: 10 (assumes full 3-man crew; Engineering Skill)
  Range: determined by bolt
  Damage profile: determined by bolt
  Qualities: Fast (20), Pre-Loaded, Braced Weapon (60)
  Special: Frame-mounted only. The bow assembly alone (~45 lbs / 20 kg) is the absolute limit for one man to carry via straps; the full assembly (110–130 lbs / 50–60 kg) stays bolted to the fortress.
           Unviable as a personal weapon. Must be braced on its permanent heavy oak timber carriage.
           A single trained man may fire a Pre-Loaded piece using BS as normal once Emplaced.
           Moving the assembly, seating, and reloading use Engineering.
           Without brace: −60 to hit and 1d10 × 12 blunt Impact to the shooter’s torso.
  Base cost: 45–80 gc
  Notes: Anchors gatehouses, bastions, and curtain walls.
         Massive laminated spring-steel prod on a permanent oak carriage.
         Dual-man heavy winch integrated or paired.
         The step up from the Rampart Crossbow; true fortress artillery in crossbow form.
         Damage and AP live on the Great Tower Bolt.

Oxybeles
  Crew: 2–3
  Setup: 4 Turns
  Cycle / Reload: 4
  Range: determined by bolt
  Damage profile: determined by bolt
  Qualities: Pre-Loaded, Braced Weapon (30)
  Special: Early tension engine. Large composite bow mounted on a frame.
           Direct-fire. Lighter and more mobile than the Great Tower.
           Damage and AP live on its dedicated bolt (draft).
  Base cost: 30–55 gc
  Notes: Historical predecessor to later torsion ballistae.
         Still seen on older walls and in specialist arsenals.

--------------------------------------------------
BOLTS — TENSION ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
These bolts are not ordinary crossbow quarrels.
They are purpose-made for the braced wall pieces.
Cost and availability sit in Economy_Finished_Siege_Arms.txt.
They do not appear as rows in Weaponry.txt.

Rampart Bolt (Mauerbolzen)
  Length: 12 inches
  Core Shaft: Dense, straight-grained European ash or hornbeam.
              Heavily tapered (widest near the head, narrowest at the tail)
              to move the centre of gravity forward and resist bending
              under the composite string’s impact.
  Head: Long, narrow bodkin of hardened carbon steel.
        Diamond-shaped or square cross-section.
        Optimised to wedge chainmail links and punch plate.
  Fletching: Thin, stiff leather sheets or scraped horn.
             Glued and bound with copper wire.
             Ordinary feathers would be stripped at these speeds.
  Weight: ~0.4–0.5 kg
  Damage profile: (1, 12, 1)
  Range: 200 / 400 yards
  Qualities when loosed from Rampart: AP (3), Impact
  Notes: Brass-lined groove on the weapon. Carried in a dedicated quiver
         by the assistant. Ineffective or unsafe in a lighter crossbow.
         Optimised for flat-trajectory accuracy at 350–400 yards against plate.
         Damage, AP, and range live on this bolt, not the engine.

Great Tower Bolt (Turmbolzen / Bolt-Spear)
  Length: 16–18 inches
  Core Shaft: Reinforced composite.
              Dense hornbeam core wrapped in a socketed iron jacket,
              or a solid forged all-iron shaft with a hollowed tail
              for balance. Wood alone is not viable.
  Head: Massive barbed or solid-pyramid chisel head.
        Wider than the shaft.
        Displaces material on impact — fractures timber, crumples iron,
        and defeats shield walls, mantlets, and horse armour.
  Fletching: Thin sheet brass or copper plates, cold-riveted into the tail.
             Slightly angled to induce spin for a straight trajectory
             in crosswinds. Closer to a javelin or iron rod than a quarrel.
  Weight: ~0.8–1.1 kg
  Damage profile: (2, 20, 2)
  Range: 450 / 900 yards
  Qualities when loosed from Great Tower: AP (5), Impact
  Notes: Requires the dual-man winch and permanent carriage.
         Not interchangeable with Rampart bolts.
         Extreme range and penetration; light artillery ammunition
         rather than an infantry bolt.
         Damage, AP, and range live on this bolt, not the engine.

Oxybeles Bolt (draft)
  Length: 10–14 inches
  Damage profile: (1, 10, 1) to (2, 14, 2) by size
  Range: 150 / 300 yards
  Qualities: AP (2–4), Impact
  Notes: Intermediate between ordinary heavy crossbow and Rampart.

General
  Bolts are Pre-Loaded into the weapon before the gunner aims.
  They take the AP and Impact listed on the bolt.
  Against structures they count as focused breach attempts scaled to the engine.
  Ammunition is never included in the engine price.

--------------------------------------------------
3. TORSION ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Rope-bundle or sinew torsion machines.
Damage and range live on the projectile.

Euthytone
  Crew: 2–3
  Setup: 6 Turns
  Cycle: 3 Rounds
  Range: 100 / 200 yards (bolt)
  Special: Early straight-spring torsion ballista.
           Direct fire. Precision against single targets.
  Base cost: 20–40 gc
  Notes: Ancestor of later ballistae. Still found in older arsenals.

Ballista
  Crew: 2–4
  Setup: 6 Turns
  Cycle: 2 Rounds
  Range: 120 / 240 yards
  Special: High accuracy against single targets or light structures.
           Bolt can pin or shatter lighter timber.
  Base cost: 25–50 gc
  Notes: Direct-fire. Preferred for precision work and against
         enemy engines or officers.

Scorpion
  Crew: 1–2
  Setup: 3 Turns
  Cycle: 2 Rounds
  Range: 80 / 160 yards
  Special: Light torsion bolt-thrower. Man-portable in sections.
           Fast for its class. Often used as a field piece.
  Base cost: 12–25 gc
  Notes: The lightest practical torsion engine.

Onager
  Crew: 4–6
  Setup: 10 Turns
  Cycle: 3 Rounds
  Range: 80 / 160 yards
  Special: Stone-throwing torsion engine. Against troops, treat as a small
           area (3-yard radius). Against walls, counts as a breach attempt.
  Base cost: 40–80 gc
  Notes: Classic torsion stone-thrower. Needs firm ground.

Springald
  Crew: 2–3
  Setup: 5 Turns
  Cycle: 2 Rounds
  Range: 100 / 200 yards
  Special: Frame-mounted torsion bolt engine, often with a protective housing.
           Common on walls and ships. Faster cycle than a full ballista.
  Base cost: 22–45 gc
  Notes: Popular late-medieval wall piece.

--------------------------------------------------
4. COUNTERWEIGHT ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Gravity and hybrid traction machines. Damage, range, and
reload live on the payload, not the engine. The engine
sets crew, footprint, maximum payload, and deployment.
Accuracy uses Artillery (or Engineering for setup).
Listed Reload assumes full crew. Engineering Skill for
setup, packing, and most loading work.

Mangonel (man-powered / traction)
  Footprint: 3 m × 2 m
  Crew: 4–8 (pullers)
  Max payload: 20–30 lbs
  Deployment: Light frame. Can be carried or wagoned.
              Setup: 10–15 minutes.
  Notes: Simplest man-powered trebuchet. No fixed ballast,
         or only a token weight. Ropes pulled by the crew.
         Fast, inaccurate, anti-personnel.

Tier 1: Traction / Light Trebuchet
  Footprint: 4 m × 3 m flat clearing
  Crew: 4 (1 Engineer, 3 Laborers)
  Max payload: 40 lbs (18 kg)
  Deployment: Disassembled on two heavy wagons.
              Setup or pack-down: Engineering Test, ~30 minutes.
  Notes: Hybrid — man-powered ropes assist a small ballast box.
         Mobile anti-personnel and light fortification piece.
         Fastest cycle of the true counterweight class.

Tier 2: Fortified Siege Trebuchet (The Couillard)
  Footprint: 8 m × 6 m cleared firing platform
  Crew: 8–10 (1 Master Engineer, 1 Spotter, 6–8 Winch Laborers)
  Max payload: 150 lbs (68 kg)
  Deployment: Built on-site over 2–3 days from structural timber.
              Cannot be moved once anchored without full disassembly.
  Notes: Workhorse of late-medieval siege. Massive pivoted box
         packed with iron ore or wet clay. Built to dismantle
         walls, towers, and gates from outside defensive
         wall-crossbow range.

Tier 3: Fortress Breaker (The Warwolf Class)
  Footprint: 15 m × 12 m leveled earthwork foundation
  Crew: 20–30 (1 Master Siege Engineer, 2 Master Carpenters,
               dedicated winch-men, beast-of-burden assistance
               for dual giant capstans)
  Max payload: 300 lbs+ (140 kg+)
  Deployment: 4–6 weeks of dedicated construction, active
              logging camp, and a hard Engineering Test to
              balance structural torque. Failure risks
              catastrophic collapse under its own weight.
  Notes: Pinnacle of gravity engines. Colossal swinging cradle
         with up to 15 tons of lead and rubble ballast.
         Beams thicker than ancient oak trunks.
         Built to break historic deadlocks. Not mobile.

--------------------------------------------------
5. SIEGE PAYLOAD OPTIONS & DAMAGE MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------
Damage profile, range, and reload are properties of the
payload when launched from the given tier.
Reload figures assume full crew for that tier.
All use Artillery Skill to hit unless noted.

Rough Fieldstone (unrefined rubble)
  Tier 1: Damage (1, 10, 2)  Range 250/400  Reload 3
  Tier 2: Damage (2, 10, 4)  Range 200/350  Reload 6
  Tier 3: Damage (3, 12, 8)  Range 150/250  Reload 12
  Special: Inaccurate. −10 to Ballistic / Artillery test to hit.
           Shifting aerodynamics.

Masonry-Cut Sphere (chiseled limestone)
  Tier 1: Damage (1, 12, 2)  Range 300/450  Reload 3
  Tier 2: Damage (2, 12, 6)  Range 250/400  Reload 6
  Tier 3: Damage (4, 10, 10) Range 200/300  Reload 12
  Special: Sunder & Demolish. Double structural integrity
           damage to vertical stone fortifications, walls,
           and wooden hoarding.

Greek Fire Ceramics (earthenware napalm flasks)
  Tier 1: Damage (1, 6, 1)  Range 200/350  Reload 4
  Tier 2: Damage (1, 8, 2)  Range 180/300  Reload 8
  Tier 3: Damage (2, 6, 4)  Range 150/220  Reload 14
  Special: Incendiary Blast. Sticky, unquenchable chemical fire.
           Blast radius 3 m / 6 m / 12 m by tier.
           Bypasses standard armour; ignites structures instantly.

Carcass & Offal (biological)
  Tier 1: Damage None  Range 200/300  Reload 3
  Tier 2: Damage None  Range 180/250  Reload 6
  Tier 3: Damage None  Range 150/200  Reload 12
  Special: Contagion. Spreads toxic rot. Persistent hazard zone.
           Living units in the impact radius must test vs Disease
           or suffer permanent Fatigue.

General
  Payload must not exceed the tier’s Max Payload.
  Ammunition is never included in the engine price.
  Local stone is the usual source for Fieldstone and
  Masonry-Cut Sphere. Greek Fire and carcasses are
  specialist stores.

--------------------------------------------------
6. BLACK POWDER ENGINES
--------------------------------------------------
Early gunpowder pieces. Damage and range live on the shot.
Crew and cycle rules still apply. Black powder is scarce
and dangerous; misfires and burst barrels are possible.

Falconet / Saker
  Crew: 2–3
  Setup: 4 Turns
  Cycle: 3 Rounds
  Special: Light field or wall gun. Anti-personnel and light structure.
  Base cost: 40–70 gc
  Notes: Smallest practical cannon. Often on a wheeled carriage
         or wall mount.

Culverin
  Crew: 4–6
  Setup: 8 Turns
  Cycle: 4 Rounds
  Special: Long-barrel gun. Good range and accuracy for its class.
           Used against ships, towers, and massed troops.
  Base cost: 80–140 gc
  Notes: Workhorse early cannon.

Demi-Cannon / Bombard
  Crew: 6–10
  Setup: 12 Turns or on-site build
  Cycle: 6–8 Rounds
  Special: Heavy stone- or iron-throwing gun. Wall-breaker.
           Slow, powerful, and temperamental.
  Base cost: 150–300 gc
  Notes: The heavy end of early artillery. Bombards are often
         built or assembled on site.

Mortar
  Crew: 3–5
  Setup: 6 Turns
  Cycle: 4 Rounds
  Special: High-angle lobber. Drops shot or explosive shells
           behind walls. Short range, high arc.
  Base cost: 60–110 gc
  Notes: Useful against courtyards and soft targets inside fortifications.

RACK OF FIRE (primitive Katyusha)
  Crew: 4–6
  Setup: 10 Turns
  Cycle: 8 Rounds (full rack) or 2 Rounds per tube
  Special: Multiple light rockets or fire-pots on a frame.
           Area saturation. Inaccurate but terrifying.
           High risk of premature ignition.
  Base cost: 50–90 gc
  Notes: Experimental and rare. More psychological than precise.
         The rack is the engine; the rockets are the payload.
