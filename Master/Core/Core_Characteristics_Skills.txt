# Status: DRAFT for Core — Characteristics, Size, ranks locked in principle.
#         Skill list begun.

PURPOSE
This file defines what an Entity is made of, mechanically: nine
Characteristics, their Bonuses, Size, and the frame Skills will hang
from. Resolution (how you roll) lives in Core_Resolution.txt. This
file answers what you roll against.

--------------------------------------------------
0. KEYWORDS (this file)
--------------------------------------------------

Characteristic
  One of nine standing capacities. Every Entity that can take a test
  has all nine, even if one of them is vestigial (a stone-bulk with
  Fellowship 0 is still an Entity; it simply cannot win a Fellowship test).

Bonus
  Tens digit of a Characteristic. Strength 41 → SB 4.
  Defined in Core_Resolution §6. Repeated here only as abbreviations.

  SB   Strength Bonus
  PB   Perception Bonus
  TB   Toughness Bonus
  FelB Fellowship Bonus
  IB   Intelligence Bonus
  AB   Agility Bonus
  WB   Willpower Bonus
  WSB  Weapon Skill Bonus
  BSB  Ballistic Skill Bonus

Parent Characteristic
  A Characteristic a Skill is built from. A Skill has one or more
  Parents. See Skill Base.

Skill Base
  The spine of a Skill: the mean of its Parent Characteristics.
  Sum the Parents, divide by how many there are, discard the
  remainder. Rank is applied to this number. Rank does not
  replace the mean and does not drop a Parent.

Rank
  How well the Entity knows the Skill. Default is Unknown
  (Skill Base ÷ 2). See section 5.

Advanced Skill (*)
  A Skill that cannot be attempted until it has been formally
  learned. No Unknown test. No guess.
  Formal learning: apprenticeship, academic study, or an equivalent
  closed instruction. No Unknown roll. No “I have a go.”

Intuitive
  A Skill this specific Entity treats as Known without instruction,
  because the body or kind already does that work. Listed on the
  Entity (people, bestiary, profile), not as a property of the Skill
  itself. Ranks above Known still have to be earned. Advanced (*)
  Skills are Intuitive only if the Entity entry says so.

Size / Size Category
  A 1–10 trait of an Entity’s body. Size 4 is Average and is
  Man-Scale. See section 3.

Man-Scale / Scale / Dominate
  Resolution terms. See Core_Resolution §0 and §6a.
  Size does not trigger Dominate. A Characteristic of 100+ does.

Natural Armour Class (NAC)
  Derived from Toughness Bonus. The body’s own soak. See section 3b.

Armour Class (AC)
  Soak from worn armour and shields when Combat writes those
  numbers. Stacks with NAC. Not a Characteristic.

Wounds
  Hit points. Starting Wounds = TB × 3. See section 3c.
  Boxes above the last one are glancing. The last one is
  the blow that counts.

Movement (M)
  How far the Entity goes in structured time.
  Base M = Agility ÷ 10 (keep one decimal), then Size.
  See section 3d. Yards are floored after the Combat formula.

Encumbrance Limit
  How much kit the body can bear, in points.
  Limit = Strength × 10. See section 3d.

Attacks
  Basic attacks allowed per turn in structured time. Default 1.

Insanity Points (IP)
  Accumulated mental trauma. See section 3d.

Fate Points (FP)
  Destiny and luck. Player Characters only. See section 3d.

Initiative
  Reaction speed in Structured Time.
  Initiative = Agility + 1d10.
  Rolled at the start of every Round. Order is rebuilt.
  See Core_Combat.txt.

Specialism
  A bracketed focus on a Skill written (various).
  Trade (Armourer) is not Trade (Glass). See section 6.

--------------------------------------------------
1. THE NINE CHARACTERISTICS
--------------------------------------------------

Every Entity has these nine, and only these nine, unless a later
Core file adds one in this list.

Human-range note
  An ordinary Size 4 adult, before Size modifiers, usually sits
  around 20–40 in each Characteristic. 30 is competent. 40 is
  marked. 10 is crippling. Exact generation is a Character Creation
  problem.

Values may exceed 100. That is Dominate territory for Man-Scale
tests of that Characteristic only (Core_Resolution §6a).

-----
Strength (S)
-----
An Entity’s ability to exert force on the world around them.

Lift, break, shove, haul, crush, hold a gate, draw a heavy bow,
keep a grip, throw mass. Raw output of body against matter.

Not fine placement (that is Agility). Not lasting the work (that
is Toughness). A smith’s hammer-blow is Strength. The hour at the
anvil is Toughness. Setting the punch on the rivet is Agility.

Bonus (SB): damage from force, distance something is moved or
thrown, how much can be lifted or broken without a test.

-----
Perception (Per)
-----
An Entity’s ability to perceive the world around them, by whatever
senses that Entity actually has: sight, smell, hearing,
echolocation, vibration through the ground, heat, the pull of the
veil — the list follows the body, not a human default.

Notice, track, spot a lie in the face if the cue is visible,
catch the changed note in a herd, feel the tripwire. Sensing,
not interpreting.

A wolf and a man can both have high Perception. They are not
sensing the same world.

Not knowing what the mark means (Intelligence). Not using the
mark in conversation (Fellowship). Seeing the mark is Perception.

Bonus (PB): range of notice, number of extra details on a success,
how early a thing is detected.

-----
Toughness (T)
-----
An Entity’s ability to withstand damage, physical duress,
endurance, fatigue, and poison.

Take a blow, march through the night, hold breath, shrug cold,
resist a tincture, keep standing when the body wants to stop.
The body as a wall.

Not force applied outward (Strength). Not keeping a hand steady
(Agility). Not holding a purpose through pain or fear
(Willpower).

Bonus (TB): Natural Armour Class (section 3b), duration of labour
before collapse, resistance amounts for poison, disease, exposure.
TB is not subtracted from damage a second time once NAC exists.

-----
Fellowship (Fel)
-----
An Entity’s ability to act inside social dynamics.

Doing, not understanding. Deliver the speech, hold the stare,
soothe the horse, command the levy, insult a knight in a way
that lands, pass as one of the hall. The work of presence.

A beast can have Fellowship: a gryphon’s bearing, a hound’s plea,
a stallion’s dominance in the herd. The audience has to be able
to receive it. Fellowship against stone is not a test.

Not knowing the custom (Intelligence). Not noticing the room
has turned (Perception). Knowing the right form of address is
Intelligence. Seeing the jaw tighten is Perception. Saying the
thing that uses both is Fellowship.

Bonus (FelB): number of people held, severity of a social result,
how far a reputation chip travels from one scene.

-----
Intelligence (Int)
-----
An Entity’s ability to reason and understand the world, and its
ability to learn and retain new skills and information.

Recall, infer, plan, read a map, learn a trade, follow a legal
argument, reconstruct how the mill jammed, understand a custom
well enough to explain it. The work of the mind.

Not noticing the mill has jammed (Perception). Not talking the
miller down (Fellowship). Not the hour of crawling in the wheel
(Toughness / Agility).

Beasts have Intelligence. Low Intelligence is not “no mind.” It
is a shorter horizon, fewer stored tricks, poorer transfer from
one problem to the next.

Bonus (IB): extra facts recalled, speed of learning when
Advancement exists, complexity of a plan that can be held
without writing.

-----
Agility (Ag)
-----
An Entity’s ability for fine motor control, flexibility, balance,
and body awareness.

Land the cut where it was aimed, pick a lock, keep the saddle,
cross the ice, catch the dropped cup, thread a needle, place
the feet on a cliff that is about balance rather than force.

Not how hard the cut hits (Strength). Not how long the climb
can be sustained (Toughness). Not seeing the next hold
(Perception).

Bonus (AB): short-burst movement (modified by Size), balance
margin, fine-work precision, initiative when Combat exists,
unless that file assigns initiative elsewhere.

-----
Willpower (WP)
-----
An Entity’s ability to withstand mental duress, temptation, pain,
fear, and the machinations of the Immaterium — or to influence it.

Hold a purpose when the body wants to flee. Refuse the drink, the
bribe, the easy lie. Stay present through pain. Stand a False Moon.
Push back on a whisper that is not yours. Where a later file allows
it, bend the veil on purpose.

Toughness is whether the flesh keeps working. Willpower is whether
the will keeps pointing. A poison is Toughness. The urge to lie down
and accept it is Willpower. A wound is Injury and Toughness. Using
the arm anyway, screaming, is Willpower.

Not noticing the wrongness (Perception). Not understanding what the
whisper wants (Intelligence). Not performing courage for an audience
(Fellowship). Fellowship can fake a steady voice. Willpower is whether
the voice should be steady.

Influencing the Immaterium uses this same Characteristic, not a
separate magic stat. How that work is done — cost, backlash, False
Moon — is not written here.

Bonus (WB): severity of fear or temptation shrugged, duration a
purpose is held, magnitude of a veil-working when that file exists.

-----
Weapon Skill (WS)
-----
An Entity’s ability to hit a target in melee.

Connect the blow. Sword, axe, spear, fist, beak, claw, a chair
leg in a tavern. Whether the attack lands on the body it was
aimed at.

Not how hard it hits (Strength). Not the footwork of a climb or
a lock (Agility), though a later combat Skill may list those as
Parents. Not seeing the foe arrive (Perception).

WS is a Characteristic, not a Skill. Melee Skills, when written,
may take WS as a Parent. They do not replace it.

Bonus (WSB): extra effect on a melee hit when Combat exists
(additional DoS converted to result, or as that file says).
Damage itself remains Strength unless Combat says otherwise.

-----
Ballistic Skill (BS)
-----
An Entity’s ability to hit a target with a shot or a thrown
object.

Arrow, bolt, stone, javelin, thrown axe, a hurled bottle, a
spit of venom if the body aims it. Whether the missile arrives
on the mark.

Not drawing a heavy bow (Strength). Not judging that a thing
is there (Perception). Not the flight of the body (Agility).
Seeing the deer is Perception. Putting the arrow in it is BS.

BS is a Characteristic, not a Skill. Ranged Skills, when written,
may take BS as a Parent. They do not replace it.

Bonus (BSB): extra effect on a missile hit when Combat exists.
Damage of the missile is a weapon and Strength question for
that file.

--------------------------------------------------
2. WHICH CHARACTERISTIC TO TEST
--------------------------------------------------

Ask what the Entity is actually doing.

  Force against matter                 Strength
  Lasting, soaking, staying upright    Toughness
  Placing the body or the hand         Agility
  Taking in a stimulus                 Perception
  Making meaning of what is known      Intelligence
  Acting on other wills, socially      Fellowship
  Holding the mind, or pressing the
  Immaterium                           Willpower
  Hitting in melee                     Weapon Skill
  Hitting with shot or throw           Ballistic Skill

Split tests when two things are both in play. A climber who must
find the hold and then commit the body: Perception or an applicable
Skill to read the face, then Agility to move. That is a chain
(Core_Resolution §8), not one mashed TN.

When a Skill exists for the action, use the Skill (section 5).
When the action has no Skill, test the one Characteristic that
is actually doing the work. Do not average Characteristics unless
a Skill says those are its Parents.

Combat uses Weapon Skill to hit in melee and Ballistic Skill to
hit with shot or thrown weapons. Size modifies those TNs
(section 3). Combat may still add Skills with WS or BS as
Parents. It does not add a tenth Characteristic for fighting.

--------------------------------------------------
3. SIZE
--------------------------------------------------

Canonical Size rules. The old Size holding file is merged here.

Every Entity has a Size Category from 1 to 10. Objects that matter
(gates, beams, boats) may be given a Size when an Entity tries to
move, break, or hide behind them.

Size 4 is Average and is Man-Scale.

The printed to-hit and stealth figures below are Man-Scale
relative: they assume the other party is Size 4. They are a
reference chart, not the live modifier.

Live play always uses the difference between the two Entities
in the test. Never apply the printed Titanic +60 (or any other
printed to-hit number) because “the target is large.” That +60
is only Size 10 against Size 4.

  Step        = 1 Size Category
  Step value  = 10

To-hit (WS or BS)
  Attacker’s TN modifier = (Target Size − Attacker Size) × 10

  Size 10 striking Size 9    (9 − 10) × 10 = −10
  Size 9 striking Size 10    (10 − 9) × 10 = +10
  Size 4 striking Size 10    (10 − 4) × 10 = +60
  Size 10 striking Size 4    (4 − 10) × 10 = −60
  Size 7 striking Size 7     (7 − 7) × 10 =   0

  A leviathan does not hit another vast thing at +60. It hits
  the next step down at −10. A monumental troll tagging that
  leviathan is at +10, not +60.
  Same Size is always +0, whatever the number on the chart.

Stealth / concealment
  Actor’s TN modifier = (Observer Size − Actor Size) × 10
  A child (3) hiding from a man (4) is +10.
  A troll (8) hiding from a man (4) is −40.
  Cover, dark, and Skill still apply on top.

Movement
  Base M = Agility ÷ 10 (keep one decimal), then apply the
  printed Size adjustment. Absolute, not relative.
  Floor M at 1.0 unless the Entity cannot move.
  Combat floors the yards after M × 2 / × 4 / × 6.
  See section 3d.

S/T scaling
  Apply the printed modifier to Strength and Toughness only.
  Absolute. This is the usual reason a large Entity reaches 100+
  and therefore Dominates Man-Scale tests of that Characteristic.
  Size does not itself trigger Dominate.

  Typical human Strength/Toughness before Size is 20–40.
  An Immense troll with a strong base of 65 and +40 Size is 105:
  Dominates Man-Scale Strength, still rolls against another troll.

  Perception, Fellowship, Intelligence, Agility, Willpower,
  Weapon Skill, and Ballistic Skill are not moved by this column.
  A titan is not automatically accurate, and is not automatically
  brave. To-hit already uses the Size difference on the WS or BS
  test. Do not also raise WS or BS for bulk.

SIZE TABLE

  1  Miniscule     −30              +30       AB − 3   −30
  2  Puny          −20              +20       AB − 2   −20
  3  Weedy         −10              +10       AB − 1   −10

EXAMPLES (Virath)

Mixed Size
  A man on a destrier is still Size 4 as a target unless the blow
  is meant for the horse. The mount is Size 6. State which Entity
  is being tested.

Caps
  Do not stack Size to-hit with a second “it is big” Difficulty
  chip for the same fact. Size already paid for that.
  Other facts (night, rain, injured wing) still stack.

Combat files may add Wound bands, reach, and trample by Size.
They do not replace this table.

--------------------------------------------------
3a. STRENGTH AND LOAD
--------------------------------------------------

Athletics is run, jump, leap, bound. It is not this section.
Kilograms come from Strength itself, not from Strength Bonus.
Size already sits inside Strength via S/T scaling, so do not
apply Size a second time.

  s = Strength ÷ 10     keep one decimal
  Strength 30 → s 3.0
  Strength 38 → s 3.8
  Strength 50 → s 5.0

  After every load or throw formula, floor the answer.
  NAC stays TB (tens digit of Toughness). Wounds stay TB × 3.
  Those are not converted to T ÷ 10.

TWO SCALES

  Day and Heavy stay linear. A 300 kg pull does not make a
  12-hour ruck scale the same way.

    Day carry, no penalty            s ×  5 kg
    Heavy carry, fatigue             s × 10 kg

  Press, Lift, and Push accelerate from s 4.0 (Strength 40).
  Below that, linear.

    s under 4.0
      Press   s × 15 kg
      Lift    s × 45 kg
      Push    s × 65 kg

    s 4.0 and up
      Press   4 × s × s kg
      Lift   12 × s × s kg
      Push   16 × s × s kg

  s 5.0 is the exceptional-human mark: 100 kg press, 300 kg
  lift/pull. That is the calibration.

  s      Day   Heavy   Press   Lift    Push     notes
  2.0     10      20      30      90     130    linear
  3.0     15      30      45     135     195    ordinary adult
  3.8     19      38      57     171     247    Ag/S 38 band
  4.0     20      40      64     192     256    trained strong
  5.0     25      50     100     300     400    exceptional human
  6.0     30      60     144     432     576
  7.0     35      70     196     588     784    edge of flesh
 10.0     50     100     400    1200    1600    troll-band
 12.0     60     120     576    1728    2304

  Strength 38 and Strength 30 are not the same column.
  Use the formula. Floor the kilograms.

What needs a roll
  Inside press, day-carry, or an easy shove well under push:
    no roll. It happens.
  At the lift ceiling, or an awkward shape, or bad footing:
    Strength test. Success moves it. Failure is a wasted effort
    or a dropped edge, not automatic injury.
  Past the lift or push ceiling:
    no. Dominate does not invent kilograms. A Strength 120
    troll has s 12.0: lift 1728 kg, push 2304 kg. That is the
    ceiling. A cottage door is not that mass; that remains a
    Dominated Man-Scale Strength test (Core_Resolution §6a).

Press
  Awkward mass taken to full extension: grain-sack, timber,
  a body. The Press number, no roll, if the Entity can stand
  and has room. Heavier than that is a Strength test up to
  the lift ceiling, and it is not held there long.

Push / drag
  Legs and friction on flat, solid ground. Movement while
  pushing at the ceiling is a crawl (Combat will set the
  yards; until then, treat it as a slow walk at best).
  Soft ground, slope, or wheels change this by Difficulty
  or by a later Labour Skill, not by rewriting the multiple.

Carry and fatigue
  s × 5 kg: tools, light kit, a day’s pack. Walk the day.
  No mechanical penalty.

  Above that, up to s × 10 kg: heavy kit, a combat load.
  The Entity may carry it for a number of hours equal to
  Toughness Bonus without a test. Each hour after that
  requires a Toughness test. Failure inflicts one Fatigue.

  Fatigue is a condition. Until Injury writes it:
    each Fatigue is −10 to physical TNs (Strength, Toughness,
    Agility, WS, Athletics, and other bodily work).
    At 3 Fatigue, movement is halved.
    At Fatigue equal to TB, the Entity cannot continue
    the march and must drop the load or stop.

  A 12-hour march at Heavy carry with TB 3 is three free
  hours and nine tests. That is supposed to ruin the day.

Throwing — combat toss, no run-up
  Distance = s × the metres in the class, then floor.
  Classes below are written for s 3.0. For any other s,
  multiply each class’s upper weight by (s ÷ 3), floor,
  minimum the listed kilograms.

  Weight class (at s 3.0)         Example           Distance
  Negligible   under 0.5 kg       stone, dagger     s × 4 m
  Light        0.5 to 2 kg        hatchet, javelin  s × 2 m
  Medium       2.1 to 10 kg       hammer, boulder   s × 1 m
  Heavy        10.1 to 25 kg      grain-sack        s × 0.5 m
  Extreme      over 25 kg         log, barrel       Strength
                                                    test; on
                                                    success,
                                                    0.5 m heave

  s 3.0: dagger 12 m, javelin 6 m, sack 1.5 m.
  s 3.8: dagger 15 m, javelin 7 m, sack 1 m.
  These are fight distances — planted feet, kit on, no run-up.
  An athletic throw with room to step is not this table.
  Combat may give missiles their own range bands. Those
  replace this row for that weapon.

Hitting a target with a thrown object is Ballistic Skill.
This table only answers how far the object can be made to go.

--------------------------------------------------
3b. NATURAL ARMOUR CLASS
--------------------------------------------------

NAC is the body’s soak. It is derived from Toughness Bonus.

  NAC = TB

A hide, carapace, or Unveiling thickness may add to NAC. That
add is written on the Entity, not invented at the table. Size
does not add a second time: Size already moved Toughness, so
it already moved NAC.

AC is soak from worn armour on the location hit
(Combat §5). A helm does not cover an arm. Economy
will give the numbers per piece. A shield adds AC
only when Combat says it covers that blow.

  Protection = NAC + AC of the location hit
  AC is typed Cut / Pierce / Blunt. Coverage may
  fail (Armour.txt).

Damage
  Incoming damage is a Combat number (weapon, SB, Degrees —
  that file).
  Subtract Protection from incoming damage.

    If incoming ≤ Protection     →  0 damage. The blow does
                                    not bite.
    If incoming > Protection     →  damage taken = incoming
                                    − Protection.

  Equal to Protection is no damage. One point over is one
  point through.

Do not also subtract TB, NAC, or AC a second time in Injury.
Wounds, crits, and bleed start from the damage that remains
after this step.

NAC 0
  Legal. Soft things. They still wear AC if they can.

Examples
  Human, Toughness 34, no armour
    NAC 3. A 3-damage hit does nothing. A 4-damage hit
    inflicts 1.

  Same human, AC 2 mail
    Protection 5. A 5-damage hit does nothing. A 8-damage
    hit inflicts 3.

  Troll, Toughness 105 (Size already in)
    NAC 10. Unarmoured. Ordinary man-scale blows that roll
    10 or less of damage do not mark it.

--------------------------------------------------
3c. WOUNDS
--------------------------------------------------

Wounds are the Entity’s hit points.

  Starting Wounds = TB × 3

  TB 2 →  6 Wounds
  TB 3 →  9 Wounds
  TB 4 → 12 Wounds
  TB 5 → 15 Wounds
  TB 10 → 30 Wounds

Current Wounds start at that number and fall as damage gets
through Protection (section 3b). Damage that Protection already
stopped does not touch Wounds.

What a lost Wound is
  Every Wound lost while the Entity still has Wounds remaining
  after the hit is minor: a dink, a bruise, a glancing blow, a
  cut that looks worse than it is, wind knocked out, a ringing
  ear, kit scored, pride hurt. These are layers coming off.
  They are not maiming. They are not the death. Describe them
  that way. Do not narrate ruined knees and opened bellies
  while boxes remain.

  The blow that takes the Entity to 0 Wounds is different.
  That single blow is fatal or debilitating — the cut that
  lands, the bone that goes, the beast that drops. Injury
  will say whether 0 is dead, down, or a crit table. Until
  that file exists: at 0 Wounds the Entity is out of the
  fight and the GM names one lasting cost that fits the
  weapon and the fiction.

  Overflow on the finishing blow (3 Wounds left, 10 through)
  may worsen that cost. It does not invent extra corpses
  behind the first one.

What Wounds are not
  They are not a drip of growing injury. Do not apply
  cumulative cripples, bleed tables, or limb loss for each
  box ticked. The plot armour is supposed to hold until it
  does not.

  Fatigue, poison, and veil-shock are not Wounds. They use
  their own tracks.

TB 0
  Starting Wounds 0. The first point through Protection is
  already the finishing blow.

Recovery
  How Wounds return is an Injury / time question. Not written
  here. Until it is: a night’s rest restores 1 Wound if the
  Entity was not at 0. Returning from 0 is treatment, not
  sleep.

Examples
  Human, TB 3, 9 Wounds, Protection 5.
    An 8-damage hit inflicts 3. Wounds 6. A bruise and a
    split lip. Still standing clean.
    Later a 12-damage hit inflicts 7. Wounds −1 → 0. That
    is the blow that drops them.

  Troll, TB 10, 30 Wounds, NAC 10, no AC.
    A 14-damage hit inflicts 4. Wounds 26. The hide is
    marked. The troll is not dying.

--------------------------------------------------
3d. MOVEMENT, ENCUMBRANCE, ATTACKS, INSANITY, FATE
--------------------------------------------------

Movement (M)
  Base M = Agility ÷ 10. Keep one decimal.
  Then add the Size column from section 3 and any Talent
  or condition modifiers.

  Agility Bonus (the tens digit) is not Movement.
  Agility 38 is M 3.8 at Size 4, not M 3.
  That is the point: 31 and 39 do not walk the same.

  Size 4, Agility 30 → M 3.0
  Size 4, Agility 38 → M 3.8
  Size 6, Agility 38 → M 5.8
  Size 2, Agility 38 → M 1.8
  Floor M at 1.0 unless the Entity cannot move.

  Combat yards: floor(M × 2), floor(M × 4), floor(M × 6).
  Difficult ground halves those yards, then rounds up.

Encumbrance Limit
  Combat load cap = Strength × 10 points.
  Strength 30 (ordinary adult) → 300.
  Strength 40 (trained adult) → 400.
  Keep this number.

  Working bridge to section 3a until Economy weights are
  mapped: 10 points ≈ 1 kg.
  Strength × 5 Enc is the Day line (s × 5 kg).
  Strength × 10 Enc is the Heavy line (s × 10 kg)
  and the cap.

  Bands (worn kit plus carried load, in Enc)

    Unencumbered    Enc < Strength × 5
                    Agility tests +5
    Light Kit       Strength × 5 ≤ Enc < Strength × 10
                    no modifier
    Heavy Kit       Enc = Strength × 10
                    Agility tests −5
                    Fatigue as section 3a
    Over the cap    Enc > Strength × 10
                    cannot fight in it. Drop or crawl.

  The ±5 is Agility tests. It does not rewrite M.

  Human check: Day at Strength 30–50 is 15–25 kg.
  That is a military march load. Heavy at Strength
  50 is 50 kg — the combat cap, not the day’s walk.

  Draft and pack quadrupeds
    Enc Limit is still Strength × 10 Enc.
    Then multiply that Limit (only the Limit) if
    the Entity is built to carry on its back:
      Size 5   ×1.5
      Size 6   ×2
      Size 7+  ×2.5
    A Size 6 destrier at Strength 65: 650 Enc
    (65 kg) ×2 → 1,300 Enc, 130 kg Heavy.
    That is the 135 kg draft mark.
    Bands use the modified Limit. Day is half
    of it. Press, Lift, and Push do not take
    this multiplier. Those stay on §3a.

Attacks
  Secondary stat. Default 1.
  The number of basic attacks (melee or ranged) the Entity
  may make in one turn of structured time.
  Talents, weapons, Size, and conditions may raise or cut
  this. Combat writes those modifiers. Do not invent extra
  attacks at the table because the Entity is large unless
  Size or a Talent says so.

Insanity Points (IP)
  A running total of mental trauma: Unveiling horrors, failed
  Fear or Terror tests, witnessed atrocity, long exposure to
  the wrong night.
  This file only stores the number. Thresholds, disorders,
  and how IP is gained or shed belong with Willpower / veil
  rules. Do not convert IP into Wounds.

Fate Points (FP)
  Destiny, luck, or heroic resilience. Player Characters only.
  NPCs, beasts, and abominations do not have Fate.
  Spending one lets the player intervene in a dire moment:
  not die on the finishing blow, force a reroll, or walk out
  of a result the dice already closed. Exact spends are an
  Advancement / Combat note. Until that exists: one FP buys
  one of those three, declared before the fiction moves on.
  FP do not refill in the same scene.

Initiative
  Initiative = Agility + 1d10.
  This is the full Agility Characteristic, not Agility Bonus.
  Agility 38 → roll 1d10 → score 39 to 48.
  Reroll at the start of every Round. The new scores are the
  new Turn order. Surprise still only applies to Round 1.
  Turn order and surprise procedure are Core_Combat.txt.

--------------------------------------------------
4. ZERO VALUES AND LEFTOVER GAPS
--------------------------------------------------

Fellowship 0 / Intelligence 0 / Willpower 0
  Legal. Mindless bulk, a thing with no social existence, or a
  husk that cannot hold a purpose. They fail those tests. They
  are still Entities if they can take any other test.

  Willpower 0 is not “unafraid.” It is no will to test. Fear,
  temptation, and the Immaterium act without opposition.

Still not written
  How veil-working is actually performed, what it costs, and
  what a False Moon does to TNs. Those use Willpower. They are
  not defined here.

--------------------------------------------------
5. SKILLS
--------------------------------------------------

A Skill is a trained or practised action. It draws on one or more
Parent Characteristics. The TN of a Skill test is the Skill value
after modifiers.

Skill Base
  Skill Base = (sum of Parent Characteristics) ÷ (number of Parents)
  Remainder discarded.

  One Parent: the Base is that Characteristic.
  Two Parents: the Base is their mean.
  Three or more: same rule.

  This is the point. A single oversized Characteristic cannot carry
  a Skill that also needs another capacity.

Example — Climbing (Parents: Strength, Agility)
  Strength is the pull and the hold. Agility is the movement.
  Climbing Base = (Strength + Agility) ÷ 2

  Troll, Strength 120, Agility 35
    Base = 155 ÷ 2 = 77
    Strong enough to hang. Not a flash of a technical route.
    Strength 120 would Dominate a Man-Scale door. It does not
    Dominate this Skill.

  Stage-hand, Strength 25, Agility 55
    Base = 80 ÷ 2 = 40
    Hands and balance. Not the athleticism of a climber who can
    lock off on a small hold. Sleight of hand, if it is Agility
    alone, stays at 55. Climbing does not.

Ranks
  No Entity knows a Skill by default. Every Skill starts Unknown
  unless it has been learned, or unless it is Intuitive for that
  Entity.

  Learned
    Life, labour, a career, an apprenticeship, or a later
    Advancement rule. First learned rank is Known.

  Intuitive
    Natural to this Entity’s body or kind. A gryphon bounds.
    A human child does not need a master to run. The Entity
    entry (people, bestiary, profile) lists which Skills are
    Intuitive. Treat those as Known from the start. Proficient
    and above are still earned.
    The Skill definition itself is not Intuitive. The Entity is.

    Unknown        Skill Base ÷ 2     remainder discarded
    Known          Skill Base
    Proficient     Skill Base + 10
    Experienced    Skill Base + 20
    Master         Skill Base + 30

  Unknown is not “Base with a penalty.” It is half the Base.
  Known is the first learned rank: the full mean, nothing added.
  Each rank after Known is +10.

  Ranks modify Skill Base. They do not drop a Parent from the mean.

  Climbing examples, continuing from above
    Troll, Base 77
      Unknown       38
      Known         77
      Proficient    87
      Experienced   97
      Master       107
    Stage-hand, Base 40
      Unknown       20
      Known         40
      Proficient    50
      Experienced   60
      Master        70

Advanced Skills (*)
  Marked with an asterisk on the Skill list.
  Require formal learning: apprenticeship, academic instruction,
  guild mystery, or an equivalent closed path.
  Cannot be attempted at Unknown. If the Entity has not learned
  the Skill, there is no test. The work is not done.
  Once Known, Proficient, Experienced, and Master apply as normal.

  Ordinary Skills may be attempted Unknown, at half Base: a
  desperate climb, a guessed haggle, a raw track. Poorly.

Dominate
  A Skill Dominates only if its Rank value (before situational
  modifiers) is 100+ and the test is Man-Scale work that Skill
  actually covers.
  One Parent at 100+ is not enough if other Parents drag the mean
  down. Tear the door off: that is a Strength test, and the troll
  Dominates. Climb the chimney: that is Climbing, and the troll rolls.

Which Bonus
  Effect amounts use the Bonus of the Parent that is doing the
  work in that moment, or the lowest relevant Bonus if both are
  in play (SB for a locked-off hang, AB for a balance step).
  Skills do not have their own Bonus unless a later rule says so.

Impossible bodies
  If a required Parent is 0 and the action needs that capacity
  at all, there is no test. A thing with Agility 0 does not
  climb at Strength ÷ 2. It does not climb.

The Skill list in section 6 names each Skill’s Parents and marks
Advanced Skills with *. Do not invent a second mean at the table
for a Skill that already has Parents written.

6. SKILL LIST
--------------------------------------------------

Format
  Name
  Parents
  Ordinary or Advanced (*)
  Use
  Specialism, if any, is the bracket. Trade (Armourer) cannot
  blow glass. Speak Language (Isernvolk) is not Speak Language
  (Syllvani). Each specialism is its own Skill for Rank.

--------------------------------------------------
ORDINARY
--------------------------------------------------

Athletics
  Parents:  Strength, Agility
  Use:      running, jumping, leaping, bounding
  Not:      lift, carry, haul; not Climb, Swim, or Ride
  Strength is the drive. Agility is the feet and the landing.

Awareness
  Parents:  Perception
  Use:      noticing details, ambushes, hidden things, a change
            in the room. Instant take, not a search of the chest.

Charm
  Parents:  Fellowship
  Use:      persuasion, making friends, calming a person or a
            willing beast. Not Intimidate. Not Haggle.

Concealment
  Parents:  Agility
  Use:      hiding from sight. Visibility. Size stealth modifier
            applies. Not Silent Move.

Dodge
  Parents:  Agility
  Use:      evading attacks in structured time. Combat will say
            when this replaces or sits beside WS.

Evaluate
  Parents:  Intelligence, Perception
  Use:      appraising value, spotting fakes, knowing what a
            thing should cost in this market.

Gossip
  Parents:  Fellowship
  Use:      gathering rumours, loosening tongues in a hall or
            yard. Not a substitute for Common Lore.

Haggle
  Parents:  Fellowship
  Use:      bartering a price. Speak Language may chain into
            this (Core_Resolution §8).

Intimidate
  Parents:  Fellowship, Strength
  Use:      threats, cowing, the promise of force. Mean of
            presence and mass. A slight tyrant still needs Fel.
            A silent brute still needs Fel.

Common Lore (various)
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      working knowledge of a field. Specialisms include
            History, Religion, Unveiling, Monsters, Regions
            (name the region). Common Lore (Kannavald) is not
            Common Lore (Heraldry — that is Academic).

Ride
  Parents:  Agility
  Use:      handling a mount that accepts a rider. Not Drive.
            Not Animal Training. The mount is a separate Entity.

Silent Move
  Parents:  Agility
  Use:      moving quietly. Not Concealment. A man can be silent
            and still visible, or hidden and still loud.

--------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED (*)
--------------------------------------------------

Cannot be attempted Unknown. Formal learning, or Intuitive
only if the Entity entry says so.

Animal Care *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      keeping and healing animals. Not surgery on men.
            Not taming a wild adult.

Animal Training *
  Parents:  Intelligence, Fellowship
  Use:      taming and training beasts (hounds, destriers,
            direwolves, gryphons). Long work. One test is not
            a bonded mount.

Disguise *
  Parents:  Perception, Fellowship
  Use:      altering appearance and behaviour to pass. Seeing
            what to copy, then wearing it.

Heal *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      first aid, treating disease and poison, keeping a
            body from the last Wound. Not Surgery.

Navigation *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      maps, stars, not getting lost. Outdoor Survival
            feeds it; it does not replace it.

Performer (various) *
  Parents:  Fellowship, Agility
  Use:      acting, singing, contortion, storytelling — name
            the specialism. Performer (Song) is not
            Performer (Contortion).

Read/Write *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      literacy in a script already spoken or being
            learned. Forgery is this Skill used badly or well.
            Does not grant Speak Language.

Search *
  Parents:  Perception
  Use:      detailed inspection, finding what is hidden, traps
            in a chest or a wall. Not Awareness. Takes time.

Sleight of Hand *
  Parents:  Agility
  Use:      pickpocketing, palming, planting. Not Evaluate.
            Not Charm.

Speak Language (various) *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      a specific tongue. Specialisms are the languages
            of Virath (Isernvolk, Syllvani, and others as World
            files name them). Speak Language (Isernvolk) is not
            Speak Language (Syllvani). Do not use Calradian
            culture names.

Torture *
  Parents:  Willpower
  Use:      extracting information through pain. Risks
            backlash (IP, reputation, veil). The subject still
            rolls Willpower to hold.

Trade (various) *
  Parents:  listed on the specialism; default Intelligence,
            Agility
  Use:      a specific craft. Trade (Armourer), Trade
            (Gunsmith), Trade (Herbalist), Trade (Brewer).
            Trade (Armourer) cannot blow glass. Each
            specialism is its own Rank.

Academic Knowledge (various) *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      deep lore. Specialisms: Engineering, Science,
            Arcane, Heraldry, and others as written. Not
            Common Lore. Not Channelling.

Channelling *
  Parents:  Willpower
  Use:      manipulating Unveiling energies (druids, rare
            mystics). The procedure, cost, and False Moon
            live in a later file. This Skill is the TN.

Drive *
  Parents:  Agility
  Use:      wagons and carts, including rough ground. Not Ride.

Outdoor Survival *
  Parents:  Intelligence, Perception
  Use:      foraging, shelter, reading weather and country.
            Chains into Stealth and travel as the Guidelines
            already allow.

Prepare Poison *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      making toxins. Risky. Heal and Toughness answer
            the other end.

Secret Language (various) *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      closed codes. Battle Tongue, thief-cant, and
            others as written. Not a public Speak Language.

Secret Signs *
  Parents:  Intelligence
  Use:      marks and gestures: hunter signs, gang marks,
            way-tokens. Reading and leaving them.

Trapping *
  Parents:  Agility, Intelligence
  Use:      building and spotting snares. Not Track.

Surgery *
  Parents:  Intelligence, Perception, Agility
  Use:      advanced medical work — openings, amputations,
            setting what Heal cannot. Mean of three Parents.
            Heal is first aid. This is the knife.

Track *
  Parents:  Intelligence, Perception
  Use:      following trails. Outdoor Survival says the
            country can be lived in. Track says who passed.
