# Armour_Rules.txt
# Virath armour — law. How layers, slots, and soak work.
# Status: DRAFT for Core.
# Companion files:
#   Armour.txt                          Models, Forms, stats
#   Core_Combat.txt                     hitting, damage
#   Core_Injury.txt                     Wallace + Limb location
#   Weaponry.txt                        Armour Piercing, type
#   Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt     Enc Limit, NAC

This file is the playable law. Piece numbers live in Armour.txt.

PURPOSE
Worn AC is per location and per type. A helm does not cover
an arm. Mail does not treat a hammer like a sword.

Law only. Layers, Limb locations, Slots, soak.
A cuirass, pauldrons, and jack chains sit on one man
because Slots match Limb locations. Stats: Armour.txt.

ASSESS

  Locations
    After a hit stands, roll 1d100 for Location
    (Wallace bands, section 1). Slots are wardrobe.
    Coverage is the chance a layer caught that
    Location. Location d100 and Coverage d100
    are two different dice.

  Soak
    One d100, read down the layers (section 5).
    Modular catch: 1d(modules on that Location)
    names the plate. Use that module’s AC, plus
    Metallic Flexible and Textile on it.
    Mail catch: mail AC plus Textile.
    Textile catch: Textile only.
    Then NAC. Armour Piercing ignores 1 AC after
    that total (minimum 0).

  Layering
    Layers: None, Clothing, Textile, Metallic Flexible,
    Modular.
    Each slot: one piece per layer. Clothing is 0 AC.

  Not imported
    Calradia culture tag lists. Cosmetic tags may be
    written on a piece; they do not change AC.
    Durability Score. Condition tags (Worn, Ruined)
    may cut AC later. Enc is points.

--------------------------------------------------
0. KEYWORDS
--------------------------------------------------

Where (do not mix them)

  Wallace location
    The limb the first d100 named after the
    hit stood. Locked bands (Injury §3):
    Head, Torso, Right Arm, Left Arm,
    Right Leg, Left Leg.

  Limb location
    Where on that limb the second d100 named.
    Locked bands (Injury §3). This is the
    combat “where” for soak and for Injury
    tables. Print it on the sheet next to
    Wallace location.

      Arm:     Hand, Forearm, Elbow,
               Upper Arm, Shoulder
      Leg:     Foot, Shin / Calf, Knee,
               Thigh, Hip / Buttock
      Torso:   Chest / Upper Back,
               Abdomen / Lower Back,
               Groin / Pelvis
               (Glutes / Sacrum from behind),
               Collarbone / Base of Neck
      Head:    Eyes, Skull / Occipital,
               Lower face / Back of neck,
               Throat

    Facing picks the front or back name on
    a pair (Injury §3).

  Slot
    The same list as Limb location. L and R
    are separate Slots on arms and legs.
    Every Slot may hold one Textile, one
    Metallic Flexible, and one Modular piece.
    A piece only answers a hit if it is
    tagged to that Limb location.
    A vambrace is Forearm. It does not
    answer Upper Arm.

  Coverage
    Chance that piece caught the Limb
    location the second d100 named. A piece
    tagged to that Slot is 100% of that Slot
    unless the piece says otherwise (jack
    chains Pierce 0, visor Eyes only).

  Short: Wallace is the limb. Limb location
  is the spot. Slot is that spot on the
  wardrobe. Coverage is the chance on that
  spot. The Coverage d100 is a third die.

AC (Cut / Pierce / Blunt)
  Three soak numbers on a piece. Use the column that
  matches the declared blow.

Layer
  None, Clothing, Textile, Metallic Flexible, Modular.
  Each Slot may hold one Textile, one Metallic Flexible,
  and one Modular piece. Clothing does not take a Slot.

How a worn piece is named

  Model
    What it is made as. The listed item.
    A Model has Coverage, Slots, and (on Textile,
    mail, and Modular torso) flat AC, Enc, Cost.
    Modular arms and legs: the Model is shape
    only — Coverage and Slots. Material supplies
    AC, and Cost and Enc per 5% Coverage.
    Textile: Aketon, Gambeson, Arming Doublet,
    Pourpoint, and the lists in section 7.
    Metallic Flexible: Riveted Mail, Butted
    Mail, Scale, and the lists in section 8.
    Modular torso: Coat of Plates, Laminar,
    Cuir Bouilli, Brigandine, Plate.
    A Modular Model lists the Slots it claims
    at minimum (Plate is Chest or Abdomen,
    same stats; the segmented coats are
    Chest + Abdomen).

  Form
    Added cut on that Model. Does not change
    the Model’s AC. Changes Slots claimed and
    modifies Coverage, Enc, and Cost.
    Textile / mail Form: sleeve length and hem
    length.
    Modular torso Form: Backplate, Pauldrons,
    and the other front plate (Plate only).
    Example: Plate is the Model, Chest or
    Abdomen at the same stats. Adding the
    other front plate, Backplate, and
    Pauldrons is Form on that Model.

  Upgrade
    A field or making extra on a finished
    piece (Armour.txt Upgrades). Applied after Model
    and Form. May change AC, Enc, Cost, or
    add a special. Dense quilting, tempered
    rings, and so on.

  Order: pick Model, apply Form, then Upgrades.
  Craftsmanship (Good / Best / Poor) sits on
  the finished piece after those three.

--------------------------------------------------
1. LOCATIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Combat location d100 (Wallace bands)
  01–10   Head          10%
  11–50   Torso         40%
  51–60   Right Arm     10%
  61–70   Left Arm      10%
  71–85   Right Leg     15%
  86–100  Left Leg      15%

  85 was written on both legs in the draft.
  Left Leg starts at 86 so the bands do not
  overlap and still sum to 100.

A piece lists which of those it covers and at what %.
Feet are Legs. Hands are Arms. Face and visor are Head.
Tassets and Faulds hang from the Waist onto Legs.

--------------------------------------------------
1a. SUB-LOCATIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Sub-locations are wardrobe slots. Every slot may
hold three layers: Textile, Metallic Flexible,
Modular. They do not replace the location d100. They do not
replace Coverage. Coverage stays on the piece.

Facing (common sense, no extra roll)
  Face-to-face: sum all Modular Coverage on that
  Location, including the Backplate. The wrap
  still counts. The 1dN may name any module that
  went into that total.
  Struck from behind: only rear modules count
  for Coverage and for the 1dN. Chest and
  Abdomen are not in the way from the back.
  Flank or scramble: GM names which modules sit
  in the total.

TORSO Slots (Limb locations)

  Slot                         Typical piece
  Chest / Upper Back           Breastplate; Backplate
                               (facing picks the name)
  Abdomen / Lower Back         Plackart; lower backplate
  Groin / Pelvis
  (Glutes / Sacrum behind)     Faulds; tassets hang here
  Collarbone / Base of Neck    Gorget

  A cuirass is Chest + Abdomen, plus Upper / Lower
  Back if a backplate Form is on. Facing names
  which side of the pair is live.

  Faulds sit on Groin / Pelvis (or Glutes / Sacrum
  from behind). Tassets hang from that Slot onto
  Thigh. They do not take the Thigh Slot. Cuisses
  may sit on Thigh under them.

  Shoulder is an Arm Limb location, not Torso.
  Pauldrons live there.

  Besagew is not a Limb location. It is a Called
  Shot / armpit extra on Shoulder. Ordinary blows
  do not land on it.

ARM Slots (Limb locations, L and R separate)

  Slot            Typical piece
  Hand            Gauntlet
  Forearm         Vambrace; Bracers; Jack chains
  Elbow           Couter
  Upper Arm       Rerebrace; Jack chains
  Shoulder        Pauldron

  Full Gauntlet claims Hand + Forearm.
  Jack chains claim Upper Arm + Forearm on that
  side. They leave Elbow and Hand free. They do
  not claim a Torso slot.

  L and R are separate wardrobes.
  One Modular piece per slot per side. A solid
  vambrace and segmented bracers cannot share a
  forearm. A cuirass plus pauldrons plus jack
  chains is legal: different slots.

LEG Slots (Limb locations, L and R separate)

  Slot            Typical piece
  Foot            Sabaton; boots
  Shin / Calf     Full greaves; rigid boot greaves
  Knee            Poleyn
  Thigh           Cuisses
  Hip / Buttock   (pair with Groin / Pelvis on
                  Torso when facing says so)

  Faulds and tassets are Torso Groin / Pelvis,
  not this list. Tassets still hang coverage
  onto Thigh. Cuisses and tassets are not
  exclusive.

  Hem-H Slots use the same Limb location
  names with H (Thigh-H, Knee-H, Shin-H,
  Hip-H). Trouser Slots are the plain names.
  Hem AC and trouser AC do not stack.

  L and R are separate.
  One Modular piece per slot per side.

HEAD Slots (Limb locations)

  Slot                         Typical piece
  Eyes                         Visor; full visor
  Skull / Occipital            Bowl; skullcap; sallet
                               (facing picks the name)
  Lower face / Back of neck    Nasal; bevor; ventail;
                               sallet tail
  Throat                       Gorget (if worn as Head);
                               aventail

  Empty Eyes is the visor zone: 0 AC until a
  visor sits there.

  A helm may claim more than one slot.
    Skullcap              Skull
    Nasal / kettle        Skull
    Sallet                Skull + Occipital
    Bascinet              Skull
    Armet / barbute       Skull + Face
    Great bascinet,
    frog-mouth,
    visored great helm    Skull + Face + Occipital
                          Eyes if a visor is fitted
    Greathelm / enclosed  Skull + Face + Occipital

  Add-ons
    Visor                 Eyes
    Full visor            Face + Eyes
    Ventail               Face
    Bevor                 Face
    Aventail              no plate slot (mail skirt)

  One Modular piece per slot. A nasal and a full
  visor cannot both take Face. A skullcap cannot
  sit under a bascinet. Visor tags stay cosmetic
  once Eyes is filled.

--------------------------------------------------
2. LAYERS
--------------------------------------------------

  None               no worn AC
  Clothing           0 AC. Weather and Fellowship only.
  Textile            padded, leather, quilt. Soft. Strong
                     vs Blunt relative to its cost.
  Metallic Flexible  riveted mail, butted mail, scale.
  Modular            slot pieces. Segmented coats,
                     jack chains, solid plate. Uses Slots (section 1a).

Typical band (not a substitute for the piece table)

  Layer                C / P / B     Notes
  Clothing             0 / 0 / 0
  Textile              2–3 / 1–2 / 2–3
  Butted mail          2 / 2 / 2
  Riveted mail         4 / 4 / 4
  Scale                4 / 3 / 5
  Modular (segmented)  4–6 / 4–6 / 4–5
  Modular (solid)      5–8 / 5–9 / 4–7

--------------------------------------------------
3. LAYERING
--------------------------------------------------

Outer to inner. Sum AC from every piece that applies.

  Each slot: one Textile, one Metallic Flexible, one
  Modular. A torso garment may claim many slots at
  once (Chest + Abdomen + Back is the default body).
  Two pieces of the same layer cannot share a slot.
  Textile under mail under Modular is legal.
  Mail voiders only fill a gap the outer piece left
  (Armpit slots).

When the outermost piece on that location applies
(coverage success), a Textile layer under it applies
in full with no roll. Other inner layers still roll.

Under-layers
  Modular on Arms requires an Arming Doublet. Body
    plate alone does not.
    Jack chains do not require a Doublet. A Doublet
    under them is legal.
  Modular on Body only (no Modular on the Arms) is
    fine over an Aketon or Pourpoint. No chafing.
    An Arming Doublet also serves. A Gambeson does
    not — it is standalone.
  Modular on Legs requires Arming Hose.
  Metallic Flexible on Body or Arms requires a Textile
    torso whose sleeve slots cover at least the mail
    or scale’s sleeve slots (no sleeves under no
    sleeves is legal; Short under Long is not).

  Missing the required piece, or sleeves too short:
    −10 Agility tests and −1 AC on that location
    (chafing).

Enc: add every worn piece. Round each piece to the
nearest 5 after mods. Enc Limit is Strength × 10.

Max four Modular pieces on one combat location
(Body, Arm, or Leg) as a bulk cap. Slots are the
mix rule. The cap is the weight of iron on one
limb or the ribs.

--------------------------------------------------
4. CRAFTSMANSHIP
--------------------------------------------------

Listed AC is Common.

  Good   +1 to each AC column. Cost ×3.
  Best   +2 to each AC column. Cost ×10.
  Poor   −1 to each column. Cost ×½.

--------------------------------------------------
5. RESOLUTION ON A HIT
--------------------------------------------------

The hit already stood (WS/BS, then Dodge or Parry).
Now find where it landed and what caught it.

  1. Location. Two d100s on a biped (Injury §3).
     First: Wallace limb.
     Second: Slot on that limb.
     3+ DoS or Called Shot may name the limb,
     the Slot, or both. These dice are not the
     Coverage die.

  2. Type. Slash / Thrust / Bash from the weapon.
     Incoming = 1d10 + SB + type + DoS (Combat §9).

  3. One coverage die. Roll 1d100 once. Keep it.
     Attacker DoS: −10 to each layer’s Coverage
     total per 2 DoS before you compare.

     Standing target. A shot or swing at a man
     on his feet does not hunt the femoral, the
     visor slit, or the armpit unless the attacker
     Called Shot that gap or has 3+ DoS and names
     it. Padded chausses, arming hose, and mail
     chausses are 100% Legs against that ordinary
     blow. The DoS Coverage penalty does not
     eat that 100%. The holes that still exist
     on a closed harness are the face (Eyes
     empty, visor up) and the groin (no fauld
     or codpiece, or a declared gap). A rondel
     in the bind may use the old lower Coverage.
     An arrow at twenty yards may not.

  4. Modular. Only modules tagged to this Slot
     are in the pool. A cuirass piece tagged
     Chest does not answer an Abdomen hit.
     Face-to-face, a Back Slot is read as Chest
     unless the blow was from behind.
     A piece tagged to the Slot is Coverage
     100% of that Slot unless it says otherwise.
     If several Modular pieces share the Slot
     (they should not) or Form listed extras
     on this Slot: 1dN names which AC and which
     sentence.
     Soak AC = that module’s matching column,
     plus Metallic Flexible tagged to this Slot,
     plus Textile tagged to this Slot.
     Log the named module (“the blow rings on
     the vambrace”).
       Stop. Go to step 7.

  5. Metallic Flexible. If the same d100 is greater
     than the Modular total, compare it to the mail
     or scale Coverage on that location.
     If the d100 ≤ that Coverage:
       Soak AC = Metallic Flexible AC on that
       location, plus Textile AC on that location.
       Stop. Go to step 7.

  6. Textile. If the same d100 is greater than the
     mail Coverage, compare it to the Textile
     Coverage on that location.
     If the d100 ≤ that Coverage:
       Soak AC = Textile AC on that location.
     If not: no worn AC. NAC only.

  7. Hem and trousers on Textile or mail do not
     stack. Use the higher column of the two.
  8. Armour Piercing: −1 AC after the total
     (minimum 0).
  9. Protection = NAC + Soak AC.
     Incoming − Protection = Wounds.

The same d100 is never rerolled. It is read down
the layers until one catches or none do.

Which module (do not add plates)
  Coverage percentages are added to see whether
  iron caught the blow. Their ACs are not added.
  The 1dN picks one module from the list that
  built that total.

  Torso, face-to-face, a Thrust.
  Breastplate Pierce 7, Coverage 20.
  Plackart Pierce 7, Coverage 20.
  Modular total Coverage 40. Two modules.
  d100 is 31: plate catches.
  1d2 names the plackart.
  Plate soak is 7 from the plackart, not 14.
  Then add mail and quilt on the Torso.
  Then NAC. Say the spear fetched up on the
  belly plate.

  Adding 7 and 7 turns two chances to meet
  iron into a double breastplate. Do not.
