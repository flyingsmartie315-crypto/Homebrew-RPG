# Core_EXP.txt
# Experience expenditure for Characteristics, Skills, and Talents.
# Status: DRAFT
# Companion files:
#   Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt
#   Core_Talents_Failings_Afflictions.txt
#   Format_NPC_Profile.txt

PURPOSE
This file defines how Experience is awarded and spent.
NPCs spend according to age and occupation.
Players spend freely provided they justify the improvement.

--------------------------------------------------
NPC EXPERIENCE
--------------------------------------------------
NPC Experience correlates to age.
A simple working figure: years of adult life × a base rate,
adjusted by rarity tier and intensity of occupation.

Working rates (draft):
Lumpen      75 XP per adult year
Common      100 XP per adult year
Uncommon    125 XP per adult year
Elite       150 XP per adult year

Adult years begin at 16 unless the occupation or culture says otherwise.
The total is the pool the NPC has already spent by the time they are met.
It is not a reserve they hold for later.

The pool is spent on skills and characteristics that match
the NPC’s profession and lived experience.
A carter spends on Drive, Athletics, and relevant Trade.
A soldier spends on Weapon Skill, Ballistic Skill, Melee, and Endurance.
A clerk spends on Literacy, Evaluate, and relevant Lore.
Do not give an NPC high ranks in skills their life has not touched.

Talents are purchased from the same pool when the occupation
and characteristic profile support them.
See Core_Talents_Failings_Afflictions.txt for costs and prerequisites.

--------------------------------------------------
PLAYER EXPERIENCE
--------------------------------------------------
Player Experience is awarded at GM discretion.
It may be given for scenes survived, goals completed,
clever play, or simple passage of time.
No fixed rate is locked.

The player spends freely.
Any Characteristic, Skill, or Talent may be improved
provided the player supplies a short justification
that the improvement follows from what the character has done.
The GM may refuse a spend that has no grounding in play.
The GM may not refuse a spend that is reasonably justified.

--------------------------------------------------
COSTS (DRAFT)
--------------------------------------------------
Characteristic advance
  +1 to a Characteristic: 10 XP × current tens digit
  (Strength 31 → 32 costs 30 XP)

Skill ranks and costs
Unknown       0 XP
Known         75 XP
Proficient    150 XP
Experienced   300 XP
Master        600 XP

A skill begins at Unknown.
Each rank is purchased in order.
The cost is the price to reach that rank from the one below.
Total spent to reach Master is 75 + 150 + 300 + 600 = 1,125 XP.

Talent
  As listed in Core_Talents_Failings_Afflictions.txt.
  If no cost is listed, treat as 200 XP for a basic Talent,
  400 XP for an advanced Talent.

These costs are a working floor. They may be adjusted
once more play has tested them.

--------------------------------------------------
RULES
--------------------------------------------------
- NPC Experience is spent before the character is met.
  It does not accumulate as a free pool in play unless the GM says so.
- Player Experience accumulates and is spent between scenes
  or at the end of a session, at the GM’s call.
- Justification for a player spend must name the scene,
  the action, or the period of practice that supports the improvement.
- An NPC’s skills must remain coherent with their occupation
  and the Peoples and Economy files that define their work.
- Characteristics generated at NPC creation (Format_NPC_Profile.txt)
  are the starting point. Experience spending may raise them further
  if age and occupation support it.

--------------------------------------------------
PRE-ADULTHOOD DEVELOPMENT
--------------------------------------------------
Human characters are not mechanical entities from birth.

Years 0–5
No characteristics. No skills.
The child is fully dependent on the social structure around them.
They are not an Entity for resolution purposes.

Year 6
Characteristics first manifest at one-quarter of their eventual adult value.
The child gains Speak Language of the tongue they were raised with.
No other skills.

Year 12
Characteristics now exist at one-half of their eventual adult value.
Situational skills become Known according to upbringing and environment.
Not every child gains every skill. Justification is required.

Athletics          Active martial or manual-labour upbringing
Awareness          Upbringing in environments that demand situational awareness
Charm              Regular social interaction beyond the immediate family
Concealment        Street, frontier, or other contexts where hiding was necessary
Dodge              Active martial upbringing or a turbulent one
Evaluate           Trades or mercantile family or community
Gossip             Regular social interaction beyond the immediate family
Haggle             Regular social interaction beyond the immediate family
                   in a village-sized settlement or larger
Common Lore        Automatically gains the common knowledge of the host culture
                   unless isolated from it
Ride               Raised with horses or formally taught
                   (nobility, steppe, or equivalent)
Silent Move        Street, frontier, or other contexts where quiet movement was necessary
Read/Write         Formal education or tutoring
Outdoor Survival   Frontier or survivalist upbringing
Trapping           Frontier or survivalist upbringing
Track              Frontier or survivalist upbringing that included hunting

Age 18
Characteristics reach their full generated value.
The character begins accumulating Experience at the adult rate
(see NPC EXPERIENCE above; adult years counted from 16 for continuity,
or from 18 if the culture delays adulthood).
A second pass at skills is available.
Any basic or advanced skill may be gained or advanced
provided the upbringing, education, or context justifies it.
This is the point at which the adult Experience pool begins to be spent.
