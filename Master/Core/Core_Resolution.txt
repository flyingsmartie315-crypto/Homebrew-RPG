# Core_Resolution.txt
# Virath core resolution — d100 tests, Degrees, opposed and chained tests
# Status: DRAFT for Core.
# Companion files:
#   Core_RP_Guidelines.txt              tone, transparency, situational modifiers
#   Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt     Characteristics, Size, Skills, M, Enc, FP
#   Log/Campaign_Log.txt                living date, time, and world state
#   Core_Combat.txt / Core_Injury.txt   not yet written

PURPOSE
This file is the spine. Every Characteristic test, Skill test, opposed
contest, and chained test uses these rules. Combat, injury, advancement,
and company files add procedures on top. They do not replace the d100 test.

GM law from Core_RP_Guidelines still applies: roll openly, state the
Target Number and modifiers before the roll, explain Degrees, and invent
situational rules only when this file is silent.

--------------------------------------------------
0. KEYWORDS
--------------------------------------------------

Living glossary. Add terms here as later Core files create them. Do not
redefine a term in a later file without updating this list.

Entity
  Anything that can take a test — and therefore anything that can sit on
  one side of an Opposed Test. A person, beast, abomination, spirit, or
  other will with Characteristics (and Skills, when those exist).
  A cliff, a lock, a storm, a language, a distance, or a night is not an
  Entity. Those have no stats and do not roll. They are represented as
  Difficulty and other modifiers on the Entity’s test.

Test
  One d100 rolled by an Entity against a Target Number. The basic unit
  of resolution.

Unopposed Test
  A Test taken by one Entity against the world. The world does not roll.
  Climbing a cliff is an Unopposed Test: the climber rolls the applicable
  Characteristic or Skill; wet rock, dark, height, and missing pitons
  are modifiers from section 4. The cliff never has a TN.

Opposed Test
  A contest between two Entities. Both roll. Compare Degrees (section 5).
  If only one side can roll, it is not Opposed. Use an Unopposed Test.

Target Number (TN)
  The number the roll must be less than or equal to. A Characteristic or
  Skill value after all modifiers.

Characteristic
  A standing capacity of an Entity (Strength, and the rest when that
  file exists). Has a Bonus equal to its tens digit. Values may exceed
  100. That does not make every test of that Characteristic an automatic
  success. See Man-Scale and Dominate.

Skill
  A trained or practised application of a Characteristic. Other files
  define the list and how the value is built. This file only uses the
  final TN.

Modifier
  A plus or minus applied to the TN before the roll. Difficulty ladder
  entries, tools, injury, aid, chain results, weather, and similar.

Difficulty
  The named band in section 4 that sets a single modifier for how hard
  the whole task is. Other modifiers may still stack on top.

Degree of Success (DoS)
  How many full 10s the roll came in under the TN. A success by 0–9 is
  0 DoS. Still a success.

Degree of Failure (DoF)
  How many full 10s the roll went over the TN. A failure by 1–9 is
  0 DoF. Still a failure.

Bonus
  Tens digit of a Characteristic. The unit of effect (damage, shove,
  short movement, and so on). Other files say which Bonus applies.
  A Characteristic of 123 has Bonus 12.

Man-Scale
  The default size and force of an adult human body and the work such a
  body is expected to do: a door, a cart-shaft, a man in a grapple, a
  barrel, a dropped beam a farm crew could lift.
  When Size Categories exist, Man-Scale is Size 4 (Average).
  Most tests in play are Man-Scale unless the GM says otherwise.

Scale
  The size, mass, or force a test is actually about. Man-Scale is the
  baseline. A troll, a siege gate, a falling mill-wheel, or another
  troll is a different Scale. Scale is judged per test and per
  Characteristic, not as a single label on the Entity.

Size / Size Category
  A trait of an Entity’s body. The numbered categories, examples,
  to-hit, stealth, movement, and S/T scaling live in
  Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt.
  This file only needs the distinction: Size is a trait; Scale is the
  frame of the present test. Man-Scale is Size 4.

Dominate
  An Entity with 100 or more in a Characteristic Dominates Man-Scale
  tests of that Characteristic. The test succeeds without a roll.
  Dominate does not apply to tests at matching or greater Scale, and
  it does not leak into other Characteristics. A troll with Strength
  120 Dominates a Man-Scale Strength test. It does not automatically
  sneak, climb a wet chimney, or win an argument.

Critical
  A successful Test that also rolled doubles, or a roll of 01.

Fumble
  A failed Test that also rolled doubles, or a roll of 100.

Threshold
  The total DoS an Extended Test must accumulate before the work is done.

Narrative Time
  Loose time. Conversation, travel, work, the story moving.
  The GM keeps a convincing clock. See section 1a.

Structured Time
  Exact time. Combat and any moment where seconds matter.
  Tracked in Rounds and Turns. See section 1a.

Round
  About ten seconds. Six Rounds are about one minute.

Turn
  One Entity’s chance to act inside a Round.

Action
  What an Entity does on its Turn. Combat names the list.
  Attacks (Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt) limits basic
  attacks per Turn.

--------------------------------------------------
1. THE TEST
--------------------------------------------------

An Entity resolves an uncertain action with a single d100 (1–100).
Treat a roll of 00 as 100.

Only an Entity rolls. If the other side of the situation cannot roll,
the test is Unopposed and the resistance is written as modifiers.

Target Number (TN)
  The TN is the relevant Characteristic or Skill value after modifiers.
  Other files define how a Skill value is derived. This file only
  cares about the final TN.

Outcome
  Success  = roll ≤ TN
  Failure  = roll > TN

Do not roll if there is no meaningful chance of failure, no meaningful
chance of success, or no interesting cost to trying. Narrate the
obvious result. Roll when the result is uncertain and the Degrees will
change what happens next.

--------------------------------------------------
1a. NARRATIVE TIME AND STRUCTURED TIME
--------------------------------------------------

Most play does not need the second. When players talk to people,
travel a road, work a Skill at leisure, or sit in a hall, the GM
keeps time as fits the scene and keeps the game moving. Be
consistent and convincing. There is no stopwatch. This is
Narrative Time. Use it when the point is to progress the story.

Narrative Time still has a clock. It is coarse: a conversation,
a watch, an hour, a morning, a ride, a night. Name the grain
that matters. Do not count seconds inside it.

The living clock is Campaign_Log.txt. When a scene ends or the
grain of time changes in a way later play will care about
(dawn, noon, after a forced march, after a fight, a False Moon
rising), update:

  === CAMPAIGN METADATA ===
  Campaign Name:
  Starting Date (in-world):
  Current In-World Date / Season:
  Current Time:
  Current Location:
  Last Updated:

Current Time is the new field. Date / Season already lived
there. Do not leave Time blank after a structured fight or a
day’s travel.

Structured Time
  Use it when exact order and elapsed seconds will change who
  lives: combat, a chase over a short burst, a fuse, a ritual
  that must finish before the watch turns, anything where
  “who acts first” or “how many breaths” is the question.

  One Round  ≈  ten seconds
  Six Rounds ≈  one minute

  In a Round, every Entity in the encounter takes one Turn.
  Order of Turns is a Combat question (initiative). This file
  only says: one Round, then every Entity that can still act
  has had a Turn, then the next Round.

  On a Turn, an Entity may take one or more Actions. Combat
  names what an Action is and how many fit. The Attacks stat
  (default 1) is the number of basic attacks — melee or
  ranged — allowed on that Turn unless a Talent, weapon,
  Size rule, or condition says otherwise. Movement, a Skill
  test, drawing steel, and speaking a short line are Actions
  when Combat says they are. Until Combat exists: one attack
  or one meaningful deed, plus a short move, is a fair Turn.

Switching
  Enter Structured Time when someone draws, flees under fire,
  or the seconds start to matter. Leave it when the fight or
  the chase is over. The last Round’s time is added to
  Current Time in the Log (six Rounds = one minute; a short
  scrap is a minute; a long one is several). Then return to
  Narrative Time.

  Do not run a market scene in Rounds. Do not run a melee as
  “a few minutes later” with no Turns.

Extended tests (section 7) usually sit in Narrative Time
(watches, hours, days). A short extended action inside a
fight uses Rounds as its increment instead.
2. DEGREES OF SUCCESS AND FAILURE
--------------------------------------------------

Degrees measure how well or how badly the roll landed. Count only
full bands of 10.

  If roll ≤ TN:
      Gap = TN − roll
      DoS = Gap ÷ 10, remainder discarded
      DoF = none
  If roll > TN:
      Gap = roll − TN
      DoF = Gap ÷ 10, remainder discarded
      DoS = none

A success by 0–9 points is still a success. It scores 0 DoS
(narrow success).
A failure by 1–9 points is still a failure. It scores 0 DoF
(narrow failure).

Worked examples (TN 40)
  Roll 40 → success,  0 DoS     (equal to TN)
  Roll 31 → success,  0 DoS     (under by 9)
  Roll 30 → success,  1 DoS     (under by 10)
  Roll 21 → success,  1 DoS     (under by 19)
  Roll 20 → success,  2 DoS
  Roll 01 → success,  3 DoS
  Roll 41 → failure,  0 DoF     (over by 1)
  Roll 49 → failure,  0 DoF     (over by 9)
  Roll 50 → failure,  1 DoF     (over by 10)
  Roll 59 → failure,  1 DoF
  Roll 60 → failure,  2 DoF
  Roll 100→ failure,  6 DoF

What Degrees do
  Quality of the result, margin in opposed tests, extra effect, extra
  harm, extra time saved or wasted, morale, and how much a chained
  test helps or hurts the next roll. Characteristic Bonuses (section 6)
  set amounts. Degrees set how many times those amounts apply, or how
  far the fiction moves.

--------------------------------------------------
3. CRITICALS
--------------------------------------------------

A roll of doubles (11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, 99) is a critical
if the test succeeded, and a fumble if the test failed.

  01 is always a critical success.
  100 is always a fumble.

A critical success is at least 1 DoS, or one extra DoS if the roll
already scored DoS. Describe it under Rule of Cool. It does not erase
prior poor decisions.

A fumble is at least 1 DoF, or one extra DoF if the roll already
scored DoF. The extra failure should be specific: dropped tool, opened
a line, said the unforgivable word, startled the herd, nicked the veil.
It should not be cartoon death unless the situation was already lethal.

01 and 100 guarantee the critical or fumble tag. Still calculate
ordinary DoS/DoF, then apply the extra Degree above.

--------------------------------------------------
4. DIFFICULTY AND MODIFIERS
--------------------------------------------------

State the base TN, then every modifier, then the final TN, then roll.

Default difficulty is Average (no modifier). Change the TN, not the dice.

  Trivial       +30     almost anyone manages this
  Easy          +20     trained or careful people rarely fail
  Routine       +10     the expected job under decent conditions
  Average        +0     the ordinary test
  Challenging   −10     pressure, poor tools, bad footing, hurried work
  Difficult     −20     real opposition from the world
  Very Hard     −30     stacked problems, night, injury, hostile ground
  Extreme       −40     possible, not reasonable

Typical live modifiers sit between ±5 and ±20 (Core_RP_Guidelines).
Use the ladder when the whole task is easier or harder. Use small
±5 / ±10 chips for one fact (better tool, off hand, rain, speaking a
second language).

Example — Unopposed Test
  A climber faces a wet cliff at night. The cliff is not an Entity.
  The climber tests the applicable Skill. The GM applies Difficulty
  (wet, dark, poor holds) and any smaller chips (good rope, injured
  hand). One roll. The rock does not roll back.

Do not stack past Extreme without a hard look. If the final TN is 0
or less, the test cannot succeed unless a later rule says otherwise —
narrate failure or let the player change the approach.

A final TN of 100 or more is not a blanket automatic success.
See section 6a. If the Entity Dominates the test, do not roll.
If the test is at matching or greater Scale, roll as normal. A TN
above 100 only fails when modifiers pull it down into the d100 range
and the roll exceeds it. When TN remains 100+ and the test was worth
rolling, treat the roll as a success and spend it on Degrees
(TN − roll, still in bands of 10).

Aid from a prior test (see section 8) is a modifier, not a second
resolution system.

Size modifiers (to-hit, stealth, movement, S/T) are assigned in
Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt. Do not invent a second “it is big”
Difficulty chip for a fact Size has already paid for.

--------------------------------------------------
5. OPPOSED TESTS
--------------------------------------------------

An Opposed Test requires two Entities. Both roll against their own TN.
A watchman is an Entity. A cliff is not. Sneaking past the watch is
Opposed. Climbing the cliff to get above the watch is Unopposed.

If one Entity Dominates the Characteristic the contest is actually
about, and the other side is Man-Scale, do not make an Opposed Test.
The dominant side wins that contest. The smaller Entity may only stay
in the fight by changing what the contest is (slip, tool, numbers,
terrain, a different Characteristic). Two Entities of matching Scale
roll as normal, even if both sit above 100.

Compare in this order:
  1. Success beats failure. Always.
  2. Among successes, higher DoS wins.
  3. Among failures, lower DoF wins.
  4. Same result and same Degree: higher TN wins (better capacity holds).
  5. TNs also equal: deadlock. No change, or both pay the same cost if
     the fiction will not stand still.

Examples (both TN 40 unless noted)
  A rolls 20 (2 DoS).  B rolls 35 (0 DoS).     A wins.
  A rolls 35 (0 DoS).  B rolls 41 (0 DoF).     A wins — success beats failure.
  A rolls 41 (0 DoF).  B rolls 60 (2 DoF).     A wins — nearer miss.
  A rolls 30 (1 DoS).  B rolls 30 (1 DoS).     Higher TN wins; if TNs match,
                                               deadlock.

Do not subtract raw roll from raw roll. Always go through Degrees.

Use opposed tests for contests: grapples, chases over a short burst,
argument, sneaking past a watch, two smiths racing a join, a beast and
a rider fighting for the bit. Long chases and sieges are extended
tests (section 7).

--------------------------------------------------
6. CHARACTERISTIC BONUSES
--------------------------------------------------

Every Characteristic has a Bonus equal to its tens digit.

  Strength 41  → Strength Bonus (SB) 4
  Agility  38  → Agility Bonus (AB)  3
  09         → Bonus 0
  100        → Bonus 10
  Strength 123 → Strength Bonus (SB) 12

Bonus = integer divide the Characteristic by 10. Do not round the units.

Bonuses are the unit of effect. Other files say which Bonus applies.

  Damage, shove, lift, break         Strength Bonus
  Distance in a short burst, balance Agility Bonus
  Time saved, careful work rate      relevant Bonus, or Skill Bonus
                                     when that is defined
  Severity of a consequence          often the Degree × a Bonus

This file does not set combat damage. It only defines what a Bonus is.

A Skill does not automatically have its own Bonus unless a later file
says so. Until then, when a rule calls for “the Bonus,” use the parent
Characteristic Bonus.

--------------------------------------------------
6a. CHARACTERISTICS ABOVE 100
--------------------------------------------------

Characteristics may exceed 100. This is normal for large, heavy, or
otherwise out-of-band Entities (a troll’s Strength, a royal gryphon’s
mass, an Unveiling bulk that was never a man).

100+ is not “always succeeds.”
100+ means the Entity Dominates any Man-Scale test of that
Characteristic.

Unopposed, Man-Scale
  Strength 120 troll, barred cottage door, fallen cart-horse, man-high
  boulder a work crew could shift.
  No roll. The troll succeeds. If the quality of the success matters
  (how cleanly the door comes off, how far the horse is thrown), grant
  DoS = (Characteristic − 100) ÷ 10, remainder discarded, minimum 1 DoS.

Unopposed, matching or greater Scale
  The same troll lifting a portcullis forged for it, bracing a collapsing
  gallery, forcing a siege-leaf, swimming a mill-race in flood.
  This is an ordinary Test. Set Difficulty for how hard it is at the
  troll’s Scale, not for how impossible it would be for a man.
  Roll against the full Characteristic after modifiers.

Opposed, Man-Scale Entity
  Troll against a man in a pure Strength contest (shove, break grip,
  hold the gate shut with the body).
  The troll Dominates. The man does not roll Strength to win.
  The man may only continue by changing the contest.

Opposed, matching Scale
  Troll against troll, or troll against something built to its force.
  Both roll as normal, even if both Characteristics sit above 100.

Other Characteristics are untouched.
  Strength 120 does not Dominate Agility, Perception, or Willpower.
  Judge which Characteristic the test is actually using, then apply
  Dominate only to that one.

Skills
  A Skill’s TN is the mean of its Parents, then training
  (Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt §5). Dominate follows that final
  Skill value and the Scale of the work, not a single oversized
  Parent. A troll with Strength 120 Dominates a Man-Scale Strength
  test (the cottage door). The same troll rolls Climbing, because
  Climbing is (Strength + Agility) ÷ 2.

How a large body reaches 100+
  Size Category S/T scaling (Core_Characteristics_Skills.txt) is the
  usual route. Size does not itself trigger Dominate. The Characteristic
  does.

GM tell
  If a man could plausibly do the job with enough men, time, or tools,
  it is Man-Scale. If the job assumes a body or force of another Scale,
  set Difficulty at that Scale and roll. When unsure, roll. Dominate
  is for the obvious cases.

--------------------------------------------------
7. EXTENDED TESTS
--------------------------------------------------

Use an extended test when the work takes time and progress can be seen:
forced march, tracking a widow-gryphon off the massif, translating a
seal, digging a palisade before night, bargaining a winter contract.

  1. Set a Threshold in required DoS (typical 3, 6, or 10).
  2. Set a Time increment per roll (a round, a watch, a day).
  3. Each successful roll adds its DoS to a running total.
  4. A 0 DoS success adds 1 progress (the work still moved).
  5. Each failure adds no progress. A fumble or DoF 2+ removes progress
     equal to the DoF, or introduces a complication, or both.
  6. The test ends when the Threshold is met, the time runs out, or a
     complication makes the original approach impossible.

Complications should be concrete: tools break, weather turns, a watchman
changes post, the veil thickens, the herd moves. Do not empty the
attempt into a void.

--------------------------------------------------
8. CHAINED TESTS
--------------------------------------------------

When one action sets up another, resolve them in order. Degrees on the
first test modify the TN of the second.

Default chain (matches Core_RP_Guidelines):

  +10 to the follow-up TN per DoS on the setup test
  −10 to the follow-up TN per DoF on the setup test
  Critical failure on the setup may impose −20, force a different Skill,
  or abort the follow-up.

Examples already in the Guidelines
  Speak Language DoS/DoF → Haggle
  Lore or Outdoor Survival DoS → Stealth in that country

Cap the chain bonus or penalty at ±30 unless the setup was a dedicated
scene of preparation. Preparation the player actually played out can
stack with a chain; a hand-waved “I also studied the map” should not.

--------------------------------------------------
9. ASSISTANCE, RETRIES, TIME
--------------------------------------------------

Assistance
  One helper who could attempt the same test may assist.
  If the helper succeeds, the main actor gains +10.
  If the helper scores 2+ DoS, the main actor gains +20.
  If the helper fumbles, the main actor takes −10 and inherits a
  complication.
  More bodies do not stack +10 forever. Extra helpers change the
  fiction (more hands, more noise, more mouths to feed) rather than
  the math.

Retry
  An immediate retry after a failure is only allowed if the situation
  has changed: new tool, new angle, extra time, help arrived, the
  watch moved. Same approach, same conditions: the failure stands.
  Trying again anyway costs time and may worsen the Degree of the
  original failure.

Extra time
  Working at leisure, with no pressure, grants +10 and may turn a
  single test into an extended test at the player’s option.
  Rushing for half the reasonable time imposes −10.
  Rushing beyond that is −20 and a fumble range the GM should describe
  before the roll.

--------------------------------------------------
10. WHAT THE ROLL MEANS IN PLAY
--------------------------------------------------

0 DoS success
  The thing happens. No flourish. No spare margin. A lock opens; the
  sentence is believed; the footing holds. Tight.

1–2 DoS
  Clean work. Spare time, spare information, a better position, a
  second useful fact, a witness who remembers you kindly.

3+ DoS or critical success
  The world notices. Rule of Cool applies. Doors open that the player
  did not know were there. Do not hand free miracles. Hand earned
  advantage.

0 DoF failure
  It does not happen. The cost is small: time, noise, a lost chance,
  a cool look from the merchant. The attempt can usually be reframed.

1–2 DoF
  A real cost. Resource, position, reputation, or safety moves against
  the actor. The next scene is harder in a specific way.

3+ DoF or fumble
  The situation changes species. Alarm, injury, broken gear, a
  veil-prick, a promise you did not mean to make. Still not automatic
  death.

--------------------------------------------------
11. TRANSPARENCY
--------------------------------------------------

Before the roll, say:
  — what is being tested (Characteristic or Skill)
  — the unmodified value
  — each modifier and why
  — the final TN
  — what success, failure, and a fumble will look like, in brief

After the roll, say the number, the Degrees, and the result.
Do not hide the math. The world is cruel. The dice are not secret.
